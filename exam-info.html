what to expect on the paper

Boards test more than memory.

Modern CBSE papers mix recall with application. Here are the four question types you'll see across most subjects.

Competency-based questions

You're given an unfamiliar situation and asked to apply what you learned. The rule is the same — the setting is new. Practice recognising which chapter a question is really testing.

Case & source-based

A short passage, data set or scenario is followed by 3–5 questions. Read the passage twice. Most marks are lost because students answer from memory instead of from what the passage actually says.

Short & long answers

Step marks matter. Write the formula first, then substitute, then answer. Even if the final number is wrong, a clear method earns partial credit. Diagrams where they're asked for.

Objective questions

MCQ, assertion-reason, fill-in-the-blank. This section is where students lose the most careless marks. Watch units, signs, negative options and "not" in the question stem.

in the exam hall

Three steps, every question.

01

Read

Read the whole question before you start writing. Identify what's given, what's asked, and what units are expected. A five-second pause saves five minutes of correction.

02

Solve

Formula first, substitution second, answer third. Keep work organised down the page. If you get stuck, move on — come back with fresh eyes.

03

Review

Reserve the last 10–15 minutes. Revisit unanswered questions, then check for missing units, wrong signs, and questions you may have misread.

Passing rules are not just one number.

A lot of students think "33% overall" is the only rule. That's usually wrong. In practical subjects you often need a minimum in theory and a minimum in practicals separately. Internal assessment is graded differently. Always check the current year's official CBSE documents.

study roadmap

A preparation plan that actually fits a month.

Five stages, in order. Not a fixed timetable — just the right sequence, so you don't spend February re-reading chapter one.

  1. 01

    Build the concepts

    Start with NCERT. Every definition, derivation, reaction and formula is on the table. Don't skip the derivations to save time — they come back as 3 and 5 mark questions.

  2. 02

    Work through exercises

    Move from solved examples to unsolved exercises. Track which chapters keep tripping you up. Those are the ones that need a second pass, not the ones you already know.

  3. 03

    Previous-year papers

    Do the last 5 years of board papers and the official CBSE sample paper. Not to memorise answers — to understand how questions are phrased and how marks are distributed.

  4. 04

    Full-length mocks, timed

    Three hours, no interruptions, no phone. Then spend an hour analysing what went wrong — not just the score. The analysis is where the marks come from.

  5. 05

    Final revision

    Formulas, definitions, diagrams, common mistakes, and the chapters you're least confident about. Nothing new. Just retrieval practice of what's already there.

common questions

Things students ask us most.

What is the actual passing mark for Class 12 Science?

For most CBSE subjects, you need a minimum of 33% overall. But for subjects with practicals — Physics, Chemistry, Biology — you also need a separate minimum in the theory paper and in the practical. So a student can theoretically score 40% overall and still fail if they score too low on one component.

Always confirm the exact rule for your exam year from the official CBSE curriculum document — not from any website, including this one.

How much time should I give each subject?

There's no universal answer, but a reasonable starting point for the final month is: equal time for your three weakest subjects, and about half that for your two strongest. The point is to close gaps, not to reward subjects you already enjoy studying.

Are previous-year questions really that important?

Yes, but not for the reason most students think. The value is not in seeing the same question again — it's in understanding the language and structure the examiner uses. Once you notice how a 3-mark question is phrased differently from a 5-mark one, you write better answers immediately.

Is it too late to start if I'm behind?

Not if you're reading this before the exam. The biggest mistake students make when they're behind is trying to cover everything equally. Instead, focus on the high-weightage chapters first, then the ones you can pick up quickly. Two chapters done well are worth more than six chapters done badly.

Do I need to write the whole derivation for full marks?

Generally, yes — the steps are the marks. Examiners award marks for the method, not just the final expression. Even if you don't remember the last line, showing the setup and half the algebra usually earns more than a blank response.
