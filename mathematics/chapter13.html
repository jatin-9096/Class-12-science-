Random Experiments and Sample Space

Random Experiment – any procedure whose outcome cannot be predicted with certainty in advance.

Sample Space (S) – the set of all possible elementary outcomes of a random experiment.

  • Each outcome is called an elementary event.
  • Any subset of $S$ is an event.

Notation & Formulas

ConceptMathematical Expression
Probability of an event $A$ (classical)$P(A)=\\frac{n(A)}{n(S)}$, where $n(A)$ and $n(S)$ are the number of favourable and total equally likely outcomes respectively.
Complementary rule$P(A^{c})=1-P(A)$
Addition rule (mutually exclusive)$P(A\\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)$

Key Points

  • The probability value lies in $0\\le P(A)\\le 1$ (dimensionless).
  • If $A\\subseteq B$ then $P(A)\\le P(B)$.

Example

Problem: A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of getting at least one head.

Solution: The sample space $S=\\{HH,HT,TH,TT\\}$, so $n(S)=4$. The favourable outcomes are $\\{HH,HT,TH\\}$, thus $n(A)=3$. Using the classical formula, $P(A)=\\frac{3}{4}=0.75$.

Events and Their Operations

Definition of an Event: An event $E$ is any subset of the sample space $S$ of a random experiment. It can be simple (single outcome) or compound (multiple outcomes). The set of all events forms a σ‑algebra closed under union, intersection and complement.

Operations on Events:

OperationNotationResulting Event
Union$A\cup B$All outcomes belonging to $A$ or $B$ (or both)
Intersection$A\cap B$Outcomes common to both $A$ and $B$
Complement$A'$Outcomes in $S$ not in $A$
Difference$A\setminus B$Outcomes in $A$ but not in $B$

Key Probability Formulas

  • Complement rule: $P(A')=1-P(A)$.
  • Addition rule (general): $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$.
  • If $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, $P(A\cap B)=0$ and $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)$.
  • For three events: $P(A\cup B\cup C)=P(A)+P(B)+P(C)-P(A\cap B)-P(A\cap C)-P(B\cap C)+P(A\cap B\cap C)$.

Units: Probability is a dimensionless quantity; its value lies in $[0,1]$ (or $0\%$ to $100\%$ when expressed as a percentage).

Solved Example:

In a survey, $P(A)=0.4$, $P(B)=0.5$ and $P(A\cap B)=0.2$. Find $P(A\cup B)$ and $P(A')$.

Using the addition rule:

$$P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)=0.4+0.5-0.2=0.7.$$

Complement of $A$:

$$P(A')=1-P(A)=1-0.4=0.6.$$

Thus, the probability that either $A$ or $B$ occurs is $0.7$, and the probability that $A$ does \not occur is $0.6$.

Probability of an Event

Definition: In a random experiment the set of all possible outcomes is the sample space $S$. An event $A$ is any subset of $S$. The probability of an event $A$ is defined as
$$P(A)=\frac{n(A)}{n(S)}$$
where $n(A)$ is the number of favourable outcomes and $n(S)$ is the total number of equally likely outcomes.

Key Properties

  • $0\le P(A)\le 1$
  • $P(S)=1$, $P(\emptyset)=0$
  • Complementary rule: $P(A')=1-P(A)$
  • Mutually exclusive events $A$ and $B$: $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)$

Derivation (Equally Likely Outcomes)

When each outcome \in $S$ has the same chance, the probability of $A$ is the ratio of favourable \to total outcomes, leading directly \to $P(A)=\frac{n(A)}{n(S)}$.

SI Unit: Probability is dimensionless; its unit is “none” (or simply “probability”).

FormulaMeaning
$P(A)=\frac{n(A)}{n(S)}$Basic definition
$P(A')=1-P(A)$Complementary rule
$P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)$Mutually exclusive events

Solved Example

A bag contains 3 red, 2 blue and 5 green balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability of drawing a red ball.

Total balls $n(S)=3+2+5=10$. Favourable outcomes $n(A)=3$ (red balls).
$$P(\text{red})=\frac{3}{10}=0.3$$

Thus the probability of drawing a red ball is $0.3$ (or $30\%$).

Conditional Probability and Multiplication Rule

Conditional Probability is the probability of an event $A$ occurring given that another event $B$ has already occurred. It is defined as $P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$, provided $P(B)>0$.

Multiplication Rule follows directly: $P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)\,P(B)=P(B\mid A)\,P(A)$.

  • Both $P(A)$ and $P(B)$ are dimensionless (unit = 1).
  • If events are independent, $P(A\mid B)=P(A)$ and $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$.

Derivation: Starting from the definition of conditional probability, multiply both sides by $P(B)$ to obtain $P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)P(B)$. Interchanging $A$ and $B$ gives the symmetric form.

FormulaExplanation
$P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$Probability of $A$ given $B$.
$P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)P(B)$Multiplication rule.

Example: In a deck of 52 cards, let $A$ be the event “draw a king” and $B$ the event “draw a face card”. Find $P(A\mid B)$ and $P(A\cap B)$.

We have $P(B)=\frac{12}{52}=\frac{3}{13}$ (12 face cards). $P(A\cap B)=P(A)=\frac{4}{52}=\frac{1}{13}$ because every king is a face card. Hence $P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{\frac{1}{13}}{\frac{3}{13}}=\frac{1}{3}$. Also $P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)P(B)=\frac{1}{3}\times\frac{3}{13}=\frac{1}{13}$, confirming the result.

Bayes’ Theorem

Definition: Bayes’ theorem relates the conditional probability of an event $A_i$ given $B$ to the reverse conditional probability.

For a partition $\{A_1,A_2,\dots,A_n\}$ of the sample space with $P(A_i)>0$,

$$P(A_i|B)=\frac{P(B|A_i)P(A_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}P(B|A_j)P(A_j)}$$

Special case ($n=2$):

$$P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$ where $P(B)=P(B|A)P(A)+P(B|\bar A)P(\bar A)$.

  • All probabilities are dimensionless (no SI unit).
  • Useful when $P(B|A)$ is known (likelihood) and $P(A)$ (prior) is given.

Derivation (brief): Start from definition of conditional probability $P(A_i\cap B)=P(A_i)P(B|A_i)=P(B)P(A_i|B)$. Rearranging gives the formula above.

SymbolMeaning
$A_i$Mutually exclusive & exhaustive events
$B$Observed event
$P(A_i)$Prior probability
$P(B|A_i)$Likelihood
$P(A_i|B)$Posterior probability

Example: A disease affects $1\%$ of a population. A test is $99\%$ sensitive ($P(+|D)=0.99$) and $95\%$ specific ($P(-|\bar D)=0.95$). Find $P(D|+)$.

Given: $P(D)=0.01$, $P(\bar D)=0.99$, $P(+|\bar D)=0.05$. Compute $P(+)=0.99\times0.01+0.05\times0.99=0.0594$. Then

$$P(D|+)=\frac{0.99\times0.01}{0.0594}=0.1667\approx16.7\%$$

Thus, even a highly accurate test yields a low posterior probability when the disease is rare.

Independent Events

Independent Events

Two events $A$ and $B$ are said to be independent if the occurrence of one does not affect the probability of the other.

Mathematically, $A$ and $B$ are independent \iff $P(A\\cap B)=P(A)\\,P(B)$.

From the definition, the conditional probability reduces to $P(A|B)=P(A)$ and $P(B|A)=P(B)$.

  • For more than two events $A_1, A_2,\\dots,A_n$, they are mutually independent if $P\\big(\\bigcap_{i\\in I}A_i\\big)=\\prod_{i\\in I}P(A_i)$ for every non‑empty subset $I$ of $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$.

Key Formulas

FormulaExplanation
$P(A\\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$Definition of independence for two events.
$P(A|B)=\\dfrac{P(A\\cap B)}{P(B)}=P(A)$Conditional probability when $A$ and $B$ are independent.
$P\\big(\\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}A_i\\big)=\\prod_{i=1}^{n}P(A_i)$Mutual independence of $n$ events.

Units

Probability is a dimensionless quantity; its SI unit is “none”.

Example

A fair die is rolled twice. Let $A$ = “first roll is a 3” and $B$ = “second roll is an even number”. Find $P(A\\cap B)$.

Since each roll is independent, $P(A)=\\dfrac{1}{6}$ and $P(B)=\\dfrac{3}{6}=\\dfrac{1}{2}$. Using independence,

$$P(A\\cap B)=P(A)P(B)=\\dfrac{1}{6}\\times\\dfrac{1}{2}=\\dfrac{1}{12}.$$

Thus the probability that the first roll is 3 and the second roll is even is $\\dfrac{1}{12}$.

Discrete Random Variables and Probability Distribution

Discrete Random Variable (DRV): A variable $X$ that can assume only a countable set of distinct values $\{x_1,x_2,\dots\}$.

Probability Distribution of a DRV is the list of pairs $(x_i,p_i)$ where $p_i=P(X=x_i)$ satisfying

  • $p_i\ge 0$ for every $i$
  • $\sum_{i}p_i=1$

Key Formulas

  • Mean (Expected value): $\mu=E(X)=\sum_{i}x_i p_i$
  • Second moment: $E(X^2)=\sum_{i}x_i^2 p_i$
  • Variance: $\sigma^2=Var(X)=E[(X-\mu)^2]=E(X^2)-\mu^2$
  • Standard deviation: $\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2}$

Since probability is dimensionless, the SI unit is none.

Example: Toss a fair coin three times. Let $X$ = number of heads.

$x_i$$p_i$
$0$$\frac{1}{8}$
$1$$\frac{3}{8}$
$2$$\frac{3}{8}$
$3$$\frac{1}{8}$

Calculations:

Mean: $$\mu=0\cdot\frac{1}{8}+1\cdot\frac{3}{8}+2\cdot\frac{3}{8}+3\cdot\frac{1}{8}=\frac{12}{8}=1.5$$

Second moment: $$E(X^2)=0^2\cdot\frac{1}{8}+1^2\cdot\frac{3}{8}+2^2\cdot\frac{3}{8}+3^2\cdot\frac{1}{8}=\frac{21}{8}=2.625$$

Variance: $$\sigma^2=2.625-1.5^2=2.625-2.25=0.375$$

Standard deviation: $$\sigma=\sqrt{0.375}\approx0.612$$

Mean and Variance of Discrete Random Variables

Mean (Expected Value) of a Discrete Random Variable

If $X$ takes values $x_i$ with probabilities $p_i$, then $$\\mu = E(X)=\\sum_{i} x_i p_i$$

Variance

$$\\sigma^2 = Var(X)=\\sum_i (x_i-\\mu)^2 p_i = \\sum_i x_i^2 p_i - \\mu^2$$

Key Properties

  • Both are dimensionless; probability has no SI unit.
  • For a linear transformation $Y = aX + b$, $E(Y)=a\\mu + b$, $Var(Y)=a^2\\sigma^2$.

Formula Summary

QuantityFormula
Mean$$\\mu = \\sum x_i p_i$$
Second Moment$$E(X^2)=\\sum x_i^2 p_i$$
Variance$$\\sigma^2 = E(X^2)-\\mu^2$$

Example

A loaded die has probabilities: $P(1)=0.1$, $P(2)=0.2$, $P(3)=0.3$, $P(4)=0.2$, $P(5)=0.1$, $P(6)=0.1$. Find the mean and variance.

Mean:

$$\\mu = 1(0.1)+2(0.2)+3(0.3)+4(0.2)+5(0.1)+6(0.1)=3.3$$

Second moment:

$$E(X^2)=1^2(0.1)+2^2(0.2)+3^2(0.3)+4^2(0.2)+5^2(0.1)+6^2(0.1)$$ $$=0.1+0.8+2.7+3.2+2.5+3.6=12.9$$

Variance:

$$\\sigma^2 = 12.9-(3.3)^2 = 12.9-10.89 = 2.01$$

Thus, $E(X)=3.3$ and $Var(X)=2.01$ (dimensionless).
