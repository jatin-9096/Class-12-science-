Chapter 07: Alternating Current (AC)

Subject: Physics, Class 12

Author: Jatin Sandilya

1. Comprehensive Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

Alternating Current (AC) is a type of electric current in which the direction of the flow of electrons reverses periodically, unlike Direct Current (DC) where the flow is in one constant direction. AC is the primary form of electricity delivered to homes and businesses globally because of its ease of generation, transmission, and transformation (voltage step-up/step-down using transformers).

Key Concepts:

  • AC vs DC: Understanding the fundamental difference in current direction and magnitude over time.
  • Peak and RMS Values: Definition and calculation of peak (maximum) voltage/current and Root Mean Square (RMS) voltage/current, which represents the effective value of AC.
  • Phasors and Phasor Diagrams: A graphical representation used to analyze AC circuits, where sinusoidal quantities (voltage, current) are represented by rotating vectors called phasors.
  • AC Circuits with Single Components:
    • Resistive Circuit: Voltage and current are in phase.
    • Inductive Circuit: Voltage leads current by $\pi/2$ (or $90^\circ$). Inductive reactance ($X_L$) opposes current flow.
    • Capacitive Circuit: Current leads voltage by $\pi/2$ (or $90^\circ$). Capacitive reactance ($X_C$) opposes current flow.
  • Series LCR Circuit: Analysis of circuits containing a resistor (R), an inductor (L), and a capacitor (C) in series. Concepts like impedance (Z), phase difference ($\phi$), and power factor ($\cos\phi$) are crucial.
  • Resonance: The condition in an LCR circuit where the inductive reactance equals the capacitive reactance ($X_L = X_C$), leading to minimum impedance, maximum current, and a phase angle of zero. This occurs at a specific resonant frequency ($\omega_0$).
  • Quality Factor (Q-factor): A measure of the sharpness of the resonance in an LCR circuit.
  • Power in AC Circuits: Understanding instantaneous power, average power, and the concept of a power factor, which accounts for the phase difference between voltage and current.
  • LC Oscillations: The phenomenon of charge and current oscillations in a circuit containing an inductor and a capacitor, analogous to mechanical oscillations.
  • Transformers: Devices used to change AC voltages. Based on mutual induction, they can be step-up (increase voltage, decrease current) or step-down (decrease voltage, increase current).

2. Key Theorems / Core Formulas

Below are the essential formulas and relationships for Alternating Current circuits.

1. Sinusoidal AC Voltage and Current:

Voltage: $V = V_0 \sin(\omega t + \phi_V)$

Current: $I = I_0 \sin(\omega t + \phi_I)$

Where $V_0, I_0$ are peak values, $\omega = 2\pi f$ is angular frequency, $t$ is time, and $\phi_V, \phi_I$ are initial phases.

2. RMS and Average Values (for sinusoidal AC):

RMS Values:

$$V_{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 V_0$$ $$I_{rms} = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 I_0$$

Average Values (for a half cycle):

$$V_{avg} = \frac{2 V_0}{\pi} \approx 0.637 V_0$$ $$I_{avg} = \frac{2 I_0}{\pi} \approx 0.637 I_0$$

For a full cycle, average value of V or I is zero.

3. Reactance:

Inductive Reactance ($X_L$): Opposition offered by an inductor to AC.

$$X_L = \omega L = 2\pi f L$$

Capacitive Reactance ($X_C$): Opposition offered by a capacitor to AC.

$$X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}$$

Units for $X_L$ and $X_C$ are Ohms ($\Omega$).

4. Impedance (Z) for Series LCR Circuit:

Total opposition offered by R, L, and C in series to AC.

$$Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$$

Units: Ohms ($\Omega$).

5. Phase Angle ($\phi$):

Phase difference between voltage and current in an LCR circuit.

$$\tan \phi = \frac{X_L - X_C}{R}$$

If $X_L > X_C$, voltage leads current (inductive circuit). If $X_C > X_L$, current leads voltage (capacitive circuit).

6. Power in AC Circuits:

Average Power:

$$P_{avg} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi$$

Power Factor ($\cos \phi$):

$$\cos \phi = \frac{R}{Z}$$

Wattless Current: The component of current ($I_{rms} \sin \phi$) that consumes no average power.

7. Resonance in Series LCR Circuit:

Condition: $X_L = X_C \implies \omega_0 L = \frac{1}{\omega_0 C}$

Resonant Frequency ($\omega_0$):

$$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$$

In Hz: $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$

At resonance, $Z = R$ (minimum impedance), current is maximum, $\phi = 0$, and $\cos \phi = 1$.

Quality Factor (Q):

$$Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{1}{R} \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$$

Higher Q implies a sharper resonance curve.

8. Transformers:

Voltage Transformation Ratio:

$$\frac{V_S}{V_P} = \frac{N_S}{N_P}$$

Ideal Transformer (Power Conservation):

$$V_P I_P = V_S I_S \implies \frac{I_S}{I_P} = \frac{V_P}{V_S} = \frac{N_P}{N_S}$$

Where $V_P, I_P, N_P$ are primary voltage, current, turns; $V_S, I_S, N_S$ are secondary voltage, current, turns.

3. Solved High-Yield CBSE Board PYQs

Question 1:

An AC voltage given by $V = 100 \sin(100t)$ is applied to a resistor of $10 \, \Omega$. Find the RMS value of voltage, RMS value of current, and the frequency of the AC source.

Step 1: Identify Given Information and Standard Equation.
Given AC voltage equation: $V = 100 \sin(100t)$.
Comparing with standard equation $V = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$:
  • Peak voltage $V_0 = 100 \, V$
  • Angular frequency $\omega = 100 \, rad/s$
  • Resistance $R = 10 \, \Omega$
(1 Mark) Step 2: Calculate RMS Value of Voltage.
The RMS value of voltage is $V_{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}}$.
$V_{rms} = \frac{100}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{100 \times \sqrt{2}}{2} = 50\sqrt{2} \, V$
$V_{rms} \approx 50 \times 1.414 = 70.7 \, V$ (1 Mark) Step 3: Calculate RMS Value of Current.
Using Ohm's Law for RMS values: $I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{R}$.
$I_{rms} = \frac{50\sqrt{2}}{10} = 5\sqrt{2} \, A$
$I_{rms} \approx 5 \times 1.414 = 7.07 \, A$ (1 Mark) Step 4: Calculate Frequency of the AC Source.
The angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$.
$100 = 2\pi f$
$f = \frac{100}{2\pi} = \frac{50}{\pi} \, Hz$
$f \approx \frac{50}{3.14} \approx 15.92 \, Hz$ (1 Mark)

Question 2:

A series LCR circuit with $L = 5 \, H$, $C = 80 \, \mu F$, $R = 40 \, \Omega$ is connected to a variable frequency $230 \, V$ AC source. Calculate:

(a) The angular frequency of the source which drives the circuit in resonance.

(b) The impedance of the circuit and the current at the resonating frequency.

Step 1: Identify Given Information.
$L = 5 \, H$
$C = 80 \, \mu F = 80 \times 10^{-6} \, F$
$R = 40 \, \Omega$
$V_{rms} = 230 \, V$ (1 Mark) Step 2: Calculate Angular Frequency at Resonance ($\omega_0$).
At resonance, $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.
$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5 \times 80 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{400 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4 \times 10^{-4}}} = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^{-2}}$
$\omega_0 = 50 \, rad/s$ (1.5 Marks) Step 3: Calculate Impedance at Resonance ($Z_{res}$).
At resonance, $X_L = X_C$, so impedance $Z_{res} = R$.
$Z_{res} = 40 \, \Omega$ (1 Mark) Step 4: Calculate Current at Resonance ($I_{rms, res}$).
$I_{rms, res} = \frac{V_{rms}}{Z_{res}} = \frac{230}{40}$
$I_{rms, res} = 5.75 \, A$ (1.5 Marks)

Question 3:

A transformer has 100 turns in the primary coil and 1000 turns in the secondary coil. If the primary voltage is $220 \, V$ and the primary current is $5 \, A$, calculate the secondary voltage and current, assuming an ideal transformer.

Step 1: Identify Given Information.
Number of turns in primary coil, $N_P = 100$
Number of turns in secondary coil, $N_S = 1000$
Primary voltage, $V_P = 220 \, V$
Primary current, $I_P = 5 \, A$ (1 Mark) Step 2: Calculate Secondary Voltage ($V_S$) using Turns Ratio.
For an ideal transformer, $\frac{V_S}{V_P} = \frac{N_S}{N_P}$.
$\frac{V_S}{220} = \frac{1000}{100}$
$V_S = 220 \times 10 = 2200 \, V$ (1.5 Marks) Step 3: Calculate Secondary Current ($I_S$) using Power Conservation (Ideal Transformer).
For an ideal transformer, $V_P I_P = V_S I_S$.
$220 \times 5 = 2200 \times I_S$
$1100 = 2200 \times I_S$
$I_S = \frac{1100}{2200} = 0.5 \, A$ (1.5 Marks)

4. Quick Revision Summary Box

  • AC vs DC: AC changes direction periodically, DC flows constantly in one direction.
  • RMS Values: $V_{rms} = V_0/\sqrt{2}$, $I_{rms} = I_0/\sqrt{2}$. Effective values of AC.
  • Reactance:
    • Inductive: $X_L = \omega L$. Voltage leads current.
    • Capacitive: $X_C = 1/(\omega C)$. Current leads voltage.
  • Impedance (Z): Total opposition in LCR circuit: $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$.
  • Phase Angle ($\phi$): $\tan \phi = (X_L - X_C)/R$. Phase difference between V and I.
  • Power Factor ($\cos \phi$): $\cos \phi = R/Z$. Average power $P_{avg} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi$.
  • Resonance: Occurs when $X_L = X_C$. Resonant frequency $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$. At resonance, $Z=R$ (minimum), $I$ is maximum, $\phi=0$, $\cos\phi=1$.
  • Q-Factor: $Q = \omega_0 L / R$. Measures sharpness of resonance.
  • Transformer: Changes AC voltage. $\frac{V_S}{V_P} = \frac{N_S}{N_P}$ and $V_P I_P = V_S I_S$ (ideal).
