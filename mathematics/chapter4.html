Chapter 04: Chemical Kinetics

Author: Jatin Sandilya

1. Comprehensive Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

Chemical Kinetics is the branch of Physical Chemistry that deals with the study of the rates of chemical reactions, the factors affecting them, and the mechanism by which reactions proceed. Understanding kinetics helps predict reaction feasibility and optimize reaction conditions.

Core Concepts:

  • Rate of a Chemical Reaction: It is defined as the change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time.
    • Average Rate: Change in concentration over a measurable time interval.
    • Instantaneous Rate: Rate at a particular instant of time, obtained from the slope of the tangent to the concentration vs. time curve.
  • Factors Affecting Reaction Rate:
    • Concentration of Reactants: Generally, rate increases with increasing concentration.
    • Temperature: Rate usually increases with temperature (approximately doubles for every 10°C rise).
    • Catalyst: Alters reaction rate without being consumed, by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.
    • Surface Area: For heterogeneous reactions, increasing surface area increases rate.
    • Nature of Reactants: Reactions involving ionic compounds are generally faster than those involving covalent compounds.
  • Rate Law (Rate Equation): An expression that relates the rate of a reaction to the concentration of reactants. For a reaction $aA + bB \to cC + dD$, the rate law is $Rate = k[A]^x[B]^y$, where $x$ and $y$ are experimentally determined orders of reaction with respect to A and B, respectively, and are not necessarily equal to the stoichiometric coefficients 'a' and 'b'.
  • Rate Constant (k): Proportionality constant in the rate law. Its value depends on temperature and the nature of the reaction.
  • Order of Reaction: The sum of the powers of the concentration terms in the rate law. It can be zero, positive, negative, or a fraction. It is an experimentally determined value.
  • Molecularity of a Reaction: The number of reacting species (atoms, ions, or molecules) that must collide simultaneously in an elementary reaction. It is always a whole number and rarely exceeds three.
  • Integrated Rate Equations: Equations derived by integrating the differential rate laws, relating concentration to time.
    • Zero Order Reaction: Rate is independent of reactant concentration.
    • First Order Reaction: Rate is directly proportional to the first power of reactant concentration.
  • Half-life Period ($t_{1/2}$): The time required for the concentration of a reactant to reduce to half of its initial value.
  • Activation Energy ($E_a$): The minimum extra amount of energy absorbed by reactant molecules to form the activated complex, which then proceeds to form products.
  • Arrhenius Equation: Relates the rate constant to activation energy and temperature.
  • Collision Theory: Explains reaction rates based on the collision of reacting molecules. Effective collisions (with proper orientation and sufficient energy) lead to product formation.

2. Key Theorems / Reaction Mechanisms / Core Formulas

Rate Laws & Orders:

For a general reaction: $aA + bB \to \text{Products}$

Rate Law: $Rate = k[A]^x[B]^y$

Where $x$ and $y$ are orders with respect to A and B, and $(x+y)$ is the overall order.

Integrated Rate Equations:

1. Zero Order Reaction ($Rate = k$):

Integrated form: $[R]_t = [R]_0 - kt$ or $kt = [R]_0 - [R]_t$

Where $[R]_0$ is initial concentration and $[R]_t$ is concentration at time $t$.

Half-life ($t_{1/2}$): $t_{1/2} = \frac{[R]_0}{2k}$

Units of $k$: $\text{mol L}^{-1} \text{s}^{-1}$

2. First Order Reaction ($Rate = k[R]$):

Integrated form: $k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]_t}$ or $\ln \frac{[R]_0}{[R]_t} = kt$

Or $\ln[R]_t = \ln[R]_0 - kt$

Half-life ($t_{1/2}$): $t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k}$

Units of $k$: $\text{s}^{-1}$

Arrhenius Equation:

Relates rate constant to temperature and activation energy:

$$k = A e^{-E_a/RT}$$

Where:

  • $k$ is the rate constant
  • $A$ is the Arrhenius factor (or pre-exponential factor, frequency factor)
  • $E_a$ is the activation energy (in J/mol or kJ/mol)
  • $R$ is the universal gas constant (8.314 J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$)
  • $T$ is the absolute temperature (in Kelvin)

Logarithmic form: $\ln k = \ln A - \frac{E_a}{RT}$

For two different temperatures ($T_1, T_2$) and rate constants ($k_1, k_2$):

$$\ln \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{R} \left( \frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2} \right)$$

Or in base 10 log:

$$\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R} \left( \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right)$$

Activation Energy Diagram:

Illustrates the energy changes during a reaction:

$$ \text{Reactants} \xrightarrow{E_a} \text{Activated Complex} \xrightarrow{-\Delta H} \text{Products} $$

A catalyst lowers $E_a$ without changing $\Delta H$ (enthalpy change).

3. Solved High-Yield CBSE Board PYQs

Question 1: First Order Reaction Calculation

A first order reaction is 20% complete in 10 minutes. Calculate the time taken for the reaction to be 80% complete.

Solution:

Step 1: Calculate the rate constant (k) using the given data.

For a first order reaction, $k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]_t}$

Let initial concentration $[R]_0 = 100$.

When reaction is 20% complete, $[R]_t = 100 - 20 = 80$. Time $t = 10$ min.

$k = \frac{2.303}{10} \log \frac{100}{80}$

$k = \frac{2.303}{10} \log(1.25) = \frac{2.303}{10} \times 0.0969$

$k = 0.0223 \text{ min}^{-1}$

(1 mark for correct formula and substitution, 1 mark for correct k value)

Step 2: Calculate the time for 80% completion using the calculated 'k'.

When reaction is 80% complete, $[R]_t = 100 - 80 = 20$.

$t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]_t}$

$t = \frac{2.303}{0.0223} \log \frac{100}{20}$

$t = \frac{2.303}{0.0223} \log(5) = \frac{2.303}{0.0223} \times 0.6989$

$t = 72.37 \text{ minutes}$

(1 mark for correct formula and substitution, 1 mark for final answer)

Question 2: Determining Order from Experimental Data

For a reaction $A + B \to P$, the following data were obtained:

Experiment $[A]_0$ (M) $[B]_0$ (M) Initial Rate (M s$^{-1}$)
1 0.1 0.1 6.0 × 10$^{-3}$
2 0.3 0.2 7.2 × 10$^{-2}$
3 0.3 0.4 2.88 × 10$^{-1}$
4 0.4 0.1 2.40 × 10$^{-2}$

Determine the order of reaction with respect to A and B, and the overall order of the reaction.

Solution:

Step 1: Write the general rate law.

$Rate = k[A]^x[B]^y$

(1/2 mark)

Step 2: Determine the order with respect to B (y).

Compare Experiments 2 and 3 where $[A]_0$ is constant.

$\frac{Rate_3}{Rate_2} = \frac{k[A]_3^x[B]_3^y}{k[A]_2^x[B]_2^y}$

$\frac{2.88 \times 10^{-1}}{7.2 \times 10^{-2}} = \frac{k(0.3)^x(0.4)^y}{k(0.3)^x(0.2)^y}$

$4 = \left( \frac{0.4}{0.2} \right)^y \Rightarrow 4 = (2)^y$

Therefore, $y = 2$. (Order with respect to B is 2).

(1 mark for comparing correct experiments and calculating y)

Step 3: Determine the order with respect to A (x).

Compare Experiments 1 and 4 where $[B]_0$ is constant.

$\frac{Rate_4}{Rate_1} = \frac{k[A]_4^x[B]_4^y}{k[A]_1^x[B]_1^y}$

$\frac{2.40 \times 10^{-2}}{6.0 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{k(0.4)^x(0.1)^y}{k(0.1)^x(0.1)^y}$

$4 = \left( \frac{0.4}{0.1} \right)^x \Rightarrow 4 = (4)^x$

Therefore, $x = 1$. (Order with respect to A is 1).

(1 mark for comparing correct experiments and calculating x)

Step 4: Determine the overall order.

Overall order = $x + y = 1 + 2 = 3$.

(1/2 mark for overall order)

Question 3: Arrhenius Equation Application

The rate constants of a reaction at 500 K and 700 K are $0.02 \text{ s}^{-1}$ and $0.07 \text{ s}^{-1}$ respectively. Calculate the activation energy ($E_a$) of the reaction. ($R = 8.314 \text{ J K}^{-1} \text{ mol}^{-1}$)

Solution:

Step 1: List the given values.

$T_1 = 500 \text{ K}$, $k_1 = 0.02 \text{ s}^{-1}$

$T_2 = 700 \text{ K}$, $k_2 = 0.07 \text{ s}^{-1}$

$R = 8.314 \text{ J K}^{-1} \text{ mol}^{-1}$

(1/2 mark for identifying correct values)

Step 2: Use the integrated form of the Arrhenius equation.

$\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R} \left( \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right)$

(1/2 mark for correct formula)

Step 3: Substitute the values and solve for $E_a$.

$\log \frac{0.07}{0.02} = \frac{E_a}{2.303 \times 8.314} \left( \frac{700 - 500}{500 \times 700} \right)$

$\log(3.5) = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \left( \frac{200}{350000} \right)$

$0.544 = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \left( \frac{2}{3500} \right)$

$0.544 = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \times 0.0005714$

$E_a = \frac{0.544 \times 19.147}{0.0005714}$

$E_a = 18230.8 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$

Or $E_a = 18.23 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

(1.5 marks for correct substitution and calculation, 0.5 mark for final answer with units)

4. Quick Revision Summary Box

  • Rate: Change in conc./time. $Rate = -\frac{1}{a}\frac{d[A]}{dt} = +\frac{1}{c}\frac{d[C]}{dt}$.
  • Factors: Conc., Temp., Catalyst, Surface area.
  • Rate Law: $Rate = k[A]^x[B]^y$. Order = $x+y$. (Experimentally determined).
  • Molecularity: No. of species colliding in an elementary step (theoretical, max 3).
  • Zero Order: $[R]_t = [R]_0 - kt$, $t_{1/2} = [R]_0 / 2k$. Units of $k$: $\text{mol L}^{-1} \text{s}^{-1}$.
  • First Order: $k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]_t}$, $t_{1/2} = 0.693 / k$. Units of $k$: $\text{s}^{-1}$.
  • Arrhenius Eq.: $k = A e^{-E_a/RT}$. $\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R} \left( \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right)$.
  • Activation Energy ($E_a$): Energy barrier. Catalyst lowers $E_a$.
