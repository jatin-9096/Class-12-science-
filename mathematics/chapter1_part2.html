Practice Questions

Attempt these problems to master the concepts of Linear Programming.

Practice Question 1 (5 marks)

Maximise the profit function $P = 40x + 30y$ subject to the constraints

$$\begin{cases} 2x + y \le 100,\\ x + 3y \le 120,\\ x \ge 0,\; y \ge 0. \end{cases}$$

Marking Scheme:

  • Formulating the LPP – 1 mark
  • Drawing the feasible region – 1 mark
  • Identifying corner points – 1 mark
  • Evaluating $P$ at each corner point – 1 mark
  • Stating the maximum profit and the corresponding values of $x$ and $y$ – 1 mark

Practice Question 2 (5 marks)

A manufacturer produces two types of gadgets, A and B. The profit per unit is $Rs.~150$ for A and $Rs.~200$ for B. The production process requires two machines. The time (in hours) required on Machine 1 is $3$ for A and $2$ for B; on Machine 2 it is $2$ for A and $4$ for B. Each machine is available for at most $120$ hours per week. Formulate and solve the LPP to obtain the weekly production schedule that maximises profit.

Marking Scheme:

  • Definition of decision variables – 1 mark
  • Objective function formulation – 1 mark
  • Writing the constraints correctly – 1 mark
  • Solution by graphical method (feasible region, corner points) – 1 mark
  • Optimal production quantities and maximum profit – 1 mark

Practice Question 3 (3 marks)

Find the values of $x$ and $y$ that satisfy the equations $2x + 3y = 12$ and $x - y = 1$ using the graphical method.

Marking Scheme:

  • Plotting both lines correctly – 1 mark
  • Identifying the point of intersection – 1 mark
  • Stating the solution $(x, y)$ – 1 mark

Practice Question 4 (3 marks)

Determine whether the point $(4,5)$ lies inside, on, or outside the feasible region defined by $x + y \le 10$, $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.

Marking Scheme:

  • Substituting the point into each inequality – 1 mark
  • Conclusion about the position of the point – 1 mark
  • Brief justification – 1 mark

Practice Question 5 (3 marks)

For the LPP with constraints $x \ge 2$, $y \ge 3$, and $x + y \le 10$, find the maximum value of $Z = 5x + 2y$.

Marking Scheme:

  • Sketching the feasible region – 1 mark
  • Evaluating $Z$ at the corner points – 1 mark
  • Stating the maximum value and the corresponding point – 1 mark

Practice Question 6 (2 marks)

Write the standard form of the LPP: maximise $3x + 4y$ subject to $x + 2y \le 8$, $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.

Marking Scheme:

  • Introducing slack variable and expressing constraints as equations – 1 mark
  • Stating the objective function in terms of variables – 1 mark

Practice Question 7 (2 marks)

State the condition for a point to be an optimal solution in a linear programming problem.

Marking Scheme:

  • Correct statement that the optimal solution occurs at a corner (extreme) point of the feasible region – 1 mark
  • Mention that if multiple optimal points exist, they lie on a line segment joining two corner points – 1 mark

Practice Question 8 (2 marks)

What is the effect on the feasible region if the inequality $2x + y \le 15$ is changed to $2x + y \ge 15$?

Marking Scheme:

  • Identifying the shift of the feasible side of the line – 1 mark
  • Brief description of the new feasible region (the half‑plane on the opposite side) – 1 mark

Case Study 1 – Modeling Population Growth in a City

The municipal corporation of a rapidly expanding city wants to predict its population for the next decade. Historical data shows that the population $P(t)$ (in lakhs) at time $t$ (in years) follows a logistic pattern: initially the growth is almost exponential, but as the city approaches its carrying capacity $K$, the growth slows down. The city planners have estimated the carrying capacity to be $K = 12$ lakh and the initial population at $t=0$ to be $P_0 = 3$ lakh. They also observed that the population increased from $3$ lakh to $5$ lakh in the first $2$ years. Using this information, they wish to formulate a mathematical model, solve it, and interpret the results.

  1. Practice Question 1: Derive the differential equation governing the logistic growth of the population, stating any assumptions clearly.
  2. Practice Question 2: Determine the value of the growth constant $r$ using the given data.
  3. Practice Question 3: Solve the differential equation to obtain $P(t)$ in explicit form.
  4. Practice Question 4: Using the obtained model, predict the population after $8$ years and comment on the long‑term behaviour as $t\to\infty$.

Solution

Derivation of the logistic differential equation (Practice Question 1)

The logistic model assumes that the rate of change of the population is proportional both to the existing population and to the fraction of the remaining carrying capacity. Mathematically, $$\frac{dP}{dt}=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right),$$ where $r>0$ is the intrinsic growth rate and $K$ is the carrying capacity. The term $1-\frac{P}{K}$ reduces the growth as $P$ approaches $K$.

Finding the growth constant $r$ (Practice Question 2)

The solution of the logistic equation can be written as $$P(t)=\frac{K}{1+Ae^{-rt}},\qquad A=\frac{K-P_0}{P_0}.$$ Using $K=12$, $P_0=3$, we get $$A=\frac{12-3}{3}=3.$$ At $t=2$ years, $P(2)=5$, so $$5=\frac{12}{1+3e^{-2r}} \;\Longrightarrow\; 1+3e^{-2r}= \frac{12}{5}=2.4.$$ Hence $3e^{-2r}=1.4$ and $e^{-2r}= \frac{1.4}{3}=0.466\overline{6}$. Taking natural logarithm, $$-2r=\ln 0.466\overline{6}\;\Longrightarrow\; r=-\frac{1}{2}\ln 0.466\overline{6}\approx -\frac{1}{2}(-0.763)=0.3815\; \text{yr}^{-1}.$$ Therefore $r\approx0.38\ \text{yr}^{-1}$.

Explicit solution (Practice Question 3)

Substituting $A=3$ and $r\approx0.3815$ into the general solution, $$P(t)=\frac{12}{1+3e^{-0.3815t}}.$$ This expression gives the population (in lakh) at any time $t$ years after the initial measurement.

Prediction for $t=8$ years (Practice Question 4)

Compute $e^{-0.3815\times8}=e^{-3.052}=0.0473$ (approximately). Then $$P(8)=\frac{12}{1+3(0.0473)}=\frac{12}{1+0.1419}= \frac{12}{1.1419}\approx10.51\ \text{lakh}.$$ Hence after eight years the population is expected to be about $10.5$ lakh.

As $t\to\infty$, $e^{-rt}\to0$ and the denominator tends to $1$, so $$\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)=\frac{12}{1}=12\ \text{lakh}=K.$$ The model predicts that the population will asymptotically approach the carrying capacity of $12$ lakh, never exceeding it.

Interpretation: The city’s growth will slow down considerably after reaching about $10$ lakh, and the population will stabilize near $12$ lakh. Urban planners can use this information to schedule infrastructure projects, allocate resources, and design policies that accommodate the expected saturation.

Case Study 2 – Cost‑Effective Design of a Rectangular Garden Adjacent to a River

A horticulture department wants to create a rectangular garden of area $A=2000\ \text{m}^2$ next to a river. The side of the garden that touches the river does not require fencing, while the other three sides must be fenced. The cost of fencing per metre is $\$15$, whereas the cost of preparing the river side (e.g., landscaping) is $\$5$ per metre. The department wishes to minimize the total cost while maintaining the required area. Determine the optimal dimensions of the garden and the minimum total cost.

  1. Practice Question 1: Let the side parallel to the river be $x$ metres and the perpendicular side be $y$ metres. Write the cost function $C(x,y)$ and the area constraint.
  2. Practice Question 2: Using the constraint, express $C$ as a function of a single variable and find its critical point.
  3. Practice Question 3: Verify that the critical point indeed gives a minimum by using the second‑derivative test.
  4. Practice Question 4: Compute the optimal dimensions $x$ and $y$, and state the minimum total cost in dollars.

Solution

Formulating the cost function (Practice Question 1)

The garden has three fenced sides: two sides of length $y$ and one side of length $x$ (the side opposite the river). The cost of fencing is $\$15$ per metre, so the fencing cost is $$C_{\text{fence}}=15(2y+x).$$ The side along the river, of length $x$, requires landscaping at $\$5$ per metre, giving $$C_{\text{river}}=5x.$$ Hence the total cost is $$C(x,y)=15(2y+x)+5x=30y+20x.$$ The area constraint is $$A=xy=2000\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=\frac{2000}{x}.$$

Single‑variable cost function and critical point (Practice Question 2)

Substituting $y=2000/x$ into $C$, $$C(x)=30\left(\frac{2000}{x}\right)+20x=\frac{60000}{x}+20x,\qquad x>0.$$ Differentiate with respect to $x$: $$C'(x)=-\frac{60000}{x^{2}}+20.$$ Set $C'(x)=0$ to locate stationary points: $$-\frac{60000}{x^{2}}+20=0\;\Longrightarrow\;20=\frac{60000}{x^{2}}\;\Longrightarrow\;x^{2}=3000.$$ Hence $$x=\sqrt{3000}=10\sqrt{30}\approx 54.77\ \text{m}.$$ The corresponding $y$ is $$y=\frac{2000}{x}= \frac{2000}{10\sqrt{30}}= \frac{200}{\sqrt{30}}= \frac{200\sqrt{30}}{30}\approx 36.51\ \text{m}.$$

Second‑derivative test (Practice Question 3)

Compute the second derivative: $$C''(x)=\frac{120000}{x^{3}}.$$ For $x>0$, $C''(x)>0$, indicating that $C(x)$ is convex and the critical point yields a global minimum.

Optimal dimensions and minimum cost (Practice Question 4)

Using $x=10\sqrt{30}$ and $y=2000/x$, the minimum total cost is $$C_{\min}=30y+20x=30\left(\frac{2000}{10\sqrt{30}}\right)+20\left(10\sqrt{30}\right).$$ Simplify: $$C_{\min}= \frac{60000}{10\sqrt{30}}+200\sqrt{30} =\frac{6000}{\sqrt{30}}+200\sqrt{30} =\frac{6000}{\sqrt{30}}+\frac{200\cdot30}{\sqrt{30}} =\frac{6000+6000}{\sqrt{30}} =\frac{12000}{\sqrt{30}}.$$ Rationalising, $$C_{\min}= \frac{12000\sqrt{30}}{30}=400\sqrt{30}\approx 400\times5.477=2190.8\ \text{dollars}.$$ Therefore, the garden should be approximately $54.8\ \text{m}$ along the river and $36.5\ \text{m}$ perpendicular to it, costing about $\$2191$.

Interpretation: By allowing the river side to serve as a natural boundary, the department saves a substantial amount compared to fencing all four sides. The calculus‑based optimisation confirms that the cost‑effective dimensions are not square but elongated along the river, which is a common feature in real‑world design problems where one side is cheaper or free.
