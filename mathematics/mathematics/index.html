Concept Core

1. Theoretical Principles & Definitions

§
Relations & Functions

A relation $R$ from a set $A$ to a set $B$ is a subset of $A \times B$. A function $f: A \to B$ is a special type of relation where every element of $A$ has one and only one image in $B$. Types of Relations:

  • **Reflexive:** $(a, a) \in R$ for all $a \in A$.
  • **Symmetric:** $(a, b) \in R \implies (b, a) \in R$.
  • **Transitive:** $(a, b) \in R$ and $(b, c) \in R \implies (a, c) \in R$.
  • **Equivalence Relation:** A relation that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.
Types of Functions:
  • **One-to-one (Injective):** Different elements of $A$ have different images in $B$.
  • **Onto (Surjective):** Every element in $B$ is the image of at least one element in $A$.
  • **Bijective:** A function that is both one-to-one and onto.
  • **Inverse Function:** If $f: A \to B$ is bijective, then $f^{-1}: B \to A$ exists.

§
Matrices & Determinants

A **Matrix** is an ordered rectangular array of numbers or functions. An $m \times n$ matrix has $m$ rows and $n$ columns. Types: Row, Column, Square, Diagonal, Scalar, Identity, Zero matrix. Operations: Addition, Subtraction, Scalar Multiplication, Matrix Multiplication (not commutative).
A **Determinant** is a scalar value associated with a square matrix. Denoted by $\det(A)$ or $|A|$. Properties of Determinants are crucial for evaluation and solving systems of linear equations.
**Adjoint of a matrix:** The transpose of the cofactor matrix. $\text{adj}(A) = (\text{cofactor}(A))^T$. **Inverse of a matrix:** $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A)$, provided $|A| \neq 0$.

§
Calculus: Continuity & Differentiability, Integration

A function $f(x)$ is **continuous** at a point $c$ if $\lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = f(c)$. Graphically, no breaks or jumps. A function $f(x)$ is **differentiable** at a point $c$ if its derivative $f'(c)$ exists. This implies the left-hand derivative equals the right-hand derivative, and the function is continuous at $c$.
**Integration** is the inverse process of differentiation. It is finding an antiderivative. Indefinite Integral: $\int f(x) dx = F(x) + C$, where $F'(x) = f(x)$ and $C$ is the constant of integration. Definite Integral: $\int_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a)$. Represents the area under the curve from $a$ to $b$.

§
Vectors & 3D Geometry

A **Vector** is a quantity that has both magnitude and direction (e.g., displacement, velocity). Represented by $\vec{a} = a_1\hat{i} + a_2\hat{j} + a_3\hat{k}$. Magnitude: $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}$. Types of vector products:

  • **Scalar (Dot) Product:** $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}| \cos\theta$. Result is a scalar.
  • **Vector (Cross) Product:** $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}| \sin\theta \hat{n}$. Result is a vector perpendicular to both $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$.
**3D Geometry:** Deals with points, lines, and planes in three dimensions. Direction Cosines: Cosines of angles made by a line with positive $x, y, z$ axes ($l, m, n$). $l^2+m^2+n^2=1$. Direction Ratios: Any numbers proportional to direction cosines ($a, b, c$).

§
Probability

**Conditional Probability:** The probability of event $A$ occurring, given that event $B$ has already occurred. $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$, provided $P(B) > 0$.
**Independent Events:** Two events $A$ and $B$ are independent if $P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$. This implies $P(A|B) = P(A)$ and $P(B|A) = P(B)$.
**Bayes' Theorem:** Relates conditional probabilities. It's used to find the probability of a cause, given that an effect has occurred. $P(E_i|A) = \frac{P(A|E_i)P(E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A|E_j)P(E_j)}$, where $E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n$ are a partition of the sample space.

2. Essential Equations & Derivations

Differentiation Rules & Derivatives

**Product Rule:** If $y = u \cdot v$, then $\frac{dy}{dx} = u \frac{dv}{dx} + v \frac{du}{dx}$.
**Quotient Rule:** If $y = \frac{u}{v}$, then $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{v \frac{du}{dx} - u \frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$.
**Chain Rule:** If $y = f(g(x))$, then $\frac{dy}{dx} = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$.
**Inverse Trigonometric Derivatives:** $$ \frac{d}{dx}(\sin^{-1} x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} $$ $$ \frac{d}{dx}(\tan^{-1} x) = \frac{1}{1 + x^2} $$

Integration Techniques & Properties

**Integration by Parts:** $\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$. A mnemonic is LIATE for choosing $u$.
**Important Standard Integrals:** $$ \int \frac{1}{x^2 + a^2} dx = \frac{1}{a} \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C $$ $$ \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}} dx = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) + C $$ **Definite Integral Property:** $\int_a^b f(x) dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x) dx$. Used for simplifying many definite integrals.

Matrices & Determinants

**Inverse of a Matrix:** For a square matrix $A$, if $|A| \neq 0$, then $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A)$.
**Properties of Determinants (Examples):** * $|AB| = |A||B|$ * $|A^T| = |A|$ * If two rows/columns are identical or proportional, $|A|=0$. * If a row/column is multiplied by $k$, determinant is multiplied by $k$.

Vectors & 3D Geometry

**Scalar (Dot) Product:** For $\vec{a} = a_1\hat{i} + a_2\hat{j} + a_3\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = b_1\hat{i} + b_2\hat{j} + b_3\hat{k}$: $$ \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3 = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta $$ **Vector (Cross) Product:** $$ \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} $$ **Shortest Distance between Skew Lines:** If lines are $\vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + \lambda \vec{b_1}$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + \mu \vec{b_2}$: $$ d = \left| \frac{(\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}) \cdot (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})}{|\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}|} \right| $$

Probability: Bayes' Theorem

For events $E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n$ forming a partition of the sample space, and $A$ is any event with $P(A) \neq 0$: $$ P(E_i|A) = \frac{P(A|E_i)P(E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A|E_j)P(E_j)} $$ This formula is central to problems involving inverse probability.

Board Examination Questions

3. Solved High-Yield CBSE PYQs

Q1

Application of Derivatives: Maxima/Minima

Problem: Find the maximum profit that a company can make, if the profit function is given by $P(x) = 41 - 72x - 18x^2$, where $x$ is the number of units produced.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given profit function: $P(x) = 41 - 72x - 18x^2$.
  2. To find maximum profit, differentiate $P(x)$ with respect to $x$ and set to zero ($P'(x) = 0$). $P'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(41 - 72x - 18x^2) = -72 - 36x$.
  3. Set $P'(x) = 0$: $-72 - 36x = 0 \implies 36x = -72 \implies x = -2$.
  4. To confirm it's a maximum, find the second derivative $P''(x)$. $P''(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(-72 - 36x) = -36$.
  5. Since $P''(-2) = -36 < 0$, the profit is maximum at $x = -2$.
  6. Substitute $x = -2$ into the profit function to find the maximum profit: $P(-2) = 41 - 72(-2) - 18(-2)^2 = 41 + 144 - 18(4) = 41 + 144 - 72 = 113$.
**Answer:** The maximum profit the company can make is $113$ units (assuming appropriate monetary units). *(Note: In real-world problems, $x$ must be non-negative. This problem is illustrative of the calculus process.)*

Q2

Vectors: Area of a Parallelogram

Problem: Find the area of the parallelogram whose adjacent sides are given by the vectors $\vec{a} = 3\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = \hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k}$.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. The area of a parallelogram with adjacent sides $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is given by the magnitude of their cross product, i.e., $|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|$.
  2. Calculate the cross product $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$: $$ \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 3 & 1 & 4 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} $$ $= \hat{i}(1 \cdot 1 - 4 \cdot (-1)) - \hat{j}(3 \cdot 1 - 4 \cdot 1) + \hat{k}(3 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1)$ $= \hat{i}(1 + 4) - \hat{j}(3 - 4) + \hat{k}(-3 - 1)$ $= 5\hat{i} + \hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$.
  3. Calculate the magnitude of the resulting vector: $$ |\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = \sqrt{5^2 + 1^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 1 + 16} = \sqrt{42} $$
**Answer:** The area of the parallelogram is $\sqrt{42}$ square units.

Q3

Probability: Bayes' Theorem

Problem: A bag contains 4 red and 4 black balls, another bag contains 2 red and 6 black balls. One of the bags is selected at random and a ball is drawn from it. If the ball drawn is red, what is the probability that it was drawn from the first bag?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Let $E_1$ be the event that bag I is selected, and $E_2$ be the event that bag II is selected. $P(E_1) = P(E_2) = \frac{1}{2}$ (since one bag is selected at random).
  2. Let $A$ be the event that a red ball is drawn.
  3. Calculate $P(A|E_1)$ (probability of drawing a red ball from bag I): Bag I has 4 red and 4 black balls (total 8). $P(A|E_1) = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$.
  4. Calculate $P(A|E_2)$ (probability of drawing a red ball from bag II): Bag II has 2 red and 6 black balls (total 8). $P(A|E_2) = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}$.
  5. We need to find $P(E_1|A)$ (probability that the ball was drawn from bag I, given it is red). Use Bayes' Theorem: $$ P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(A|E_1)P(E_1)}{P(A|E_1)P(E_1) + P(A|E_2)P(E_2)} $$ $$ P(E_1|A) = \frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)} $$ $$ P(E_1|A) = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8}} = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{2+1}{8}} = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{3}{8}} = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{8}{3} = \frac{2}{3} $$
**Answer:** The probability that the red ball was drawn from the first bag is $\frac{2}{3}$.

Fast Revision

4. Quick Mind-Map & Summary

Core Rule 1 (Calculus) Master differentiation and integration formulas. Practice substitution, by parts, and partial fractions extensively. Remember applications of derivatives (tangents/normals, maxima/minima, rate of change) and definite integral properties.
Core Rule 2 (Algebra) Understand matrix operations, especially multiplication and finding the inverse. For determinants, properties are key for quick evaluation. Solve systems of equations using matrix inversion or Cramer's rule (if taught).
Core Rule 3 (Vectors & 3D) Clearly distinguish between scalar and vector products and their geometric interpretations (area, volume). Memorize formulas for lines (vector & Cartesian forms), planes, shortest distance between skew/parallel lines, and angle between lines/planes.
Core Rule 4 (Probability) Focus on Conditional Probability, Independent Events, Total Probability Theorem, and Bayes' Theorem. Define events clearly and organize your steps for complex problems. Binomial distribution concepts are also important.
Board Tip (General) Show all steps clearly. Double-check calculations. Practice graphical representations for functions, LPP, and areas under curves. Don't forget Inverse Trigonometric Functions and their properties.
Common Mistake Misapplying chain rule in differentiation or forgetting the constant of integration ($C$) in indefinite integrals. Also, mixing up dot and cross product properties, or errors in matrix multiplication order.
