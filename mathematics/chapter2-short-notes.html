Key Definitions & Laws

Key Definitions & Laws

  • Inverse Trigonometric Function: For a one‑to‑one restriction of a trig function $f$, its inverse $f^{-1}$ satisfies $f\left(f^{-1}(x)\right)=x$ for $x$ in the domain of $f^{-1}$.
  • Principal Value Ranges (as per CBSE):
    • $\sin^{-1}x\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]$
    • $\cos^{-1}x\in[0,\pi]$
    • $\tan^{-1}x\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$
  • Co‑function Identities:
    $\sin^{-1}x+\cos^{-1}x=\frac{\pi}{2}$
    $\sec^{-1}x+\csc^{-1}x=\frac{\pi}{2}$
  • Complementary Angle Relations:
    $\tan^{-1}x+\cot^{-1}x=\frac{\pi}{2}$ (for $x>0$)
  • Odd–Even Properties:
    $\sin^{-1}(-x)=-\sin^{-1}x$, $\tan^{-1}(-x)=-\tan^{-1}x$, $\cos^{-1}(-x)=\pi-\cos^{-1}x$

Graphs & Theorems Summary

Graphs & Theorems Summary

  • General Shape: All three inverse functions are continuous, strictly monotonic and pass through the origin (except $\cos^{-1}x$, which passes through $(1,0)$).
  • Symmetry:
    • $y=\sin^{-1}x$ and $y=\tan^{-1}x$ are odd functions (symmetric about the origin).
    • $y=\cos^{-1}x$ is neither odd nor even but satisfies $\cos^{-1}(-x)=\pi-\cos^{-1}x$.
  • Key Theorems:
    • Derivative Theorem: If $y=\sin^{-1}x$, then $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}$ (similarly for $\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$ as listed in the formula table).
    • Integration by Parts yields the standard integrals shown in the formula table.
  • Graph Sketch Tips:
    • Mark end‑points using domain‑range limits.
    • Use asymptotes for $\tan^{-1}x$ at $y=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$.
    • Show smooth curvature; no sharp corners within the principal interval.

Common Exam Mistakes

Common Exam Mistakes

  • Confusing domain and range – always write domain $[-1,1]$ for $\sin^{-1}$ and $\cos^{-1}$, and range as per principal values.
  • Using $\sin^{-1}x$ as $\arcsin x$ without restricting $x$ to $[-1,1]$ in integration problems.
  • Sign error in derivatives: $\frac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}x$ is negative.
  • For $\tan^{-1}x$, forgetting the vertical asymptotes $y=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ while sketching the graph.
  • Applying co‑function identity incorrectly for negative arguments – remember $\cos^{-1}(-x)=\pi-\cos^{-1}x$.
  • Mixing up $\sec^{-1}x$ and $\csc^{-1}x$ ranges; they follow the same principal values as $\cos^{-1}$ and $\sin^{-1}$ respectively.
