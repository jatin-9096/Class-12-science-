Chapter 11: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

Subject: Chemistry | Class: 12

This chapter explores the structure, nomenclature, methods of preparation, physical and chemical properties, and uses of alcohols, phenols, and ethers. It is crucial for understanding organic reactions and synthesis.

1. Comprehensive Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

Alcohols, phenols, and ethers are organic compounds characterized by the presence of a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to an alkyl group (alcohols), an aryl group (phenols), or an oxygen atom bridging two alkyl/aryl groups (ethers), respectively.

1.1. Alcohols

  • Definition: Hydroxyl derivatives of alkanes, general formula $R-OH$. Classified as primary ($1^\circ$), secondary ($2^\circ$), or tertiary ($3^\circ$) based on the carbon atom to which the -OH group is attached.
  • Nomenclature: IUPAC rules (longest carbon chain, -ol suffix, position of -OH) and common names (alkyl alcohol).
  • Preparation:
    • From Alkenes (acid-catalyzed hydration, hydroboration-oxidation, oxymercuration-demercuration).
    • From Carbonyl Compounds (reduction of aldehydes/ketones by $LiAlH_4$ or $NaBH_4$; reduction of carboxylic acids/esters by $LiAlH_4$).
    • From Grignard Reagents ($RMgX$ reacts with aldehydes/ketones to form $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ alcohols).
  • Physical Properties: Higher boiling points than corresponding alkanes due to intermolecular H-bonding. Soluble in water (lower members) due to H-bonding with water.
  • Chemical Reactions:
    • Acidity: Weakly acidic, acidity order $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$ (due to inductive effect). React with active metals (Na, K) to form alkoxides.
    • Esterification: Reaction with carboxylic acids or acid derivatives in presence of acid catalyst to form esters.
    • Reaction with HX: Formation of alkyl halides ($S_N1$ for $3^\circ$, $S_N2$ for $1^\circ$). Lucas test uses HCl/ZnCl₂.
    • Dehydration: Elimination of water to form alkenes (acid-catalyzed, $E_1$ mechanism for $2^\circ/3^\circ$, $E_2$ for $1^\circ$). Ease of dehydration: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$.
    • Oxidation: $1^\circ$ alcohols $\to$ aldehydes $\to$ carboxylic acids; $2^\circ$ alcohols $\to$ ketones; $3^\circ$ alcohols are resistant to oxidation under mild conditions. Oxidizing agents: $PCC, CrO_3, KMnO_4, K_2Cr_2O_7$.

1.2. Phenols

  • Definition: Hydroxyl derivatives of arenes, where the -OH group is directly attached to an aromatic ring. General formula $Ar-OH$.
  • Nomenclature: Simplest is phenol. Substituted phenols are named as derivatives of phenol.
  • Preparation:
    • From Haloarenes (Dow's process).
    • From Benzene Sulphonic Acid (fusion with NaOH).
    • From Diazonium Salts (hydrolysis).
    • From Cumene (industrial method).
  • Physical Properties: Higher boiling points than aromatic hydrocarbons due to H-bonding. Sparingly soluble in water.
  • Chemical Reactions:
    • Acidity: More acidic than alcohols due to resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion. Acidity is increased by electron-withdrawing groups (EWG) and decreased by electron-donating groups (EDG). React with NaOH (stronger base than Na, K).
    • Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution: -OH group is ortho-para directing and strongly activating.
      • Nitration (dilute $HNO_3$ gives o- and p-nitrophenol; conc. $HNO_3$ gives picric acid).
      • Bromination (bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol; $Br_2/CS_2$ gives o- and p-bromophenol).
      • Kolbe's Reaction: Formation of salicylic acid (o-hydroxybenzoic acid) by reaction with $CO_2$ and subsequent acidification.
      • Reimer-Tiemann Reaction: Formation of salicylaldehyde by reaction with $CHCl_3/NaOH$ and subsequent acidification.
      • Friedel-Crafts Alkylation/Acylation (not usually good due to complex formation with Lewis acid).
    • Oxidation: To quinones (e.g., with $Na_2Cr_2O_7/H_2SO_4$).
    • Reduction: To benzene (with Zinc dust).
  • Distinguishing Test: Ferric Chloride Test (phenols give characteristic violet, green, or blue coloration with neutral $FeCl_3$ solution).

1.3. Ethers

  • Definition: Organic compounds where an oxygen atom is bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups. General formula $R-O-R'$, $Ar-O-R'$, or $Ar-O-Ar'$. Can be symmetrical ($R=R'$) or unsymmetrical ($R \ne R'$).
  • Nomenclature: Common names (alkyl alkyl ether, e.g., diethyl ether). IUPAC names (alkoxyalkane, e.g., ethoxyethane).
  • Preparation:
    • Williamson's Ether Synthesis: Reaction of an alkyl halide (preferably $1^\circ$) with a sodium alkoxide or phenoxide. Best for $1^\circ$ alkyl halides to avoid elimination.
    • From Alcohols (dehydration of alcohols by conc. $H_2SO_4$ at specific temperatures, e.g., $140^\circ C$ for symmetrical ethers).
  • Physical Properties: Lower boiling points than corresponding alcohols (no H-bonding). Less soluble in water than alcohols. Highly volatile and flammable.
  • Chemical Reactions:
    • Cleavage by HX: Ethers react with hot concentrated HX (HI, HBr, HCl) to form alkyl halides and alcohol (or phenol).
      • Mechanism depends on $1^\circ/2^\circ$ or $3^\circ$ nature: $S_N2$ for $1^\circ/2^\circ$ alkyl groups; $S_N1$ for $3^\circ$ alkyl groups.
      • Aryl-alkyl ethers give phenol and alkyl halide.
    • Electrophilic Substitution (in aromatic ethers): -OR group is ortho-para directing and activating. (e.g., Anisole undergoes nitration, bromination, Friedel-Crafts reactions).
    • Peroxide Formation: Ethers slowly react with atmospheric oxygen to form highly explosive peroxides.

2. Key Reaction Mechanisms / Core Formulas

Core Formulas:

  • Alcohols: $C_nH_{2n+1}OH$ or $R-OH$
  • Phenols: $Ar-OH$ (e.g., $C_6H_5OH$)
  • Ethers: $R-O-R'$, $Ar-O-R'$, or $Ar-O-Ar'$

Key Reaction Mechanisms:

2.1. Dehydration of Alcohols (e.g., $E_1$ for $2^\circ/3^\circ$ alcohols)

Step 1: Protonation of Alcohol

$$ CH_3-CH_2-OH + H^+ \rightleftharpoons CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2 $$

Step 2: Formation of Carbocation (slow, rate-determining step)

$$ CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2 \longrightarrow CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H_2 + H_2O $$

Step 3: Deprotonation to form Alkene

$$ CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H_2 \longrightarrow CH_2=CH_2 + H^+ $$

2.2. Williamson's Ether Synthesis

This is an $S_N2$ reaction.

$$ R-X + Na^+O^--R' \longrightarrow R-O-R' + NaX $$

Example: $CH_3CH_2-Br + Na^+O^-CH_3 \longrightarrow CH_3CH_2-O-CH_3 + NaBr$

Mechanism (for $1^\circ$ alkyl halide): Nucleophilic attack of alkoxide on the primary alkyl halide, displacing the halide ion.

Best for $1^\circ$ alkyl halides. If $3^\circ$ alkyl halide is used, elimination ($E2$) predominates, forming alkenes.

2.3. Cleavage of Ethers by HX (e.g., with HI)

Case 1: Primary/Secondary Alkyl Groups (predominantly $S_N2$)

$$ R-O-R' + HI \longrightarrow R-OH + R'-I $$

Mechanism involves protonation of ether, followed by nucleophilic attack of $I^-$ on the less hindered carbon.

Case 2: Tertiary Alkyl Group / Phenyl-Alkyl Ether (predominantly $S_N1$ for $3^\circ$ alkyl; Phenol for Aryl-alkyl ether)

$$ (CH_3)_3C-O-CH_3 + HI \longrightarrow (CH_3)_3C-I + CH_3OH $$

Mechanism involves protonation, then carbocation formation ($3^\circ$ carbocation is stable), followed by $I^-$ attack.

For $Ar-O-R$: The $C_{aryl}-O$ bond is stable due to resonance and does not cleave. The $C_{alkyl}-O$ bond cleaves.

$$ C_6H_5-O-CH_3 + HI \longrightarrow C_6H_5-OH + CH_3I $$

2.4. Kolbe's Reaction (Phenols)

Phenoxide ion reacts with $CO_2$ (a weak electrophile) under pressure and heating, followed by acidification to yield salicylic acid.

  1. $C_6H_5OH + NaOH \longrightarrow C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + H_2O$
  2. $C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + CO_2 \xrightarrow{140^\circ C, 4-7 atm} \text{Intermediate}$
  3. $\text{Intermediate} + H^+ \longrightarrow \text{Salicylic Acid (o-hydroxybenzoic acid)}$
$$ \xrightarrow{1. NaOH, 2. CO_2/140^\circ C, 4-7 atm, 3. H_3O^+} $$ $$ C_6H_5OH \longrightarrow o-HOC_6H_4COOH $$

3. Solved High-Yield CBSE Board PYQs

Question 1: Conversions

Q: How will you convert:

(i) Propene to Propan-1-ol

(ii) Anisole to Phenol

(iii) Phenol to Salicylaldehyde

Solution:

(i) Propene to Propan-1-ol (1 mark)

This conversion requires anti-Markovnikov addition of water. Hydroboration-oxidation is the suitable method.

$$ CH_3-CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{1. (BH_3)_2, THF \\ 2. H_2O_2, OH^-} CH_3-CH_2-CH_2-OH $$

Marking Scheme: 1 mark for correct reagents and product.

(ii) Anisole to Phenol (1 mark)

Anisole ($C_6H_5-O-CH_3$) is a methoxybenzene. To convert it to phenol, we need to cleave the alkyl-oxygen bond.

$$ C_6H_5-O-CH_3 \xrightarrow{HI (conc.), heat} C_6H_5-OH + CH_3I $$

Marking Scheme: 1 mark for correct reagent (HI/HBr) and product.

(iii) Phenol to Salicylaldehyde (1 mark)

This is a named reaction, Reimer-Tiemann reaction.

$$ C_6H_5OH \xrightarrow{1. CHCl_3, NaOH(aq) \\ 2. H_3O^+} o-HOC_6H_4CHO \text{ (Salicylaldehyde)} $$

Marking Scheme: 1 mark for correct reagents and named reaction (if mentioned, optional but good).

Question 2: Reasoning (Acidity)

Q: Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their acidic strength: Propan-1-ol, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, 3-nitrophenol, Phenol.

Solution:

The acidic strength of phenols depends on the stabilization of the phenoxide ion by electron-withdrawing groups (EWG). Nitro groups are strong EWGs, and their effect is more pronounced at ortho and para positions.

  • Propan-1-ol: A simple alcohol, very weakly acidic ($pK_a \approx 16-18$).
  • Phenol: More acidic than alcohols due to resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion ($pK_a \approx 10$).
  • 3-nitrophenol: Nitro group at meta position is an EWG, but its resonance effect is not direct. It exerts an inductive electron-withdrawing effect, making it more acidic than phenol ($pK_a \approx 8.4$).
  • 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (Picric acid): Three strong nitro EWGs (two ortho, one para) strongly stabilize the phenoxide ion, making it highly acidic, even comparable to mineral acids ($pK_a \approx 0.3$).

Increasing order of acidic strength:

Propan-1-ol < Phenol < 3-nitrophenol < 2,4,6-trinitrophenol

Marking Scheme:

  • 1 mark for understanding the general trend (alcohol < phenol).
  • 1 mark for correctly comparing substituted phenols based on number and position of nitro groups.
  • 1 mark for the final correct order.

Question 3: Mechanism / Distinguishing Test

Q: Give a chemical test to distinguish between phenol and ethanol. Write the mechanism of dehydration of ethanol to ethene.

Solution:

Distinguishing Test (1.5 marks):

Ferric Chloride Test:

  • Add a few drops of neutral $FeCl_3$ solution to both ethanol and phenol.
  • Phenol: Gives a characteristic violet/blue/green coloration due to the formation of a complex.
    • $$ 6C_6H_5OH + FeCl_3 \longrightarrow [Fe(OC_6H_5)_6]^{3-} + 3H^+ + 3HCl $$
  • Ethanol: Does not give any characteristic coloration.

Marking Scheme: 0.5 mark for mentioning $FeCl_3$ test. 0.5 mark for observation with phenol. 0.5 mark for observation with ethanol.

Mechanism of dehydration of ethanol to ethene (2.5 marks):

Dehydration of ethanol to ethene occurs via an $E_1$ mechanism under acidic conditions (e.g., conc. $H_2SO_4$ at $170^\circ C$).

Step 1: Protonation of Ethanol (Fast, reversible)

The oxygen atom of ethanol gets protonated by the acid, forming protonated alcohol (ethyloxonium ion).

$$ CH_3-CH_2-OH + H^+ \rightleftharpoons CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2 $$

Step 2: Formation of Carbocation (Slow, rate-determining step)

The protonated alcohol loses a water molecule to form a carbocation (ethyl carbocation).

$$ CH_3-CH_2-\overset{+}{O}H_2 \longrightarrow CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H_2 + H_2O $$

Step 3: Deprotonation (Fast)

A proton is removed from the $\beta$-carbon by a base (like $H_2O$ or $HSO_4^-$) leading to the formation of ethene and regeneration of the acid catalyst.

$$ CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H_2 \longrightarrow CH_2=CH_2 + H^+ $$

Marking Scheme: 0.5 mark for each step (protonation, carbocation formation, deprotonation). 1 mark for correctly showing electron movement/charges and products for each step.

4. Quick Revision Summary Box

  • Alcohols (R-OH): Preparation from alkenes/carbonyls/Grignard. Acidity $1^\circ>2^\circ>3^\circ$. React with HX, dehydrate to alkenes, oxidize to aldehydes/ketones/acids.
  • Phenols (Ar-OH): Preparation from haloarenes/diazonium salts/cumene. More acidic than alcohols (resonance stabilized phenoxide). Ortho-para directing in EAS. Key reactions: Kolbe's, Reimer-Tiemann, Bromination, Nitration. $FeCl_3$ test.
  • Ethers (R-O-R'): Preparation by Williamson's synthesis ($1^\circ$ RX + NaOR'). Less reactive. Cleaved by conc. HI/HBr (alkyl halide + alcohol/phenol). Aromatic ethers undergo EAS.
  • Key Mechanisms: Dehydration of alcohols ($E_1$), Williamson's ($S_N2$), Ether cleavage by HX ($S_N1/S_N2$ depending on groups).
  • Acidity Order: 2,4,6-trinitrophenol > 3-nitrophenol > Phenol > Alcohols. Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity.
  • Boiling Points: Alcohols > Phenols > Ethers > Hydrocarbons (due to H-bonding).
