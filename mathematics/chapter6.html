Chapter 06: Electromagnetic Induction (EMI)

Authored by: Jatin Sandilya

1. Comprehensive Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

Electromagnetic Induction (EMI) is a fundamental phenomenon in electromagnetism where a changing magnetic field through a coil or conductor induces an electromotive force (EMF) and consequently an electric current. This principle forms the basis for many modern electrical technologies, including generators, transformers, and induction motors.

Key Concepts:

  • Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B$): It is a measure of the total number of magnetic field lines passing through a given area. It quantifies the 'amount' of magnetic field passing through a surface.
    • Mathematically, $\Phi_B = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} = BA \cos\theta$, where $\vec{B}$ is the magnetic field, $\vec{A}$ is the area vector, and $\theta$ is the angle between them.
    • S.I. unit: Weber (Wb) or Tesla-meter2 (T m2).
  • Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction:
    • First Law: Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed circuit changes, an EMF is induced in it. This induced EMF lasts only as long as the change in magnetic flux continues.
    • Second Law: The magnitude of the induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit.
      • For a single turn: $\mathcal{E} \propto -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$
      • For a coil with $N$ turns: $\mathcal{E} = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$
  • Lenz's Law: This law states that the direction of the induced EMF or induced current is such that it opposes the cause producing it. It is a direct consequence of the principle of conservation of energy. The negative sign in Faraday's law represents Lenz's Law.
  • Motional EMF: When a conductor moves in a uniform magnetic field, an EMF is induced across its ends. This is due to the Lorentz force acting on the free charge carriers in the conductor.
    • If a straight conductor of length $l$ moves with velocity $\vec{v}$ perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ (and $\vec{l}$ is also perpendicular to both $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{B}$), the induced EMF is $\mathcal{E} = Blv$.
  • Eddy Currents: These are circulating currents induced in bulk pieces of conductors (e.g., metal plates or cores of transformers) when they are subjected to changing magnetic flux. They are often undesirable as they lead to energy loss in the form of heat (Joule heating) and can cause damping effects.
    • Applications: Magnetic braking in trains, induction furnaces, electromagnetic damping in galvanometers.
    • Minimization: Using laminated cores in transformers.
  • Self-Induction: It is the phenomenon of induction of EMF in a coil due to a change in current or magnetic flux in the *same* coil.
    • Self-Inductance (L): The ratio of the magnetic flux linked with a coil to the current flowing through it. $\Phi_B = LI$.
    • Induced EMF: $\mathcal{E} = -L \frac{dI}{dt}$.
    • S.I. unit: Henry (H).
    • Energy stored in an inductor: $U = \frac{1}{2}LI^2$.
  • Mutual Induction: It is the phenomenon of induction of EMF in one coil due to a change in current or magnetic flux in a *neighbouring* coil.
    • Mutual Inductance (M): If $\Phi_{B2}$ is the magnetic flux linked with coil 2 due to current $I_1$ in coil 1, then $M_{21} = \frac{\Phi_{B2}}{I_1}$. Similarly, $M_{12} = \frac{\Phi_{B1}}{I_2}$. It can be shown that $M_{12} = M_{21} = M$.
    • Induced EMF: $\mathcal{E}_2 = -M \frac{dI_1}{dt}$.
    • S.I. unit: Henry (H).
  • AC Generator: Works on the principle of electromagnetic induction. Mechanical energy (rotating a coil in a magnetic field) is converted into electrical energy by continuously changing the magnetic flux linked with the coil, thereby inducing an alternating EMF and current.

2. Key Theorems / Core Formulas

Magnetic Flux:

$$ \Phi_B = \int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} $$

For uniform magnetic field and planar area: $ \Phi_B = BA \cos\theta $

Faraday's Law of EMI (Magnitude):

$$ \mathcal{E} = \left| -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} \right| $$

Motional EMF:

For a conductor of length $l$ moving with velocity $v$ perpendicular to magnetic field $B$: $$ \mathcal{E} = Blv $$

More generally: $ \mathcal{E} = (\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot \vec{l} $

Self-Inductance (L):

Definition: $ L = \frac{\Phi_B}{I} $

Induced EMF: $ \mathcal{E} = -L \frac{dI}{dt} $

Energy Stored in Inductor: $ U = \frac{1}{2}LI^2 $

Self-inductance of a long solenoid: $ L = \mu_r \mu_0 \frac{N^2 A}{l} $

Mutual Inductance (M):

Definition: $ M_{21} = \frac{\Phi_{B2}}{I_1} $

Induced EMF: $ \mathcal{E}_2 = -M \frac{dI_1}{dt} $

Mutual inductance of two long coaxial solenoids: $ M = \mu_r \mu_0 \frac{N_1 N_2 A}{l} $

Energy Density of Magnetic Field:

$$ u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} $$

3. Solved High-Yield CBSE Board PYQs

Question 1:

A circular coil of radius 10 cm and 200 turns is rotated about its vertical diameter with an angular speed of 50 rad/s in a uniform horizontal magnetic field of 3.0 × 10-2 T. Calculate the maximum and average EMF induced in the coil.

Solution:

Given: $N = 200$, $r = 10 \text{ cm} = 0.1 \text{ m}$, $\omega = 50 \text{ rad/s}$, $B = 3.0 \times 10^{-2} \text{ T}$.

Area of the coil, $A = \pi r^2 = \pi (0.1)^2 = 0.01\pi \text{ m}^2$.

Magnetic flux linked with the coil at any time $t$ is $\Phi_B = NBA \cos(\omega t)$.

Induced EMF, $\mathcal{E} = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -N \frac{d}{dt}(NBA \cos(\omega t))$

$\mathcal{E} = -N(NBA)(-\sin(\omega t))\omega = NBA\omega \sin(\omega t)$.

  1. Step 1: Calculate the Maximum Induced EMF ($\mathcal{E}_{max}$).
    Maximum EMF occurs when $\sin(\omega t) = 1$.
    $\mathcal{E}_{max} = NBA\omega$
    $\mathcal{E}_{max} = 200 \times (3.0 \times 10^{-2}) \times (0.01\pi) \times 50$ (1 Mark for formula & substitution)
    $\mathcal{E}_{max} = 200 \times 3 \times 10^{-2} \times 0.01 \times 3.14 \times 50$
    $\mathcal{E}_{max} = 9.42 \text{ V}$ (1 Mark for correct calculation)
  2. Step 2: Calculate the Average Induced EMF ($\mathcal{E}_{avg}$).
    For a complete cycle, the induced EMF is sinusoidal.
    The average value of a sinusoidal function over a complete cycle is zero.
    Therefore, the average induced EMF over a full cycle is $\mathcal{E}_{avg} = 0$. (1 Mark for correct reasoning)

Final Answer: Maximum induced EMF = 9.42 V, Average induced EMF = 0 V.

Question 2:

A metallic rod of length $L$ is rotated with an angular speed $\omega$ about an axis passing through one end and perpendicular to the rod. A uniform magnetic field $B$ parallel to the axis is present everywhere. Derive the expression for the EMF induced across the ends of the rod.

Solution:

Consider a small element $dr$ of the rod at a distance $r$ from the pivot. This element is moving with a linear velocity $v = r\omega$ perpendicular to the magnetic field $B$.

  1. Step 1: EMF induced in the small element.
    The motional EMF induced across this small element $dr$ is $d\mathcal{E} = Bv dr$. (1 Mark)
    Substituting $v = r\omega$, we get $d\mathcal{E} = B(r\omega) dr$. (1 Mark)
  2. Step 2: Integrate to find total EMF.
    To find the total EMF induced across the ends of the rod, we integrate $d\mathcal{E}$ from $r=0$ to $r=L$.
    $\mathcal{E} = \int_{0}^{L} B\omega r dr$ (1 Mark)
    $\mathcal{E} = B\omega \int_{0}^{L} r dr$
    $\mathcal{E} = B\omega \left[ \frac{r^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{L}$
    $\mathcal{E} = B\omega \left( \frac{L^2}{2} - 0 \right)$
    $\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{2} B\omega L^2$ (1 Mark for final expression)

Final Answer: The induced EMF across the ends of the rod is $\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{2} B\omega L^2$.

Question 3:

Define mutual inductance between two coils. On what factors does the mutual inductance of two coils depend?

Solution:

  1. Step 1: Definition of Mutual Inductance.
    Mutual inductance (M) between two coils is defined as the ratio of the magnetic flux linked with one coil ($\Phi_2$) due to a current flowing in the other coil ($I_1$) to the current itself. (1 Mark)
    Alternatively, it is defined as the EMF induced in one coil when the rate of change of current in the other coil is unity. (1 Mark)
    Mathematically, $\Phi_2 = M I_1$ or $\mathcal{E}_2 = -M \frac{dI_1}{dt}$.
  2. Step 2: Factors affecting Mutual Inductance. (2 Marks for any 4 factors)
    • Geometry of the coils: Size, shape, and number of turns of the coils.
    • Relative orientation of the coils: How the coils are positioned with respect to each other (e.g., coaxial, perpendicular).
    • Distance between the coils: As the distance increases, mutual inductance generally decreases.
    • Nature of the core material: The presence of a magnetic material (like soft iron) between the coils significantly increases mutual inductance due to its high permeability ($\mu_r$).
    • Coupling factor: How effectively the magnetic flux produced by one coil links with the other coil.

Final Answer: Definition provided, and factors listed.

4. Quick Revision Summary Box

  • Electromagnetic Induction (EMI): Production of induced EMF/current by changing magnetic flux.
  • Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B$): $BA \cos\theta$. Unit: Weber (Wb).
  • Faraday's Laws: Induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux ($\mathcal{E} = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$).
  • Lenz's Law: Direction of induced current opposes the cause (Conservation of Energy).
  • Motional EMF: EMF induced in a moving conductor in a magnetic field ($\mathcal{E} = Blv$ for perpendicular orientation).
  • Eddy Currents: Induced currents in bulk conductors, cause heating and damping.
  • Self-Inductance (L): EMF induced in a coil due to current change in *itself*. ($\mathcal{E} = -L \frac{dI}{dt}$, $U = \frac{1}{2}LI^2$). Unit: Henry (H).
  • Mutual Inductance (M): EMF induced in one coil due to current change in a *neighbouring* coil. ($\mathcal{E}_2 = -M \frac{dI_1}{dt}$). Unit: Henry (H).
  • AC Generator: Converts mechanical energy to electrical energy using EMI.
