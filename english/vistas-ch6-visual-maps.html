Memories of Childhood — Two Voices Against Social Prejudice

Part I: The Cutting of My Long Hair

  • Author: Zitkala-Sa (Gertrude Simmons Bonnin)
  • Setting: Carlisle Indian School (Missionary Boarding School, USA)
  • Oppression: Racial prejudice & forced Western assimilation
  • Trigger: Forced cutting of long Sioux braids
  • Response: Immediate physical defiance (hides, kicks, scratches)

Part II: We Too Are Human Beings

  • Author: Bama (Faustina Mary Fatima Rani), from Karukku
  • Setting: Rural village in Tamil Nadu, India
  • Oppression: Caste-based untouchability & feudal hierarchy
  • Trigger: Village elder carrying vadai packet by its string
  • Response: Intellectual empowerment (studies with frenzy, tops class)

Key Symbols & Literary Devices

  • Long Hair: Sioux cultural pride, dignity, and freedom
  • Eating by Formula: Mechanical, soulless institutional control
  • String-Held Packet: Invisible yet rigid wall of untouchability
  • Muzzled Cattle: Silenced laborers denied fruits of their work
  • Metaphors: "Wooden puppet" & "animals driven by a herder"

Core Themes & Board Takeaways

  • Universal Prejudice: Racism and casteism both dehumanize the marginalized
  • Loss of Innocence: Social cruelty invades childhood far too early
  • Courage & Dignity: Refusal to internalize inferiority
  • Education as Weapon: Knowledge and excellence dismantle hierarchy

Chronological Flowchart — Part I (Zitkala-Sa)

Step-by-step sequence of events at the Carlisle Indian School

Part I: The Cutting of My Long Hair
Step 01 · Arrival & Alienation

Bitter Cold First Morning

  • Loud metallic breakfast bell jars Zitkala-Sa's ears; stiff shoes clatter on bare floors.
  • Stripped of her shawl, she feels exposed in tight Western clothes and unfamiliar tongue.
Step 02 · Dining Hall Humiliation

"Eating by Formula"

  • Three-bell drill confuses her: sits on 1st bell, rises on 2nd, breaks down crying on 3rd.
  • Intimidated by the sharp, cold stare of the paleface woman.
Step 03 · Warning & Resolve

Judewin's News vs. Zitkala-Sa's Defiance

  • Judewin overhears the plan to cut their hair and advises submission.
  • Remembering Sioux custom (short hair = mourners/cowards), Zitkala-Sa resolves to struggle first.
Step 04 · Resistance & Capture

Hiding Under the Bed

  • Creeps upstairs into a dim room with green curtains and hides under the farthest bed.
  • Found by searchers; kicks and scratches wildly while dragged downstairs and tied to a chair.
Step 05 · Climax & Aftermath

Cold Blades & Broken Spirit

  • Scissors gnaw off her thick braids; she loses her spirit and moans for her mother.
  • Feels reduced to a "wooden puppet" and "one of many little animals driven by a herder."

Chronological Flowchart — Part II (Bama)

Step-by-step sequence from childhood innocence to academic triumph

Part II: We Too Are Human Beings
Step 01 · Innocent Childhood

The 30-to-60 Minute Walk Home

  • Third-grade Bama stretches a 10-minute walk watching bazaar novelties.
  • Monkey, snake charmer, 3-day cyclist, temple Pongal, snacks, street plays, and speeches.
Step 02 · The Threshing Floor

The Elder & The String-Held Parcel

  • Upper-caste landlord sits on a ledge watching Dalit workers drive muzzled cattle.
  • Respected village elder crouches, bows low, and holds a vadai packet strictly by its string.
Step 03 · Irony & Revelation

Laughter Shattered by Annan's Truth

  • Bama laughs heartily and enacts the scene at home for her elder brother Annan.
  • Annan explains the bitter truth of caste "pollution"—Bama's laughter turns into shock and fury.
Step 04 · Mentorship

Annan's Blueprint for Dignity

  • Annan recalls being asked his street name to subtly identify his caste.
  • Advises Bama: study with care and stay ahead in lessons to throw away social indignities.
Step 05 · Resolution

Empowerment Through Excellence

  • Bama channels her anger into intense study ("with a frenzy").
  • Stands first in her class, commanding respect and winning many friends through merit.
