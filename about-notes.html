why this page exists

A note that helps is a note that respects your time.

Class 12 Science is the year everything gets bigger. Chapters go from 20 pages to 60. Concepts stop being standalone and start referring back to chapters you did two months ago. Board exams don't just test what you know — they test how well you can retrieve it under pressure.

Most students don't need more information. They already have the NCERT textbook, a coaching module, a guide book, and probably three YouTube playlists open. What they're missing is a single, quiet place to check whether they actually understood something.

"The problem is rarely a lack of material. It's a lack of order."

Shorveer Notes exists to be that place. It isn't trying to replace your teacher, your textbook, or your coaching class. It's the resource you open the night before a test when you need the chapter compressed into something readable in twenty minutes.

how a note gets made

Five steps, every chapter, every time.

  1. 01

    Start with the official syllabus

    Every chapter begins by reading the CBSE curriculum document and the corresponding NCERT chapter. This sets the boundary — what's in scope, what's been removed, what's marked as "for understanding only" versus what's explicitly examinable.

  2. 02

    Break the chapter into readable units

    A 40-page chapter is unusable as a study unit. It gets split into sections small enough to read in one sitting — typically 5 to 8 per chapter. Each section has one clear job: define, explain, derive, or apply. Nothing mixes.

  3. 03

    Cross-check facts and figures

    Definitions, formulas, constants, reaction conditions, dates and names are checked against the NCERT textbook and at least one second reliable reference. If a value has changed between editions, the current one is used and the change is noted.

  4. 04

    Structure it for revision, not reading

    Key formulas, definitions and diagrams are pulled out of the body text so they're findable. Common question patterns from previous years are flagged. Each chapter ends with a short list of "if you only remember three things" points.

  5. 05

    Read it as a student would

    The final pass is a plain read-through on a phone. If a sentence needs to be re-read twice to make sense, it gets rewritten. If a paragraph could be a bullet, it becomes one. If a section is only there because it's in the textbook, it gets cut.

what we lean on

Sources, and how we use them.

NCERT textbook

The primary reference for everything on this site. Chapter order, terminology and scope follow NCERT, so the notes line up with what you're already reading in class.

CBSE curriculum documents

The official syllabus for each subject tells us what's examinable this year, what's been dropped, and how marks are distributed across units. This shapes the emphasis in every chapter.

Previous-year question papers

Board papers from the last several years are read to identify how often concepts are tested, what format they appear in, and which topics students consistently lose marks on.

Your feedback

If a chapter has a mistake, an unclear explanation, or is missing something your teacher emphasised, tell us. Corrections from actual students are the fastest way a note improves.

what we aim for

Six things every note should do.

Respect your time

If a paragraph doesn't help you understand, remember or apply something, it gets cut.

Be checkable

Every claim, formula and definition should be verifiable against NCERT or the syllabus.

Separate what matters

Not every paragraph is equally important. The notes make the hierarchy visible.

Stay consistent

The same structure applies across Physics, Chemistry, Maths and English, so you don't relearn navigation every time.

Improve, don't stagnate

Notes get revisited. If a chapter reads badly or a diagram is wrong, it gets fixed rather than left.

Stay student-first

No sign-ups, no paywalls, no pop-ups. The whole point is that you can open a chapter and read it.
