Structure & nomenclature of aldehydes and ketones

Definition: Aldehydes contain a carbonyl group ($>C=O$) bonded to at least one hydrogen atom, represented as –$CHO$. Ketones have a carbonyl group flanked by two carbon atoms, represented as –$CO-$.

General formulas (saturated):

  • Aldehyde: $C_{n}H_{2n+1}CHO$ (or $C_{n}H_{2n+2}O$)
  • Ketone: $C_{n}H_{2n}O$

Key IUPAC naming rules:

  1. Identify the longest carbon chain containing the carbonyl carbon.
  2. Number the chain so that the carbonyl carbon gets the lowest possible locant.
  3. For aldehydes, replace the –$e$ of the parent alkane with –$al$; for ketones, replace –$e$ with –$one$.
  4. If substituents are present, name them in alphabetical order with their locants.
  5. When the carbonyl is at the end of the chain, the aldehyde locant is omitted (e.g., $propanal$).

Summary table:

Functional groupSuffixExample
Aldehyde–$al$CH$_3$CH$_2$CHO → $propanal$
Ketone–$one$CH$_3$COCH$_3$ → $propanone$ (acetone)

Solved example:

Give the IUPAC name of the following structure:
CH$_3$–CH$_2$–CH(CH$_3$)–CHO

Solution:

  1. Longest chain containing –$CHO$ has 4 carbons → base name $butanal$.
  2. Number from the carbonyl end: carbonyl carbon is C‑1.
  3. There is a methyl substituent on C‑3.
  4. Combine: $3$‑$methyl$ $butanal$.

Important SI units: Boiling point, melting point, density etc. are expressed in $^circ C$, $g,cm^{-3}$ respectively.

Methods of preparation of aldehydes and ketones

Definitions

  • Aldehyde: $RCHO$ (carbonyl carbon attached to at least one hydrogen).
  • Ketone: $RCOR'$ (carbonyl carbon attached to two alkyl/aryl groups).

Key Methods of Preparation

MethodReagents / ConditionsProduct
Oxidation of primary alcohol$RCH_2OH$ + $[O]$ (e.g., $PCC$, $KMnO_4$ (cold))$RCHO$
Ozonolysis of alkenes$RCH=CH_2$ + $O_3$ → $H_2O_2$ work‑up$RCHO$ + $R'CHO$
Hydration of terminal alkyne$RC\equiv CH$ + $H_2O$ (HgSO$_4$, $H_2SO_4$)$RCHO$
Reduction of acid derivatives$RCOCl$, $RCOOR'$, $RCOOH$, $RCONH_2$ + $LiAlH_4$ (or $NaBH_4$ for acid chloride)$RCHO$ (or $RCOR'$ for ketone)
From nitriles$RCN$ + $H_2$ (Raney Ni) or $LiAlH_4$$RCH_2NH_2$ → $RCHO$ (via hydrolysis)
From carboxylic acids$RCOOH$ + $P_2O_5$ (dry distillation)$RCHO$
Grignard addition to CO$R MgX$ + $CO$ + $H_2O$$RCHO$

Typical conditions: temperature $25-80^\circ C$, pressure $1\ atm$, solvents $CH_2Cl_2$, $EtOH$; yields are reported in %.

Representative Example (Oxidation of Ethanol)

Convert ethanol ($CH_3CH_2OH$) to acetaldehyde ($CH_3CHO$) using pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC).

StepReaction
1$CH_3CH_2OH$ + $PCC$ → $CH_3CHO$ + $Cr^{3+}$ + $Cl^-$
2Work‑up with $H_2O$ gives pure $CH_3CHO$ (boiling point $20.2^\circ C$).

Key points: Use a mild oxidising agent to stop at aldehyde; avoid over‑oxidation to acetic acid.

Physical & chemical properties of aldehydes

Physical & Chemical Properties of Aldehydes (RCHO)

Aldehydes contain the carbonyl group ($\mathrm{C=O}$) attached to at least one hydrogen atom. They are polar, have characteristic pungent odours and are generally soluble in water up to $\sim$5 % (by mass) due to hydrogen‑bonding.

  • Boiling point: Higher than the corresponding alcohol because of dipole‑dipole interactions, but lower than the analogous ketone (no extra alkyl group).
  • Density: $1.0\text{–}1.2\,\text{g cm}^{-3}$ (close to water).
  • Reactivity: The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic; aldehydes undergo nucleophilic addition, oxidation to carboxylic acids and reduction to primary alcohols.
PropertyTrend in Aldehydes
Boiling point (°C)Increases with molecular weight; $\mathrm{CH_2O}$ = -19, $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O}$ ≈ 120
Solubility in waterDecreases with chain length; $\mathrm{CH_2O}$ fully miscible, $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O}$ ≈ 5 %
Acidity of $α$‑HpK_a ≈ 17 (more acidic than alkanes)

Key Chemical Reactions

  • Oxidation: $\mathrm{RCHO + [O] \rightarrow RCOOH}$ (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_2O + 2[O] \rightarrow HCOOH}$). Rate law often first order: $\text{Rate}=k[\text{aldehyde}]$.
  • Reduction: $\mathrm{RCHO + 2[H] \rightarrow RCH_2OH}$ (e.g., NaBH$_4$).
  • Nucleophilic addition (e.g., formation of hemiacetal with $\mathrm{ROH}$).

SI Units

  • Temperature – kelvin (K) or degree Celsius (°C).
  • Pressure – pascal (Pa).
  • Amount – mole (mol).

Solved Example

Calculate the moles of formaldehyde ($\mathrm{CH_2O}$) formed when $5.0\,\text{g}$ of methanol is oxidised completely.

Step 1: Molar mass of $\mathrm{CH_2O}$ = $12.01 + 2(1.008) + 16.00 = 30.03\,\text{g mol}^{-1}$.

Step 2: $n = \frac{m}{M} = \frac{5.0\,\text{g}}{30.03\,\text{g mol}^{-1}} = 1.66\times10^{-1}\,\text{mol}$.

Thus, $0.166\,\text{mol}$ of formaldehyde is produced.

Physical & chemical properties of ketones

Definition: Ketones are organic compounds containing a carbonyl group ($C=O$) bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups.

Physical properties

  • Polarity: dipole moment $\mu$ \approx $2.9\,\text{D}$ for acetone; higher than alkanes.
  • Boiling points: follow $\text{BP} \uparrow$ with molecular weight; acetone $56\,^{\circ}\text{C}$, 2‑butanone $80\,^{\circ}\text{C}$.
  • Solubility: miscible with water up to $\approx 100\,\%$ for acetone due to H‑bond acceptor ability.

Key formula – Heat of vapourisation

Compound$\Delta H_{vap}^\circ$ (kJ·mol$^{-1}$)
Acetone$31.3$
2‑Butanone$38.0$

Chemical properties

  • Nucleophilic addition is the principal reaction; carbonyl carbon is electrophilic.
  • Reaction with $\text{NaBH}_4$ gives secondary alcohols: $\ce{RCOR'} + \text{NaBH4} \rightarrow \ce{RCH(OH)R'}$.
  • Oxidation: secondary ketones are resistant; α‑hydrogen can be removed by $\ce{LDA}$ to form enolates.
  • Halogenation in acidic medium yields α‑halo ketones: $\ce{RCOR' + X2 \xrightarrow{H+} RCHXCO R'}$.

Solved example

Oxidise $\pu{10.0\,g}$ of 2‑propanol ($\text{M}=60.1\,\text{g·mol}^{-1}$) with $\ce{NaOCl}$ to acetone. Theoretical yield of acetone = $\pu{10.0\,g} \times \frac{58.1}{60.1}=\pu{9.66\,g}$. If $\pu{8.50\,g}$ acetone is isolated, % yield = $\frac{8.50}{9.66}\times100 = 88\%$.

Important reactions of aldehydes & ketones (nucleophilic addition, oxidation, reduction, haloform)

Definitions

  • Aldehyde: Organic compound containing the carbonyl group attached to at least one hydrogen atom, general formula $RCHO$.
  • Ketone: Carbonyl group flanked by two carbon atoms, general formula $RCOR'$.

Nucleophilic Addition

  • Mechanism: $C=O$ is electrophilic; nucleophile $Nu^-$ attacks carbon, forming alkoxide intermediate $RCH(O^-)Nu$ which is protonated to give $RCH(OH)Nu$.
  • Typical reagents: $NaBH_4$, $LiAlH_4$, $H_2$, $HCl$ (for addition of $HCl$).

Oxidation

  • Aldehydes $\rightarrow$ carboxylic acids; ketones are generally resistant.
  • Common oxidising agents: $KMnO_4$, $K_2Cr_2O_7$ in $H_2SO_4$, $Na_2Cr_2O_7$.

Reduction

  • Aldehydes/ketones $\rightarrow$ primary/secondary alcohols.
  • Reagents: $NaBH_4$ (mild), $LiAlH_4$ (strong).

Haloform Reaction

  • Applicable to methyl ketones $RC(O)CH_3$ and ethanol.
  • Reagents: $X_2$ (Cl$_2$, Br$_2$) + $NaOH$.
  • Overall: $RC(O)CH_3 + 3X_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow RCOONa + CHX_3 + 3NaX + 3H_2O$.
ReactionReagentProduct
Oxidation of $RCHO$$KMnO_4$ (acidic)$RCOOH$
Reduction of $RCOR'$$NaBH_4$$RCH(OH)R'$

Solved Example

Reduce $CH_3CHO$ (acetaldehyde) to ethanol using $NaBH_4$. 5 mmol of $NaBH_4$ is taken. Write the balanced equation and calculate moles of ethanol formed.

Balanced: $CH_3CHO + NaBH_4 + 3H_2O \rightarrow CH_3CH_2OH + NaBO_2 + 3H_2$.

One mole of $NaBH_4$ reduces one mole of aldehyde; thus 5 mmol $NaBH_4$ gives 5 mmol $CH_3CH_2OH$ (0.005 mol, $0.005 \times 46 = 0.23$ g).

Structure & nomenclature of carboxylic acids

Definition: A carboxylic acid is an organic compound containing the functional group $\text{-COOH}$ (carboxyl group) attached to a carbon skeleton.

General formula: For a saturated monocarboxylic acid, $C_{n}H_{2n+1}COOH$ or equivalently $C_{n+1}H_{2n+2}O_{2}$ (where $n\ge 1$). The molar mass $M$ is obtained by $M=\sum M_{i}$ (sum of atomic masses in g·mol$^{-1}$).

IUPAC nomenclature rules

  • Identify the longest chain containing the $\text{-COOH}$ group; it must be numbered so that the carbon of $\text{-COOH}$ gets the lowest possible locant (always 1).
  • Replace the alkane suffix “‑e” with “‑oic acid”.
  • Number substituents on the chain and name them as prefixes (e.g., 2‑methyl, 3‑chloro).
  • If the acid has more than one $\text{-COOH}$, use “‑dioic acid”, “‑trioic acid”, etc., with appropriate locants.

Common (trivial) names

IUPAC nameCommon name
methanoic acidformic acid
ethanoic acidacetic acid
propanoic acidpropionic acid

Physical properties

  • Boiling points increase with molecular weight due to extensive hydrogen‑bonding.
  • Acids are strong hydrogen‑bond donors; $\text{-COOH}$ has a dipole moment of about $1.4\,\text{D}$.

Solved example

Calculate the percentage mass of carbon in acetic acid ($CH_{3}COOH$).

Molar mass $M_{CH_{3}COOH}=2(12.01)+4(1.008)+2(16.00)=60.05\,\text{g·mol}^{-1}$.

Mass of carbon $=2\times12.01=24.02\,\text{g}$.

\[\%\,C = \frac{24.02}{60.05}\times100 = 40.0\%\]

Thus, acetic acid contains $40.0\%$ carbon by mass.

Methods of preparation & physical properties of carboxylic acids

Definition: Carboxylic acids are organic compounds containing the –$COOH$ functional group.

Physical properties (typical trends):

PropertyTrend with chain length
Boiling pointIncreases due to stronger H‑bonding and Van‑der‑Waals forces
Solubility in waterDecreases; short‑chain acids (\le 4 C) are miscible

Acidic nature: $K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}$, $pK_a = -\log K_a$. Typical $pK_a$ values: formic $\approx 3.75$, acetic $\approx 4.76$.

Methods of preparation:

  • Oxidation of primary alcohols/aldehydes (e.g., $CH_3CH_2OH \xrightarrow{[O]} CH_3COOH$).
  • Hydrolysis of nitriles: $RCN + 2H_2O \xrightarrow{H^+} RCOOH + NH_3$.
  • Carboxylation of organometallic reagents: $RMgX + CO_2 \xrightarrow{} RCOOMgX \xrightarrow{H^+} RCOOH$.
  • From acid chlorides: $RCOCl + H_2O \rightarrow RCOOH + HCl$.

SI units: Temperature $^\circ C$, pressure $atm$, concentration $mol\,L^{-1}$.

Solved example: Prepare acetic acid by oxidation of ethanol.

Reaction: $CH_3CH_2OH + \frac{1}{2}O_2 \rightarrow CH_3COOH + H_2O$.

Given 46 g ethanol (0.99 mol) and excess $O_2$, theoretical yield of $CH_3COOH$ = 0.99 mol $\times$ 60.05 g·mol$^{-1}$ = 59.4 g. If 52 g acid is obtained, % yield = $\frac{52}{59.4}\times100 = 87.5\%$.

Important reactions of carboxylic acids and their derivatives (esterification, reduction, decarboxylation)

Definitions

  • Esterification: Acid‑catalysed equilibrium between a carboxylic acid ($RCOOH$) and an alcohol ($R'OH$) forming an ester ($RCOOR'$) and water.
  • Reduction: Conversion of a carboxylic acid or its derivative to the corresponding alcohol using strong reducing agents.
  • Decarboxylation: Thermal removal of $CO_2$ from a carboxylic acid, yielding a hydrocarbon.

Key Equations & Conditions

ReactionReagentsConditions
$RCOOH + R'OH \\rightleftharpoons RCOOR' + H_2O$Conc. $H_2SO_4$ (catalyst)$60-80\\,^{\\circ}C$, removal of $H_2O$ (Dean‑Stark)
$RCOOH \\xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} RCH_2OH$LiAlH$_4$, dry ether$0\\,^{\\circ}C$ to $25\\,^{\\circ}C$, inert atmosphere
$RCOOH \\xrightarrow{\\Delta} R-H + CO_2$Heat$>200\\,^{\\circ}C$, often with $CuO$ or $NaOH$ as catalyst

Mechanistic Insight

  • Protonation of the carbonyl oxygen increases electrophilicity, allowing nucleophilic attack by the alcohol.
  • In reduction, LiAlH$_4$ donates hydride to the carbonyl carbon; subsequent work‑up gives the primary alcohol.
  • Decarboxylation proceeds via a six‑membered cyclic transition state when a β‑keto acid is involved.

Solved Example

Esterify acetic acid with ethanol.

StepDetails
1Mix $CH_3COOH$ (0.1 mol) and $C_2H_5OH$ (0.1 mol) with 5 mL conc. $H_2SO_4$.
2Heat at $70\\,^{\\circ}C$ for 2 h; water removed by Dean‑Stark apparatus.
3Cool, neutralise with NaHCO$_3$, extract ester with diethyl ether.
4Yield $CH_3COOC_2H_5$ = 0.085 mol (85 %).

All reactions are reversible; shifting equilibrium by removing water or using excess alcohol drives ester formation.
