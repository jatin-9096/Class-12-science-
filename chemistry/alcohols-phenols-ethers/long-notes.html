Nomenclature of Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

Definition: Alcohols, phenols and ethers are organic compounds containing –OH or –O– groups attached to saturated carbon atoms.

  • Alcohols: Hydroxyl group ($-OH$) bonded to a saturated carbon.
  • Phenols: Hydroxyl group directly attached to an aromatic ring.
  • Ethers: Two alkyl/aryl groups linked by an oxygen atom ($R-O-R'$).

IUPAC Naming Rules – Alcohols

StepRule
1Identify the longest carbon chain containing the –OH group; this chain is the parent hydrocarbon.
2Number the chain so that the carbon bearing –OH gets the lowest possible locant.
3Replace the –OH suffix with “‑$\\text{ol}$”. If more than one –OH, use “‑$\\text{diol}$”, “‑$\\text{triol}$”, etc., with appropriate locants.
4Insert prefixes for substituents and give their locants.

Phenols

  • Parent name is the aromatic ring (benzene → “phenol”).
  • If substituents are present, use “‑$\\text{ol}$” as a suffix and number the ring to give the –OH the lowest locant.

Ethers

  • Two naming methods: (i) “alkoxy‑alkane” (e.g., $\\text{methoxypropane}$) where the shorter alkyl is the alkoxy part; (ii) “symmetrical ethers” named as “dialkyl ether” (e.g., $\\text{diethyl ether}$).
  • When both sides are different, the alkoxy name is preferred.

Solved Example

Name the compound: $\\text{CH}_3\\text{CH}_2\\text{CH}(\\text{OH})\\text{CH}_2\\text{CH}_3$.

Solution: Longest chain = 5 C → “pentane”. Number from the end nearest the –OH → locant 3. Replace –e with –$\\text{ol}$ → “3‑pentanol”.

Methods of Preparation of Alcohols (e.g., hydration of alkenes, reduction of carbonyls)

Definition: Alcohols are organic compounds containing a hydroxyl group ($-\!OH$) attached to a saturated carbon atom.

Key Methods:

  • Acid‑catalysed hydration of alkenes
  • Reduction of aldehydes and ketones

Acid‑catalysed hydration

General equation: $C_nH_{2n}+H_2O\xrightarrow{H_2SO_4,\,\Delta}C_nH_{2n+1}OH$

Conditions: 60–80 $^\circ$C, 1–2 atm, conc. $H_2SO_4$ (≈98 %).

Reduction of carbonyls

For aldehydes: $RCHO+H_2\xrightarrow{Ni,\,\Delta}RCH_2OH$

For ketones: $RCOR'+H_2\xrightarrow{Ni,\,\Delta}RCH_2R'$

Using metal hydrides: $RCHO+NaBH_4\rightarrow RCH_2OH$ (room temp, $MeOH$).

ReagentProduct
$NaBH_4$Primary alcohol
$LiAlH_4$Secondary/tertiary alcohol

Solved Example

Hydrate 10 g ethene ($C_2H_4$) to ethanol. Molar masses: $M_{C_2H_4}=28.05\,$g·mol$^{-1}$, $M_{C_2H_5OH}=46.07\,$g·mol$^{-1}$. Moles of ethene $n=\frac{10}{28.05}=0.357\,$mol. Assuming 100 % yield, $n_{ethanol}=0.357\,$mol. Mass of ethanol $m=n\times M=0.357\times46.07=16.45\,$g.

Key points: the reaction follows Markovnikov’s rule, the acid catalyst is regenerated, and reduction must be controlled to avoid over‑reduction.

Methods of Preparation of Phenols (e.g., hydrolysis of aryl halides, cumene process)

Definition: Phenols are aromatic compounds containing a hydroxyl group directly attached to an sp$^2$ hybridised carbon of a benzene ring (Ar–OH). They exhibit acidic behaviour ($pK_a\approx10$) and are important industrial intermediates.

1. Hydrolysis of aryl halides (high‑temperature alkaline hydrolysis)

  • General reaction: $\text{Ar–X} + \text{NaOH}_{(aq)} \xrightarrow{\text{300–350\,°C}} \text{Ar–OH} + \text{NaX}$ where X = Cl, Br, I.
  • Requires a strong base and a sealed tube to achieve temperatures $\ge300\,\text{°C}$ and pressures $\approx10\,\text{atm}$.

2. Cumene (isopropylbenzene) process

  • Step 1 – Oxidation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{70–80\,°C}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OOH}$ (cumene hydroperoxide).
  • Step 2 – Acidic cleavage: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{OOH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CO}$ (acetone).

Key conditions

StepTemperature (°C)Pressure (atm)
Alkali hydrolysis300–350~10
Cumene oxidation70–801 (atmospheric)

Solved Example (Cumene process)

Calculate the mass of phenol obtained from $10\,\text{g}$ of cumene ($M_{\text{cumene}}=120.19\,\text{g\,mol}^{-1}$) assuming 100 % conversion.

\[\text{Moles of cumene}=\frac{10}{120.19}=0.0832\,\text{mol}\]

One mole of cumene yields one mole of phenol ($M_{\text{phenol}}=94.11\,\text{g\,mol}^{-1}$).

\[\text{Mass of phenol}=0.0832\times94.11=7.83\,\text{g}\]

Thus, $10\,\text{g}$ cumene can theoretically give $7.8\,\text{g}$ phenol.

Methods of Preparation of Ethers (e.g., Williamson ether synthesis, dehydration of alcohols)

Definition: Ethers are compounds having an alkoxy group ($-OR$) bonded to another carbon atom ($R'-O-R$). General formula: $R-O-R'$ where $R$ and $R'$ may be alkyl or aryl.

1. Williamson Ether Synthesis

  • Reaction: $R-O^-\;(\text{alkoxide}) + R'X \rightarrow R-O-R' + X^-$
  • Alkoxide is prepared by treating alcohol with Na or K metal: $R-OH + 2\,\text{Na} \rightarrow R-O^-Na^+ + NaH$
  • Best for primary alkyl halides; secondary/tertiary give elimination.
  • Typical conditions: anhydrous ether, reflux, inert atmosphere.
ReagentCondition
$R-OH$ + Na/Kdry $\\text{THF}$, $0-25^\circ\text{C}$
$R'X$ (primary)reflux $\\text{ether}$, $50-80^\circ\text{C}$

2. Acid‑catalyzed Dehydration of Alcohols

  • Reaction: $2\,R-OH \xrightarrow[\\text{conc. }H_2SO_4]{\\Delta} R-O-R + H_2O$
  • Effective for symmetrical ethers (e.g., di‑ethyl ether) and phenols.
  • Requires strong acid, temperature $140-180^\circ\text{C}$; higher temperature leads to alkenes.

Solved Example

Prepare ethyl phenyl ether using Williamson method.

  1. Generate phenoxide: $C_6H_5OH + Na \rightarrow C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + \frac{1}{2}H_2$
  2. React with ethyl bromide: $C_6H_5O^-Na^+ + C_2H_5Br \rightarrow C_6H_5-O-C_2H_5 + NaBr$
  3. Yield $\approx 78\%$; product confirmed by $\\text{m.p.}=-$ (liquid) and IR $\\nu_{C-O}\approx 1100\\,\\text{cm}^{-1}$.

Key Points for Exams

  • Use primary halides for Williamson; avoid steric hindrance.
  • Dehydration gives symmetrical ethers; control temperature to prevent side reactions.
  • Write balanced equations with correct stoichiometry and state symbols.

Physical and Chemical Properties of Alcohols (including $C_nH_{2n+2}O$, boiling point trends, oxidation)

Definition: Alcohols are organic compounds containing a hydroxyl group (–OH) attached to a saturated carbon atom. General formula $C_nH_{2n+2}O$ (for acyclic, non‑cyclic primary, secondary, tertiary).

Physical properties

  • Boiling point (BP) is higher than corresponding alkanes due to H‑bonding.
  • BP trend: $BP_{primary} > BP_{secondary} > BP_{tertiary}$ because steric hindrance reduces H‑bonding ability.
  • Density: $1.0\text{–}1.1\ \text{g cm}^{-3}$ (higher than water for most low‑molecular alcohols).
AlcoholStructureBP (°C)
Methanol$CH_3OH$$64.7$
Ethanol$CH_3CH_2OH$$78.4$
2‑Propanol$CH_3CHOHCH_3$$82.5$
tert‑Butanol$(CH_3)_3COH$$82.2$

Chemical properties (oxidation)

  • Primary alcohols → aldehydes → carboxylic acids (or further to CO2).
  • Secondary alcohols → ketones.
  • Tertiary alcohols resist oxidation under mild conditions.

Key oxidation equations

  • Primary: $RCH_2OH \xrightarrow{[O]} RCHO$ (mild) ; $RCHO \xrightarrow{[O]} RCOOH$ (strong).
  • Secondary: $R_2CHOH \xrightarrow{[O]} R_2C=O$.

Example

Oxidation of ethanol with acidic $KMnO_4$:

$$CH_3CH_2OH + 2[O] \rightarrow CH_3COOH + H_2O$$

Steps: ethanol → acetaldehyde (intermediate) → acetic acid. The reaction is carried out at $80\text{–}100^\circ\text{C}$, and the amount of $KMnO_4$ required is calculated using $n_{KMnO_4}=2n_{ethanol}$.

Physical and Chemical Properties of Phenols (including $C_6H_5OH$, acidity, electrophilic substitution)

Phenol (C6H5OH) is a weakly acidic aromatic compound containing a hydroxyl group directly attached to a benzene ring.

Acidity

  • Phenol ionises in water: $C_6H_5OH \rightleftharpoons C_6H_5O^- + H^+$.
  • Acid dissociation constant: $K_a = \frac{[C_6H_5O^-][H^+]}{[C_6H_5OH]}$ (dimensionless).
  • pKa = $-\log_{10}K_a$; for phenol $pK_a \approx 10$.
  • Acidity is enhanced by electron‑withdrawing groups (e.g., –NO2) and reduced by electron‑donating groups (e.g., –CH3).

Physical Properties

PropertyValue
Melting point$-11.5^\circ\text{C}$
Boiling point$182^\circ\text{C}$
Density (20°C)$1.07\,\text{g cm}^{-3}$
Solubility in water$8.3\,\text{g L}^{-1}$

Electrophilic Substitution

  • The –OH group is strongly activating and ortho/para directing due to resonance donation of lone‑pair electrons.
  • Typical reactions: nitration, sulfonation, halogenation give mainly o‑ and p‑substituted products.
  • Example (nitration): $C_6H_5OH + HNO_3 \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4} \text{ortho- and para‑nitrophenol} + H_2O$.

Solved Example

Calculate the pH of a $0.01\,\text{M}$ phenol solution. Given $K_a = 1.0\times10^{-10}$.

  1. Assume $x = [H^+] = [C_6H_5O^-]$ and $[C_6H_5OH] \approx 0.01 - x \approx 0.01$.
  2. From $K_a = \frac{x^2}{0.01}$, $x = \sqrt{K_a\times0.01}=\sqrt{1.0\times10^{-12}}=1.0\times10^{-6}\,\text{M}$.
  3. pH $= -\log_{10}x = -\log_{10}(1.0\times10^{-6}) = 6.0$.

Thus a dilute phenol solution is mildly acidic (pH ≈ 6).

Physical and Chemical Properties of Ethers (including $R-O-R'$, cleavage reactions)

Definition: Ethers are a class of organic compounds having an oxygen atom bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups, represented as $R-O-R'$.

Physical Properties

PropertyTypical Value/Trend
Boiling pointLower than corresponding alcohols; $< 100^\circ\text{C}$ for most simple ethers
Solubility in waterIncreases with decreasing carbon chain; $R-O-R'$ (C \le 2) miscible
PolarityDipole moment $\mu = q \times d$ (\approx $1.5$–$2.0$ D); non‑hydrogen‑bonding
Dielectric constant~$4.5$–$5.0$ (dimensionless)

Chemical Properties

  • Cleavage by hydrogen halides: $R-O-R' + HX \rightarrow R-X + R'-OH$ (if $R$ is primary, $R'$ tertiary, etc.).
  • Cleavage by hot concentrated $H_2SO_4$: yields alcohols and alkyl sulfates.
  • Reaction with sodium metal: $R-O-R' + 2Na \rightarrow R-Na + R'-ONa$ (formation of alkoxides).
  • Oxidation of allylic/benzylic ethers gives carbonyl compounds.

Key Formula

Dipole moment: $\mu = q \times d$ (SI: C·m; 1 D = $3.33564 \times 10^{-30}$ C·m).

Solved Example

Complete the cleavage of $0.50\ \text{mol}$ diethyl ether ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-O-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$) with hydroiodic acid.

Reaction: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2-O-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + HI \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2I + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2OH$.

From stoichiometry, $1\ \text{mol}$ ether requires $1\ \text{mol}$ $HI$. Hence moles of $HI$ needed = $0.50\ \text{mol}$. Mass of $HI$ = $0.50 \times 127.9\ \text{g mol}^{-1}=63.95\ \text{g}$.

Important Uses of Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers in industry and daily life

Alcohols – organic compounds containing a hydroxyl group $-OH$ attached to a saturated carbon atom. General formula $C_nH_{2n+1}OH$.

Phenols – aromatic compounds where $-OH$ is directly bonded to a benzene ring. General formula $C_6H_5OH$.

Ethers – compounds having an oxygen atom linked to two alkyl or aryl groups, general formula $R-O-R'$.

  • Industrial uses
    • Alcohols: solvent (e.g., $CH_3CH_2OH$), antifreeze ($C_2H_5OH$), raw material for ethyl acetate, acetic acid, and polymers.
    • Phenols: production of bis‑phenol A, phenolic resins, disinfectants, and pharmaceuticals.
    • Ethers: diethyl ether as a laboratory solvent and anesthetic; MTBE as gasoline octane enhancer.
  • Daily life
    • Alcohols: beverages, hand sanitizers, cosmetics.
    • Phenols: antiseptic creams, oral care products.
    • Ethers: flavoring agents, propellants in aerosol cans.

ReactionBalanced equation
General$C_nH_{2n+1}OH + \\frac{3n+1}{2} O_2 \\rightarrow n CO_2 + (n+1) H_2O$

Solved Example:

Calculate the mass of ethanol required to produce $50\,\text{g}$ of ethyl acetate ($C_4H_8O_2$) via esterification with acetic acid. Reaction: $CH_3CH_2OH + CH_3COOH \\rightarrow CH_3COOCH_2CH_3 + H_2O$.

Steps:

  1. Molar mass of ethyl acetate = $88\,\text{g mol}^{-1}$. Moles needed = $\\frac{50}{88}=0.568\,\text{mol}$.
  2. Stoichiometry: 1 mol ethanol gives 1 mol ethyl acetate → need $0.568\,\text{mol}$ ethanol.
  3. Molar mass of ethanol = $46\,\text{g mol}^{-1}$. Mass = $0.568 \times 46 = 26.1\,\text{g}$.

Thus $26\,\text{g}$ of ethanol is sufficient (theoretical yield).
