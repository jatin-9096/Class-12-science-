Nomenclature of Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

Definition: Alcohols, phenols and ethers are organic compounds containing –OH or –O– groups attached to saturated carbon atoms.

Key Points for Nomenclature

  • Parent chain is the longest continuous carbon chain containing the –OH (alcohol) or –O– (ether) group.
  • Numbering starts from the end nearest to the principal functional group; the –OH gets the lowest possible locant.
  • Suffix “‑$\\text{ol}$” for alcohols, “‑$\\text{phenol}$” for phenols; ethers are named using the “alkoxy‑” prefix (e.g., $\\text{methoxy}$‑propane).
  • Multiple –OH groups: “‑$\\text{diol}$”, “‑$\\text{triol}$”, etc., with locants.
  • When –OH is attached to an aromatic ring, the compound is named as a phenol; the aromatic ring is treated as a substituted benzene.
Number of –OHSuffix
1$\\text{ol}$
2$\\text{diol}$
3$\\text{triol}$

General formula: $C_{n}H_{2n+2}O$ for saturated alcohols; molecular weight $M=\sum M_{i}$ (g·mol$^{-1}$).

Example (Solved): Name $CH_{3}CH(CH_{3})CH_{2}OH$.

Step 1: Longest chain containing –OH has 3 carbon atoms → propan‑.

Step 2: –OH receives the lowest locant → 1‑propanol.

Step 3: Methyl substituent on C‑2 → 2‑methyl‑1‑propanol.

Answer: 2‑methyl‑1‑propanol.

Methods of Preparation of Alcohols

Methods of Preparation of Alcohols – Alcohols are mainly obtained by (i) addition of water to unsaturated compounds, (ii) reduction of carbonyl compounds, (iii) Grignard reaction, (iv) fermentation, and (v) hydrolysis of alkyl halides.

  • Acid‑catalysed hydration of alkenes: $R-CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4, 300^\circ C} R-CH_2-CH_2-OH$.
  • Hydroboration‑oxidation: $R-CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{BH_3, THF} R-CH_2-CH_2-BH_2 \xrightarrow{H_2O_2/NaOH} R-CH_2-CH_2-OH$ (anti‑Markovnikov).
  • Reduction of carbonyl compounds: $R-CHO + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni, 150^\circ C} R-CH_2OH$; $R_2C=O + H_2 \xrightarrow{Ni} R_2CHOH$.
  • Grignard reaction: $R-MgX + R'CHO \xrightarrow{Et_2O} R-R'CH(OH)R$ (primary/secondary alcohol).
  • Fermentation: $C_6H_{12}O_6 \xrightarrow{yeast} 2 C_2H_5OH + 2 CO_2$ (ethanol).
MethodRepresentative Reaction
Acidic hydration$C_2H_4 + H_2O \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4, 300^\circ C} C_2H_5OH$
Hydroboration‑oxidation$CH_2=CHCH_3 \xrightarrow{BH_3} CH_3CH_2CH_2BH_2 \xrightarrow{H_2O_2/NaOH} CH_3CH_2CH_2OH$

Solved Example: Prepare ethanol by hydration of ethene. 10.0 g ethene ($M=28.05\,\text{g mol}^{-1}$) is reacted. Moles of ethene $=\frac{10.0}{28.05}=0.357\,\text{mol}$. Theoretical ethanol $=0.357\times46.07=16.4\,\text{g}$. If 14.0 g ethanol is obtained, % yield $=\frac{14.0}{16.4}\times100=85.4\%$. Key formula: $\%\,\text{yield}=\frac{\text{actual mass}}{\text{theoretical mass}}\times100$ (SI units: mass in g, temperature in $^\circ C$, pressure in $\text{atm}$).

Methods of Preparation of Phenols

Definition: Phenols are aromatic compounds in which a hydroxyl group is directly bonded to a benzene ring, formula $C_6H_5OH$.

Key Methods of Preparation

  • Cumene (Isopropylbenzene) Process: Alkylation of benzene to cumene, oxidation to cumene hydroperoxide, acid‑catalysed cleavage giving phenol and acetone.
    Overall: $C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 + O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{acid}} C_6H_5OH + (CH_3)_2CO$.
  • Hydrolysis of Phenylmagnesium Bromide: $C_6H_5MgBr + H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_5OH + Mg(OH)Br$ (dry ether, $0\!\,^\circ C$).
  • Diazonium Salt Hydrolysis: $C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} C_6H_5OH + N_2 + HCl$ (70–80$\,^{\circ}C$, $\approx 1\,atm$).
  • Fusion of Sodium Phenoxide: $C_6H_5SO_3H + NaOH \xrightarrow{\text{fusion 300$\,^{\circ}C$}} C_6H_5ONa + Na_2SO_3$, then acidification gives phenol.
  • Hydrolysis of Phenyl Acetate: $C_6H_5OCOCH_3 + NaOH \xrightarrow{\text{reflux}} C_6H_5OH + CH_3COONa$.
  • Substitution of Phenyl Halides: $C_6H_5Cl + NaOH \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH, $\approx 200\,^{\circ}C$}} C_6H_5OH + NaCl$ (requires strong nucleophile).

Solved Example (Cumene Process)

Calculate mass of phenol obtained from $150\,g$ cumene ($C_9H_{12}$) with $85\%$ yield.

Molar mass $M_{cumene}=120\,g\,mol^{-1}$, $M_{phenol}=94\,g\,mol^{-1}$.
\[n_{cumene}=\frac{150}{120}=1.25\,mol\]
Stoichiometry: 1 mol cumene → 1 mol phenol.
\[m_{theoretical}=1.25\times94=117.5\,g\]
Actual mass $=117.5\times0.85=99.9\,g\approx100\,g$.

SI Units: Mass – $g$, Temperature – $\,^{\circ}C$, Pressure – $atm$, Yield – $\%$.

Methods of Preparation of Ethers

Definition: An ether is a class of organic compounds having the general formula $R\! -\! O\! -\! R'$ where $R$ and $R'$ are alkyl or aryl groups. The oxygen atom is divalent and links two carbon chains.

Major methods of preparation (exam‑oriented):

  • Williamson ether synthesis: Alkoxide ion $RO^-$ reacts with a primary alkyl halide $R'X$ under SN2 conditions.
    $$R O^- + R' X \xrightarrow[\text{dry ether}]{\text{Na}} R O R' + X^-$$
    Key points: $R'$ must be primary (or methyl); $R$ may be primary, secondary or phenyl; no elimination side‑reaction.
  • Acid‑catalysed dehydration of alcohols: Two molecules of a primary alcohol condense \in the presence of concentrated $H_2SO_4$ at $140\!\,^\circ\!C$.
    $$2\,R\! -\! OH \xrightarrow[\text{140\,°C}]{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} R\! -\! O\! -\! R + H_2O$$
    Works best for symmetrical ethers (e.g., diethyl ether).
  • Dehydration of vicinal diols: Heating a 1,2‑diol with $H_2SO_4$ at $180\!\,^\circ\!C$ gives a cyclic ether (tetrahydrofuran from 1,4‑butanediol).
    $$HO\! -\! (CH_2)_n \! -\! OH \xrightarrow[\text{180\,°C}]{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} \text{cyclic ether} + H_2O$$

SI units \to remember: Temperature $\text{K}$ (or $\!^\circ\!C$), pressure $\text{Pa}$ (or $\text{atm}$), concentration $\text{mol\,L}^{-1}$.

Solved example (1 mark – 2 marks): Prepare diethyl ether from ethanol.

  1. Take $50\,\text{mL}$ of absolute ethanol \in a dry flask.
  2. Add $5\,\text{mL}$ of conc. $H_2SO_4$ dropwise while maintaining the temperature below $140\!\,^\circ\!C$.
  3. Heat the mixture at $140\!\,^\circ\!C$ for $30\,\text{min}$; collect the ether vapour by distillation (boiling point $34.6\!\,^\circ\!C$).
  4. Dry the distillate over anhydrous $CaCl_2$ and measure the yield.

Overall reaction:
$$2\,CH_3CH_2OH \xrightarrow[\text{140\,°C}]{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} CH_3CH_2OCH_2CH_3 + H_2O$$

Key take‑away: For unsymmetrical ethers, Williamson synthesis is preferred; for symmetrical ethers, acid‑catalysed dehydration is the quickest laboratory method.

Physical and Chemical Properties of Alcohols

Alcohols – Definition: Organic compounds containing one or more hydroxyl ($-\\mathrm{OH}$) groups attached to a saturated carbon atom.

Physical Properties

  • Boiling point: higher than corresponding hydrocarbons due to H‑bonding. Approx. $\\Delta T_b = K_b \\cdot m$ where $K_b$ is ebullioscopic constant (unit $\\,^{\\circ}\\mathrm{C\\,kg\\,mol^{-1}}$).
  • Density: $0.79-0.88\\,\\mathrm{g\\,cm^{-3}}$ at $20\\,^{\\circ}\\mathrm{C}$.
  • Refractive index ($n_D$): $1.33-1.48$.
  • Solubility: miscible with water if $\\le 3$ carbon atoms; decreases with chain length.

Chemical Properties

  • Oxidation: primary alcohol $\\xrightarrow{[O]}$ aldehyde $\\rightarrow$ acid; secondary alcohol $\\rightarrow$ ketone; tertiary alcohol resistant.
  • Dehydration (acid catalysed): $\\mathrm{RCH_2CH_2OH \\xrightarrow{H_2SO_4} RCH=CH_2 + H_2O}$.
  • Esterification: $\\mathrm{RCH_2OH + R'COOH \\xrightarrow{H^+} R'COOR + H_2O}$.
  • Substitution (SN1/SN2) with $\\mathrm{HX}$ or $\\mathrm{ROH}$.

Ethanol $K_b$$0.52\\,^{\\circ}\\mathrm{C\\,kg\\,mol^{-1}}$
Density (20°C)$0.789\\,\\mathrm{g\\,cm^{-3}}$ (ethanol)

Solved Example

Calculate the mass of ethanol required to raise the boiling point of $500\\,\\mathrm{g}$ of ethanol by $0.5\\,^{\\circ}\\mathrm{C}$. Use $K_b = 0.52\\,^{\\circ}\\mathrm{C\\,kg\\,mol^{-1}}$ and $M_{ethanol}=46.07\\,\\mathrm{g\\,mol^{-1}}$.

  1. Molality needed: $m = \\frac{\\Delta T_b}{K_b}=\\frac{0.5}{0.52}=0.962\\,\\mathrm{mol\\,kg^{-1}}$.
  2. Mass of solvent (ethanol) = $0.500\\,\\mathrm{kg}$, so moles of solute $= m \\times 0.500 = 0.481\\,\\mathrm{mol}$.
  3. Mass of solute = $0.481\\,\\mathrm{mol}\\times 46.07\\,\\mathrm{g\\,mol^{-1}} = 22.2\\,\\mathrm{g}$.

Thus $22\\,\\mathrm{g}$ of a non‑volatile solute (e.g., glucose) would raise the boiling point by $0.5\\,^{\\circ}\\mathrm{C}$.

Physical and Chemical Properties of Phenols

Definition: Phenol (hydroxybenzene) is an aromatic compound with the formula $C_6H_5OH$ where a hydroxyl group is directly attached to a benzene ring.

Physical Properties (typical values):

  • Melting point: $40.5^\circ\text{C}$
  • Boiling point: $182^\circ\text{C}$
  • Density: $1.07\ \text{g cm}^{-3}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$
  • Solubility in water: $8.3\ \text{g per 100 mL}$ (at $25^\circ\text{C}$)
  • Acidic character: weak acid, $pK_a \approx 10$

Key Chemical Properties:

  • Acidity: $C_6H_5OH \rightleftharpoons C_6H_5O^- + H^+$; $K_a = 10^{-pK_a} \approx 1.0\\times10^{-10}$.
  • Reaction with NaOH: $C_6H_5OH + NaOH \\rightarrow C_6H_5ONa + H_2O$ (forms phenoxide salt).
  • Electrophilic substitution: ortho-/para-directing; e.g., bromination gives $2,4,6$‑tribromophenol.
  • Oxidation: strong oxidants (e.g., $KMnO_4$) convert phenol to $p$‑benzoquinone.
  • Formation of ethers: phenol + alkyl halide $\\xrightarrow{NaOH}$ alkyl phenyl ether.
Compound$pK_a$Acid Strength
Phenol$10$Weak
Carboxylic acid (e.g., acetic acid)$4.8$Stronger

Solved Example: Calculate the pH of a $0.010\ \text{M}$ phenol solution.

Given $K_a = 1.0\\times10^{-10}$, let $x$ be $[H^+]$ formed.

$K_a = \\frac{x^2}{c-x} \approx \\frac{x^2}{c}$ (since $x \ll c$). Hence $x = \\sqrt{K_a c} = \\sqrt{1.0\\times10^{-10}\\times0.010} = 1.0\\times10^{-6}\ \text{M}$.

pH $= -\\log x = -\\log(1.0\\times10^{-6}) = 6.00$.

Thus a dilute phenol solution is only mildly acidic.

Physical and Chemical Properties of Ethers

Definition: An ether is an organic compound containing an oxygen atom bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups, general formula $R\!{-}\!O\!{-}\!R'$.

Physical properties

  • Boiling point ($T_b$) lies between corresponding alcohols and alkanes; increases with molecular weight and polarity.
  • Solubility: Low in water due to weak hydrogen‑bonding; soluble in organic solvents.
  • Polarity: Dipole moment ($\mu$) arises from C–O–C angle (~$110^\circ$). Typical values $\mu = 1.5\text{–}5.0\,\text{D}$ (1 D $=3.33564\times10^{-30}\,\text{C·m}$).
  • Density: $0.70\text{–}0.85\,\text{g·cm}^{-3}$.

Key formulae

  • Dipole moment: $\mu = q\,d$ where $q$ is effective charge separation and $d$ distance.
  • Boiling‑point elevation: $\Delta T_b = K_b\,i\,m$ (SI: $K_b$ in K·kg·mol$^{-1}$, $m$ in mol·kg$^{-1}$).

Chemical properties

  • Cleavage with HX: $R\!{-}\!O\!{-}\!R' + HX \rightarrow R\!{-}\!X + R'\!{-}\!OH$ (e.g., $\text{CH}_3\!{-}\!O\!{-}\!\text{CH}_3 + HBr \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\!{-}\!Br + \text{CH}_3\!{-}\!OH$).
  • Oxidation: Strong oxidizers convert ethers to carbonyl compounds (e.g., $\text{R}_2\!O + \text{[O]} \rightarrow \text{RCHO}$ for methyl ethyl ether).
  • Peroxide formation: $\text{R}_2\!O + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{ROOR}$ – hazardous.

Solved example (dipole moment)

Calculate the dipole moment of diethyl ether if the effective charge separation is $q = 1.6\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$ and the distance $d = 1.2\times10^{-10}\,\text{m}$.

Solution:

  • $\mu = q\,d = (1.6\times10^{-19})(1.2\times10^{-10}) = 1.92\times10^{-29}\,\text{C·m}$
  • Convert to Debye: $\mu_{\text{D}} = \dfrac{1.92\times10^{-29}}{3.33564\times10^{-30}} \approx 5.8\,\text{D}$

Thus, diethyl ether has a dipole moment of approximately $5.8\,\text{D}$, explaining its moderate polarity and solubility behaviour.

Uses and Applications

Definitions

  • Alcohol: organic compound containing a hydroxyl group $-OH$ attached to a saturated carbon; general formula $C_nH_{2n+1}OH$.
  • Phenol: aromatic compound where $-OH$ is directly bonded to a benzene ring; formula $C_6H_5OH$.
  • Ether: compound with an oxygen atom linking two alkyl or aryl groups; general formula $R-O-R'$.

Key Uses & Applications

CompoundMajor Applications
AlcoholsSolvent (e.g., ethanol), antiseptic (e.g., isopropanol), fuel additive (e.g., methanol), raw material for esters, polymers.
PhenolsDisinfectant (e.g., carbolic acid), precursor to plastics (Bakelite), pharmaceuticals (e.g., aspirin), antioxidants.
EthersOrganic solvent (diethyl ether), anesthetic, gasoline additive (MTBE), synthesis of polymers.

Typical Quantitative Relation

For solution preparation: $M = \dfrac{n}{V}$ where $M$ is molarity ($mol\,L^{-1}$), $n$ is amount of solute in moles, $V$ is volume in litres.

Solved Example

Prepare $500\;mL$ of $0.20\;M$ ethanol solution. Find mass of ethanol required (density $=0.789\;g\,mL^{-1}$, molar mass $=46.07\;g\,mol^{-1}$).

  1. Convert volume to litres: $V = 0.500\;L$.
  2. Calculate moles: $n = M \times V = 0.20 \times 0.500 = 0.10\;mol$.
  3. Mass: $m = n \times M_{r} = 0.10 \times 46.07 = 4.607\;g$.
  4. Volume of ethanol: $V_{eth} = \dfrac{m}{\rho} = \dfrac{4.607}{0.789} \approx 5.84\;mL$.

Thus $5.8\;mL$ of ethanol is diluted to $500\;mL$ to obtain the required solution.
