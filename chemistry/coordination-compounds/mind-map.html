Definition, scope and importance of coordination compounds

Definition: A coordination compound is a discrete chemical species containing a central metal atom/ion (the central atom) bonded to a group of molecules or ions called ligands through coordinate (dative) covalent bonds.

Scope & Importance:

  • Explains the structure and reactivity of many transition‑metal complexes used in catalysis, bio‑inorganic systems and material science.
  • Provides the basis for colour, magnetic and kinetic behaviour of metal complexes.
  • Essential for understanding analytical techniques (e.g., complexometric titration) and industrial processes (e.g., plating, pigments).

Key Concepts & Formulas

  • Coordination number (CN): $\text{CN}=\text{total number of donor atoms attached \to the metal}$.
  • Oxidation state (OS) of the metal: $\text{OS}=\text{charge of complex} - \sum \text{charges contributed by ligands}$.
  • Stability (formation) constant: $K_f=\frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{M}^{z+}][\text{L}]^n}$ (unitless).

SI Units

  • Charge – coulomb (C) (often expressed as oxidation number, dimensionless).
  • Stability constant – dimensionless (ratio of activities).

Solved Example

Find the oxidation state of Cr in $[\text{Cr}(NH_3)_4Cl_2]Cl_3$.

  1. Write the overall charge of the complex ion: the outer $Cl_3^-$ gives a net charge of $-3$ to the whole compound.
  2. Inside the brackets, ligands contribute: $4\times NH_3$ (neutral) and $2\times Cl^-$ (each $-1$). Total ligand charge = $2(-1) = -2$.
  3. Let the oxidation state of Cr be $x$. Then $x + (-2) = $ charge of the complex ion. Since the outer $Cl_3^-$ balances the $-3$ charge, the bracketed ion must be $+3$.
  4. Thus $x - 2 = +3 \Rightarrow x = +5$.

Hence, the oxidation state of Cr in the given complex is $+5$.

Ligands: classification, denticity and ambidentate behaviour

Ligands – Definition: A ligand is an atom, ion or molecule that donates at least one electron pair to a metal centre forming a coordinate covalent bond.

Classification (based on donor atoms, charge, denticity):

ClassDonor atom(s)ChargeDenticity
Monodentate$\text{N, O, S, P, Cl, etc.}$Neutral / Anionic$1$
BidentateTwo donor atomsNeutral / Anionic$2$
Polydentate (Chelate)≥3 donor atomsNeutral / Anionic$\ge 3$
AmbidentateSingle donor atom but two possible sites (e.g., $NO_2^-$, $SCN^-$)Anionic$1$

Denticity ($n$): Number of donor atoms of a ligand that are simultaneously bonded to the metal. The total number of donor atoms attached to the metal equals the coordination number (C.N.):

$$\text{C.N.}=\sum n_i$$ where $n_i$ is denticity of the $i^{th}$ ligand.

Higher denticity enhances stability (chelate effect). The overall stability constant is expressed as $K_f=\frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]^n}$.

Ambidentate behaviour: Ligands such as $NO_2^-$ (nitro vs nitrito) and $SCN^-$ (thiocyanato‑S vs thiocyanato‑N) can bind through either of two atoms, giving rise \to linkage isomers, a subclass of structural isomerism.

SI Units: No separate unit; donor‑atom count is dimensionless. Charge, when required, is expressed \in coulombs ($\text{C}$). Oxidation state of the metal is denoted \in Roman numerals without units.

Solved Example:

Find the coordination number and identify the type of each ligand \in $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2(\text{NO}_2)]$.

  • NH$_3$ – monodentate ($n=1$), neutral.
  • Cl$^-$ – monodentate ($n=1$), anionic.
  • NO$_2^-$ – ambidentate monodentate ($n=1$), anionic.

Using $$\text{C.N.}=1+1+1+1+1=5$$, the complex has a coordination number of $5$ and contains three monodentate ligands (one ambidentate).

Coordination number, geometry and oxidation state of central metal ion

Coordination Number (CN) – total number of donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal ion.

Oxidation State (OS) – calculated from overall charge of the complex.

Geometry – dictated by CN and electronic configuration.

  • CN = 2 → linear
  • CN = 4 → tetrahedral ($\\text{Td}$) or square planar ($\\text{D}_{4h}$)
  • CN = 6 → octahedral ($\\text{Oh}$)
  • CN = 8 → square antiprismatic or cubic

Key formulae

ParameterExpression
Oxidation State$OS = \\text{charge of complex} - \\sum \\text{charges of ligands}$
Effective atomic number (EAN)$EAN = Z - OS + \\sum n_i$ where $Z$ = atomic number, $n_i$ = electrons donated by $i^{th}$ ligand

SI Units

  • Charge – coulomb (C) (but oxidation state is dimensionless)
  • Bond length – metre (m)

Solved Example

Find CN, geometry and OS of $[\\text{Co}(\\text{NH}_3)_5\\text{Cl}]\\text{Cl}_2$.

  1. Identify ligands: five $\\text{NH}_3$ (neutral, monodentate) and one $\\text{Cl}^-$ (monodentate). Total donor atoms = $5+1=6$. Hence $CN=6$.
  2. CN $=6$ → octahedral geometry.
  3. Overall charge of complex ion = $+2$ (since two external $\\text{Cl}^-$ balance). Using formula: $OS = (+2) - [5(0) + (-1)] = +3$. Thus Co is in $+3$ oxidation state.

Result: $CN=6$, octahedral, $OS(\\text{Co})=+3$.

Bonding in coordination compounds: Valence Bond Theory and Crystal Field Theory

Valence Bond Theory (VBT) explains the geometry of a complex by hybridisation of the central metal ion’s atomic orbitals. The number of hybrid orbitals = coordination number. Example: $\\text{[Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}$ is square planar → $sp^2d$ hybridisation.

Crystal Field Theory (CFT) treats ligands as point charges that split the degenerate d‑orbitals in a specific geometry.

  • Octahedral: $e_g$ (higher) and $t_{2g}$ (lower) split by $\\Delta_0$.
  • Tetrahedral: $e$ (higher) and $t_2$ (lower) split by $\\Delta_t = \\frac{4}{9}\\Delta_0$.

Key formulas:

QuantityExpression
Crystal‑field splitting energy$\\Delta_0$ (kJ·mol$^{-1}$)
Crystal‑field stabilisation energy (octahedral)$\\text{CFSE}=(-0.4n_{t_{2g}}+0.6n_{e_g})\\Delta_0$
Spin‑only magnetic moment$\\mu_{\\text{eff}}=\\sqrt{n(n+2)}\\,\\text{BM}$

Example: Find $\\mu_{\\text{eff}}$ and CFSE for $\\text{[Fe(H}_2\\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$ (high‑spin $d^6$).

  1. High‑spin $d^6$ → electron configuration $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$, $n=4$ unpaired electrons.
  2. $\\mu_{\\text{eff}}=\\sqrt{4(4+2)}=\\sqrt{24}=4.90\\,\\text{BM}$.
  3. CFSE $= (-0.4\\times4+0.6\\times2)\\Delta_0 = (-1.6+1.2)\\Delta_0 = -0.4\\Delta_0$.

Thus the complex is paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons and a modest stabilisation of $0.4\\Delta_0$.

Electronic configurations, colour and magnetic properties

Electronic configuration of a transition metal ion in a complex is obtained by removing electrons from the outermost $s$ orbital first, then filling the $d$ orbitals according to the crystal‑field splitting diagram. In an octahedral field the five $d$ orbitals split into a lower‑energy $t_{2g}$ set (three orbitals) and a higher‑energy $e_g$ set (two orbitals) with a separation $\Delta_{o}$ (crystal‑field splitting energy, unit: kJ·mol$^{-1}$).

Orbital setNumber of orbitalsEnergy relative to barycentre
$t_{2g}$3$-\frac{2}{5}\,\Delta_{o}$
$e_{g}$2$+\frac{3}{5}\,\Delta_{o}$

Colour arises from electronic transitions between the split $d$ levels (d–d transition) or charge‑transfer transitions. The wavelength $\lambda$ of the absorbed light is related to $\Delta$ by $\lambda=\frac{hc}{\Delta}$, where $h$ is Planck’s constant ($6.626\times10^{-34}\,\text{J·s}$) and $c$ is the speed of light ($3.00\times10^{8}\,\text{m·s}^{-1}$). The complementary colour is observed.

Magnetic properties are governed by the number $n$ of unpaired electrons. The spin‑only magnetic moment is given by $\mu_{so}=\sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\text{BM}$ (Bohr magneton, $\mu_{B}=9.274\times10^{-24}\,\text{J·T}^{-1}$). For complexes with strong spin–orbit coupling, the orbital contribution may be added.

Example: Determine $\mu_{so}$ for the high‑spin complex $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$ (Fe$^{2+}$, $d^6$).

  • Octahedral high‑spin $d^6$ configuration: $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$ → $n=4$ unpaired electrons.
  • $\mu_{so}=\sqrt{4(4+2)}=\sqrt{24}=4.90\,\text{BM}$.

The observed magnetic moment (~5.0 BM) confirms the high‑spin arrangement and validates the crystal‑field theory for this complex.

Isomerism in coordination compounds: structural and stereoisomerism

Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

Isomerism refers to the existence of two or more compounds having the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms or ligands, leading to distinct chemical and physical properties.

  • Structural (Constitutional) Isomerism
    • Ionisation isomerism: Exchange of an anionic ligand with the counter‑ion. Example: $[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br$ ↔ $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$.
    • Hydrate isomerism: Water molecules are either coordinated or present as water of crystallisation. $[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3$ ↔ $[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2·H_2O$.
    • Coordination isomerism: Interchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complexes. $[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]$ ↔ $[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$.
    • Linkage isomerism: Ligand can bind through different donor atoms. $[Co(NH_3)_5NO_2]Cl_2$ (NO2–N) ↔ $[Co(NH_3)_5ONO]Cl_2$ (NO2–O).
  • Stereoisomerism
    • Geometrical (cis‑trans) isomerism: Different spatial arrangement of ligands in octahedral or square planar complexes. $[MA_2B_2]$ octahedral gives cis and trans forms.
    • Optical isomerism: Non‑superimposable mirror images (enantiomers) due to chiral centre or arrangement. Exists in complexes like $[Co(en)_3]^{3+}$ where $en$ = ethylenediamine.

Key Formula

ParameterExpression
Coordination number$CN$
Oxidation state$OS$
Number of geometrical isomers for $[MA_2B_2]$$n_{geom}=2$

Solved Example

Determine the geometrical isomers of $[MA_2B_2]$ (octahedral).

  1. Place the two $A$ ligands either adjacent (cis) or opposite (trans) to each other.
  2. For each arrangement, the two $B$ ligands occupy the remaining positions uniquely.
  3. Thus, $n_{geom}=2$ – one cis and one trans isomer.

Both isomers have the same formula but differ in colour and magnetic behaviour, a typical test in exams.

Nomenclature of coordination compounds (IUPAC rules)

Definition: A coordination compound consists of a central metal atom/ion surrounded by ligands (neutral molecules or anions) that donate one or more electron pairs. The IUPAC name reflects the composition, oxidation state of the metal and the nature of the counter‑ion.

Key Rules (IUPAC)

  • List ligands in alphabetical order (ignore prefixes such as di‑, tri‑, tetra‑).
  • Use the anionic form of ligand names ending in ‑ido (e.g., chloro, cyanido); neutral ligands retain their common name (e.g., ammine, water).
  • Prefix the number of identical ligands with di‑, tri‑, tetra‑, penta‑, hexa‑ etc.
  • State the oxidation state of the metal in Roman numerals in parentheses immediately after the metal name.
  • If the complex is an anion, suffix ‑ate to the metal name (e.g., ferrate); if neutral, no suffix.
  • Write the counter‑ion (if any) after the complex name, separated by a space.

Useful Formulas

  • Oxidation state of metal: $OS=\text{overall charge of complex}-\sum(\text{charges of ligands})$.
  • Coordination number: $CN=\sum(\text{donor atoms supplied by all ligands})$.

Both $OS$ and $CN$ are dimensionless (unit: –).

Typical Naming Order (Table)

StepContent
1Alphabetical list of ligands with prefixes
2Central metal name + oxidation state in Roman numerals
3Suffix ‑ate if complex is anionic
4Counter‑ion (if any)

Solved Example

Given complex: $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2$.

  • Identify ligands: ammine (neutral) ×5, chloro (anionic) ×1.
  • Alphabetical order: ammine, chloro → name = pentaamminechloro.
  • Calculate oxidation state of Co:
    $OS=+2-(-1)=+3$ (overall charge of complex ion is $+2$, ligand charges sum to $-1$).
  • Metal name with oxidation state: cobalt(III).
  • Complex is a cation, so no ‑ate suffix.
  • Counter‑ion: $\text{Cl}^-$ → write as chloride.

Final IUPAC name: pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride.

Preparation, stability constants and applications of coordination compounds

Definition: A coordination compound consists of a central metal ion/atom surrounded by ligands forming coordinate covalent bonds.

Common preparation methods

MethodReaction type
Direct combinationMetal ion + excess ligand → complex
Ligand substitution$\\text{[M(L)}_x\\text{] + L'} \rightarrow \\text{[M(L)}_{x-1}\\text{L'}] + L$
RedoxMetal oxidation state changes during complex formation
Template synthesisLigand framework formed around metal ion

Stability (Formation) constant

  • $\\frac{[ML_n]}{[M][L]^n}$ (dimensionless)
  • Stepwise constants $K_1, K_2, \dots, K_n$ and cumulative constant $\\beta_n = K_1 K_2 \dots K_n$.
  • Logarithmic form $\\log \\beta_n$ is often used.

Applications

  • Analytical chemistry – EDTA titration (complexometric analysis).
  • Medicinal chemistry – metal‑based drugs (e.g., cisplatin).
  • Catalysis – homogeneous catalysts like $\\text{[Rh(CO)_2Cl]_2}$.
  • Biomedical imaging – Gd$^{3+}$ complexes as MRI contrast agents.

Solved example

Given $K_1 = 1.0\\times10^5$ and $K_2 = 2.0\\times10^3$ for the stepwise formation of $ML_2^{2+}$, calculate $\\beta_2$.

Solution: $\\beta_2 = K_1 \times K_2 = (1.0\\times10^5)(2.0\\times10^3) = 2.0\\times10^8$. Hence $\\log \\beta_2 = 8.30$.
