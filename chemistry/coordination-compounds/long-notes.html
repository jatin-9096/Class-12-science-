Definition and scope of coordination compounds

Definition: A coordination compound (or complex) consists of a central metal atom/ion surrounded by a set of ligands that are bound through coordinate covalent bonds.

\n

Scope: Includes mononuclear, polynuclear, homoleptic, heteroleptic, chelates, and organometallic complexes. It covers concepts such as coordination number, oxidation state, geometry, isomerism, and stability constants.

\n
    \n
  • Coordination number (CN): Number of donor atoms directly attached to the metal centre.
    • \n
  • Oxidation state (OS) of metal: $\text{OS}=\text{overall charge of complex}-\sum \text{(charges of ligands)}$.
    • \n
  • Ligand classification: L‑type (neutral), X‑type (anionic), Z‑type (neutral with lone pair donation).
    • \n
  • Chelate effect: Multidentate ligands form more stable complexes; stability expressed by formation constant $K_f$.
    • \n
\n\n\n\n\n
ParameterExpressionSI Unit
Coordination number$\text{CN}=\text{number of donor atoms}$dimensionless
Formation constant$K_f=\frac{[ML_n]}{[M][L]^n}$dimensionless
\n

Solved Example: Find the oxidation state of Cr in $[Cr(NH_3)_4Cl_2]Cl_3$.

\n
    \n
  1. Overall charge of the complex ion $[Cr(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^{3+}$ is $+3$ (since three external $Cl^-$ balance the charge).
    2. \n
  2. Charges of ligands inside the ion: $NH_3$ (neutral) contributes $0$, each $Cl^-$ contributes $-1$. Total ligand charge $=2\times(-1)=-2$.
    3. \n
  3. Using $\text{OS}=+3-(-2)=+5$, the oxidation state of Cr is $+5$.
    4. \n
\n

Understanding these fundamentals provides the foundation for studying geometry, isomerism, and applications of coordination chemistry in catalysis, bio‑inorganic systems, and material science.

Ligands: classification and donor atoms

Ligand – Definition: A species that donates at least one electron pair to a metal centre forming a coordinate covalent bond.

Classification (based on donor atoms):

  • Monodentate – one donor atom (e.g., $\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{Cl^-}$).
  • Bidentate – two donor atoms (e.g., ethylenediamine, $\mathrm{en}$).
  • Polydentate (chelating) – three or more donor atoms (e.g., $\mathrm{EDTA^{4-}}$).
  • Ambidentate – same atom can bind through two different sites (e.g., $\mathrm{NO_2^-}$, $\mathrm{SCN^-}$).

Donor atom groups (common):

GroupTypical donor atoms
O‑donor$\mathrm{H_2O}$, $\mathrm{OH^-}$, $\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$
N‑donor$\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{en}$, $\mathrm{CN^-}$
S‑donor$\mathrm{HS^-}$, $\mathrm{SCN^-}$ (through S)
P‑donor$\mathrm{PR_3}$, $\mathrm{PF_3}$

Key formulas:

  • Coordination number (CN): $\text{CN}=\text{number of donor atoms attached \to the metal}$.
  • Chelate‑ring size: $\text{size}=\text{number of atoms \in the smallest ring formed}$ (usually 5‑ or 6‑membered rings are most stable).

Solved example:

Find the coordination number of the complex $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2}]\mathrm{Cl}$.

  • Identify donor atoms in the inner sphere: four $\mathrm{NH_3}$ (each N‑donor) + two $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ (each Cl‑donor).
  • Total donor atoms $=4+2=6$.
  • Therefore $\text{CN}=6$, which corresponds to an octahedral geometry.

Remember: monodentate ligands increase CN by 1, bidentate by 2, etc. Chelating ligands give extra stability (chelate effect) due to entropy gain.

Coordination number, geometry and oxidation state of central metal ion

Definitions

  • Coordination number (CN): total number of donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal ion.
  • Oxidation state (OS): formal charge on the metal ion calculated as $OS = \text{charge of complex} - \sum \text{ligand charges}$.
  • Geometry: spatial arrangement of ligands around the metal, dictated by CN.

Key Relations

CNCommon Geometry
$2$Linear
$4$Tetrahedral / Square planar
$6$Octahedral
$8$Square antiprismatic / Dodecahedral

All CN and OS are dimensionless (no SI unit). Bond lengths are expressed in Å ($10^{-10}\,\text{m}$).

Worked Example

Find CN, geometry and OS of the metal ion in $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]\text{Cl}\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

  • Identify donor atoms: $4$ NH$_3$ (each N donates 1) + $2$ Cl$^-$ (each Cl donates 1) → $CN = 4+2 = 6$.
  • Geometry for $CN=6$ is octahedral.
  • Complex charge: inner sphere $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^+$ (overall +1). Outer‑sphere Cl$^-$ and H$_2$O are not counted.
  • Ligand charges inside sphere: NH$_3$ (neutral), Cl$^-$ (–1 each) → total ligand charge = $-2$.
  • Using $OS = (+1) - (-2) = +3$, so cobalt is in $+3$ oxidation state.

Thus, $CN=6$, geometry = octahedral, $OS_{\text{Co}}=+3$.

Bonding in coordination compounds – Valence Bond Theory and Crystal Field Theory

Definition: A coordination compound consists of a central metal ion/atom bonded to one or more ligands through coordinate covalent bonds.

Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

  • Hybridisation of metal ion determines geometry: $sp^3$ (tetrahedral), $d^2sp^3$ (octahedral), $sd^2$ (square planar).
  • Number of unpaired electrons $n$ gives magnetic moment $\\mu = \\sqrt{n(n+2)}\\ \\text{BM}$.

Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

  • Octahedral field splits $d$ orbitals into lower $t_{2g}$ and higher $e_g$ with energy gap $\\Delta_o$ (kJ·mol⁻¹).
  • CFSE (octahedral) $= (-0.4\\Delta_o) \times n_{t_{2g}} + (0.6\\Delta_o) \times n_{e_g}$.
  • Tetrahedral splitting $\\Delta_t \approx 0.4\\Delta_o$.
ComplexHybridisationGeometry
$\\text{[Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}$$sp^2d$Square planar
$\\text{[CoF}_6]^{3-}$$d^2sp^3$Octahedral

Solved Example

Find the CFSE and magnetic moment of $\\text{[Fe(H}_2\\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$ (high‑spin $d^6$ octahedral).

  • Electron configuration: $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$ → $n_{t_{2g}}=4$, $n_{e_g}=2$.
  • CFSE $= (-0.4\\Delta_o)\times4 + (0.6\\Delta_o)\times2 = -1.6\\Delta_o + 1.2\\Delta_o = -0.4\\Delta_o$.
  • Number of unpaired electrons $n=4$ → $\\mu = \\sqrt{4(4+2)} = \\sqrt{24}=4.90\\ \text{BM}$.

Isomerism in coordination compounds – structural and stereoisomerism

Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

Isomerism refers to the existence of two or more compounds having the same empirical formula but different arrangement of atoms or ligands. In coordination chemistry it is classified into structural (constitutional) isomerism and stereoisomerism.

  • Structural isomers
    • Ionisation isomerism: exchange of an anionic ligand with the counter‑ion (e.g., $[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br$ ↔ $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$).
    • Hydrate isomerism: water molecules inside the coordination sphere vs. outside (e.g., $[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3$, $[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2\cdot H_2O$, $[Cr(H_2O)_4Cl_2]Cl\cdot2H_2O$).
    • Coordination isomerism: interchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complexes (e.g., $[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]$ ↔ $[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$).
    • Linkage isomerism: same ligand binds through different donor atoms (e.g., $[Co(NH_3)_5NO_2]^{2+}$ vs. $[Co(NH_3)_5ONO]^{2+}$).
  • Stereoisomers
    • Geometrical (cis‑trans, fac‑mer): different spatial arrangement of ligands in complexes with fixed geometry (e.g., $[MA_2B_2C]$ octahedral gives cis and trans).
    • Optical isomerism: non‑superimposable mirror images (enantiomers) arise when the complex lacks an internal plane of symmetry; common in octahedral $[M(AA)_3]$ and tetrahedral $[M(AB)_2]$.

Key formula: For an octahedral complex $[MA_2B_2C]$, the number of geometrical isomers $=2$ (cis and trans).

Solved Example:

Find the geometrical isomers of $[Co(NH_3)_2Cl_2(NO_2)]$ (octahedral).

  1. Identify the set of ligands: two $NH_3$, two $Cl^-$ and one $NO_2^-$.
  2. Place the unique $NO_2^-$ at any position (reference). The remaining four positions are occupied by two $NH_3$ and two $Cl^-$.
  3. Two distinct arrangements are possible:
    • cis: $NH_3$ ligands adjacent → $Cl^-$ ligands also adjacent.
    • trans: $NH_3$ ligands opposite → $Cl^-$ ligands opposite.
  4. Thus, $[Co(NH_3)_2Cl_2(NO_2)]$ exhibits two geometrical isomers (cis and trans). No optical isomerism because the complex possesses a plane of symmetry.

Remember: Isomerism influences colour, magnetic behaviour and biological activity, making it a high‑yield topic for CBSE exams.

Stability constants, chelate effect and kinetic aspects

Stability Constant (Formation Constant) – For the equilibrium $M + nL \\rightleftharpoons ML_n$, the overall formation constant is $\\beta_n = \\frac{[ML_n]}{[M][L]^n}$ (dimensionless, expressed in $\\text{M}^{-n}$).

Stepwise Constants – $K_1 = \\frac{[ML]}{[M][L]}$, $K_2 = \\frac{[ML_2]}{[ML][L]}$, …, $\\beta_n = \\prod_{i=1}^{n} K_i$.

  • Chelate Effect: Multidentate ligands form rings; each additional bond releases a solvent molecule, increasing entropy. Hence $\\beta_{chelate} \\gg \\beta_{monodentate}$ for the same donor atoms.
  • Ring‑size trend: 5‑ and 6‑membered rings give maximum stability.

Kinetic Aspects

  • Complexes are classified as labile (fast substitution, $t_{1/2} \\sim 10^{-4}\\,\\text{s}$) or inert (slow, $t_{1/2} \\sim 10^{5}\\,\\text{s}$).
  • Rate law for a first‑order substitution: $\\frac{d[ML]}{dt} = -k[ML]$, $k = A\\,e^{-E_a/RT}$.
  • Activation parameters: $\\Delta G^{\\ddagger}= -RT\\ln\\frac{k h}{k_B T}$.
QuantityExpressionUnit
$\\beta_n$$\\frac{[ML_n]}{[M][L]^n}$dimensionless (M$^{-n}$)
$K_i$$\\frac{[ML_i]}{[ML_{i-1}][L]}$dimensionless (M$^{-1}$)
$k$$A\\,e^{-E_a/RT}$s$^{-1}$ (first order)

Solved Example

Given $K_1 = 1.0\\times10^{3}$, $K_2 = 1.0\\times10^{2}$, $K_3 = 1.0\\times10^{1}$, find $\\beta_3$.

Solution: $\\beta_3 = K_1 K_2 K_3 = (1.0\\times10^{3})(1.0\\times10^{2})(1.0\\times10^{1}) = 1.0\\times10^{6}$. The overall formation constant is $10^{6}$, indicating a very stable $ML_3$ complex.

Nomenclature of coordination compounds (IUPAC rules)

Definition: A coordination compound consists of a central metal atom/ion surrounded by ligands forming coordinate covalent bonds.

Key IUPAC Rules (summary)

  • Ligands are named first in alphabetical order (ignoring prefixes such as di-, tri-). Anionic ligands end in “‑ate”; neutral ligands retain their name (e.g., $\text{NH}_3$ → ammine, $\text{H}_2$O → aqua).
  • Number of identical ligands is indicated by prefixes: mono‑, di‑, tri‑, tetra‑, penta‑, hexa‑, etc.
  • The metal name follows the ligands; its oxidation state is given in Roman numerals in parentheses.
  • For anionic complexes the metal name ends with “‑ate” and the oxidation state is increased by one (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ → ferrate).
  • If the complex is neutral, the metal name is unchanged; if the complex is an anion, the metal name is modified as above.

Useful formulas

QuantityExpression
Oxidation state of metal ($x$)$x = \text{overall charge} - \sum \text{(charge of ligands)}$
Coordination number (CN)$\text{CN}=\sum \text{(donor atoms contributed by each ligand)}$

Example (solved)

Find the name of $\left[\text{Co(NH}_3\text{)}_5\text{Cl}\right]\text{Cl}_2$.

  1. Identify ligands: $\text{ammine}$ (5) and $\text{chloro}$ (1).
  2. Arrange alphabetically: $\text{ammine}$ before $\text{chloro}$.
  3. Write ligand part: $\text{pentaamminechlorido}$.
  4. Metal centre: cobalt; overall charge of complex ion = $+1$ (since two external $\text{Cl}^-$ give net $+1$). Hence oxidation state of Co = $+3$.
  5. Since the complex is a cation, metal name remains “cobalt”.
  6. Final name: pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride.

Applications and significance of coordination compounds in industry and biology

Definition: A coordination compound consists of a central metal ion/atom bonded to one or more ligands through coordinate covalent bonds.

Key parameters

  • Stability (formation) constant: $K_f = \frac{[ML_n]}{[M][L]^n}$ (dimensionless)
  • Effective ionic radius, crystal field splitting energy $\Delta_{oct}$ (kJ·mol$^{-1}$)

Industrial applications

  • Catalysis: $[RhCl(PPh_3)_3]$ in hydrogenation; $[PtCl_2(NH_3)_2]$ (Zeise’s salt) in polymerisation.
  • Electroplating: $[Cu(CN)_4]^{3-}$ for copper plating; $[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}$ for nickel.
  • Analytical chemistry: EDTA titration; $[Fe(CN)_6]^{3-}$ as redox indicator.
  • Medicinal: $[Co(NH_3)_6]^{3+}$ (cobalt‑based vitamin B12); $[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]$ (cisplatin) anticancer drug.

Biological significance

  • Hemoglobin: $[Fe^{2+}(O_2)_4]$ transports O$_2$.
  • Cytochrome oxidase: contains $[Cu]$ and $[Fe]$ centers for electron transfer.
  • Vitamin B12: $[Co(CN)C_{12}H_{12}N_5O_3]$ essential for DNA synthesis.

Solved example

ProblemSolution
Calculate $K_f$ for $[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}$ if at equilibrium $[Cu^{2+}] = 2.0\times10^{-4}\,\text{M}$, $[NH_3] = 0.010\,\text{M}$ and $[Cu(NH_3)_4^{2+}] = 1.6\times10^{-3}\,\text{M}$.$K_f = \frac{[Cu(NH_3)_4^{2+}]}{[Cu^{2+}][NH_3]^4}=\frac{1.6\times10^{-3}}{(2.0\times10^{-4})(0.010)^4}=8.0\times10^{9}$ (dimensionless).
