Mathematical Notations & Formulas

Before solving numericals, make sure you understand every symbol and its standard unit:

i (Van 't Hoff Factor)
$i = \frac{\text{Observed Colligative Property}}{\text{Calculated Colligative Property}} = \frac{\text{Normal Molar Mass}}{\text{Abnormal Molar Mass}}$
Kb (Ebullioscopic Constant)
Molal elevation constant. Elevation in boiling point for a 1 molal solution. Unit: $\text{K kg mol}^{-1}$.
Kf (Cryoscopic Constant)
Molal depression constant. Depression in freezing point for a 1 molal solution. Unit: $\text{K kg mol}^{-1}$.
KH (Henry's Law Constant)
$p = K_H \cdot x$. Higher $K_H$ at a given temperature implies lower gas solubility in liquid.
Π (Osmotic Pressure)
$\Pi = iCRT = i\left(\frac{w_B}{M_B \cdot V}\right)RT$. Measured in $\text{atm}$ or $\text{bar}$.
wB & MB
$w_B$ = Given mass of solute ($\text{g}$), $M_B$ = Molar mass of solute ($\text{g mol}^{-1}$).
wA & MA
$w_A$ = Given mass of solvent ($\text{g}$), $M_A$ = Molar mass of solvent ($\text{g mol}^{-1}$).
α (Degree of Dissociation / Association)
Dissociation: $\alpha = \frac{i-1}{n-1}$. Association: $\alpha = \frac{1-i}{1 - 1/n}$.
topic one

Module 1: Concentration Units & Basic Concepts

CBSE 2023, Set 21 Mark (MCQ)
Q1. Which of the following concentration terms is temperature-dependent? Give the scientific reason.

(A) Molality      (B) Mole fraction      (C) Molarity      (D) Mass percentage

Solution (Marking Scheme)

Correct Option: (C) Molarity

Reason: Molarity ($M = \frac{n}{V}$) is defined as moles of solute per litre of solution. Because volume expands or contracts with changes in temperature ($V \propto T$), molarity changes with temperature. Molality, mole fraction, and mass percentage depend purely on masses, which remain independent of temperature.

CBSE 2020, Delhi2 Marks
Q2. Define "Parts Per Million" (ppm). When is this concentration unit preferred in chemical calculations?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Definition: Parts per million (ppm) is defined as the parts of a solute component per million ($10^6$) parts of the total solution by mass:

$$\text{ppm} = \frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Total mass of solution}} \times 10^6$$

Application: It is preferred when a solute is present in extremely minute trace quantities, such as quantifying atmospheric pollutants ($\text{SO}_2, \text{NO}_x$), dissolved chlorine in drinking water, or heavy metal toxicities.

CBSE 2018, All India3 Marks
Q3. Calculate the molarity and molality of a $20\%\ (w/w)$ aqueous solution of potassium iodide ($\text{KI}$). The density of the solution is $1.202\text{ g mL}^{-1}$. (Molar mass of $\text{KI} = 166\text{ g mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Step 1: Determine masses: $20\%\ (w/w)$ means $20\text{ g}$ of $\text{KI}$ is dissolved in $(100 - 20) = 80\text{ g}$ of water ($\text{solvent}$). Moles of $\text{KI}$ ($n_{\text{KI}}$) = $\frac{20}{166} = 0.1205\text{ mol}$.

Step 2: Calculate Molality ($m$):

$$m = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{\text{Mass of solvent (kg)}} = \frac{0.1205\text{ mol}}{0.080\text{ kg}} = \mathbf{1.51\text{ mol kg}}^{-1}$$

Step 3: Calculate Molarity ($M$): Volume = $\frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Density}} = \frac{100}{1.202} = 83.19\text{ mL} = 0.08319\text{ L}$.

$$M = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{V\text{ (L)}} = \frac{0.1205}{0.08319} = \mathbf{1.45\text{ M (mol L}}^{-1}\text{)}$$
CBSE 2016, Foreign2 Marks
Q4. Calculate the mole fraction of ethylene glycol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}_2$) in an aqueous solution containing $20\%$ of $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}_2$ by mass.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Molar mass: $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}_2 = 62\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $\text{H}_2\text{O} = 18\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
In $100\text{ g}$ solution: $w_{\text{glycol}} = 20\text{ g}$, $w_{\text{water}} = 80\text{ g}$.
$n_{\text{glycol}} = \frac{20}{62} = 0.322\text{ mol}$,    $n_{\text{water}} = \frac{80}{18} = 4.444\text{ mol}$.

$$x_{\text{glycol}} = \frac{0.322}{0.322 + 4.444} = \frac{0.322}{4.766} = \mathbf{0.0676}$$
topic two

Module 2: Henry's Law & Gas Solubility

CBSE 2024, Set 32 Marks
Q5. State Henry's Law. How does the solubility of gases in liquids vary with: (i) increase in temperature, and (ii) increase in pressure?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Henry's Law: At constant temperature, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid surface: $p = K_H \cdot x$.

(i) Temperature: Gas solubility decreases as temperature rises because dissolution is exothermic ($\Delta_{\text{sol}}H < 0$), shifting equilibrium backward (Le Chatelier's Principle).

(ii) Pressure: Gas solubility increases with increasing pressure because compression increases the collision rate of gas molecules with the liquid surface.

CBSE 2023, Set 11 Mark
Q6. The values of Henry's constant ($K_H$) for two gases $A$ and $B$ in water at $298\text{ K}$ are $40.3\text{ kbar}$ and $1.67\text{ kbar}$ respectively. Which gas is more soluble in water at the same partial pressure?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Answer: Gas B is more soluble.

Reason: From Henry's law, $x = \frac{p}{K_H}$. Solubility ($x$) is inversely proportional to $K_H$. Because gas $B$ has a significantly lower $K_H$ value ($1.67\text{ kbar} < 40.3\text{ kbar}$), it dissolves to a much greater extent.

CBSE 2020, Delhi2 Marks
Q7. Why do aquatic species feel more comfortable in cold water than in warm water?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Dissolution of oxygen in water is exothermic ($\Delta_{\text{sol}}H < 0$). As temperature decreases, Henry's constant ($K_H$) decreases, resulting in higher solubility of oxygen in cold water. Cold water therefore provides a significantly higher dissolved oxygen concentration, facilitating easier respiration for aquatic life.

CBSE 2019, All India2 Marks
Q8. State the medical condition "Bends" experienced by scuba divers. How is it prevented?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Cause: High pressure deep underwater forces large amounts of atmospheric nitrogen to dissolve in a diver's blood. When the diver surfaces rapidly, the pressure drops quickly, releasing dissolved nitrogen as gas bubbles in the bloodstream that block blood capillaries and pinch nerves.

Symptoms: Extreme pain, cramping, and risk of paralysis known as the bends.

Prevention: Breathing tanks are filled with air diluted with helium ($11.7\%\text{ He}, 56.2\%\text{ N}_2, 32.1\%\text{ O}_2$), as helium has exceptionally low solubility in blood.

CBSE 2017, Delhi2 Marks
Q9. Explain why mountain climbers suffer from "Anoxia" at high altitudes.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

At high altitudes, atmospheric pressure is substantially lower than at ground level. According to Henry's Law, the partial pressure of oxygen drops, reducing the concentration of oxygen dissolved in the climber's blood and tissues. This deficiency leads to muscular fatigue and cognitive confusion, termed Anoxia.

CBSE 2015, All India3 Marks
Q10. If $\text{N}_2$ gas is bubbled through water at $293\text{ K}$, how many millimoles of $\text{N}_2$ gas would dissolve in $1\text{ litre}$ of water? Assume $\text{N}_2$ exerts a partial pressure of $0.987\text{ bar}$. ($K_H \text{ for } \text{N}_2 = 76.48\text{ kbar}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)
$$x_{\text{N}_2} = \frac{p_{\text{N}_2}}{K_H} = \frac{0.987\text{ bar}}{76,480\text{ bar}} = 1.29 \times 10^{-5}$$

In $1\text{ L}$ of water ($1000\text{ g}$): $n_{\text{water}} = \frac{1000}{18} = 55.55\text{ mol}$. Since $n_{\text{N}_2} \ll n_{\text{water}}$:

$$n_{\text{N}_2} = 1.29 \times 10^{-5} \times 55.55 = 7.16 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol} = \mathbf{0.716\text{ millimoles}}$$
topic three

Module 3: Raoult's Law, Ideal Solutions & Azeotropes

CBSE 2024, Set 22 Marks
Q11. Explain how Raoult's Law can be viewed as a special case of Henry's Law.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Raoult's Law: $p_i = p_i^\circ \cdot x_i$.    Henry's Law: $p_i = K_H \cdot x_i$.

Both equations state that the partial vapour pressure of a component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in solution. They differ only in the proportionality constant: $p_i^\circ$ in Raoult's law and $K_H$ in Henry's law. When $K_H = p_i^\circ$, Henry's law becomes identical to Raoult's law.

CBSE 2023, Set 33 Marks
Q12. What type of deviation from Raoult's law is shown by an ethanol and acetone mixture? Explain in terms of intermolecular forces.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Deviation: Positive Deviation ($\Delta_{\text{mix}}H > 0$, $\Delta_{\text{mix}}V > 0$).

Explanation: Pure ethanol molecules are held together by strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds. When acetone is added, its molecules insert themselves between ethanol molecules, breaking existing hydrogen bonds. Because the new ethanol-acetone attractive forces ($A-B$) are weaker than the original ethanol-ethanol forces ($A-A$), molecules escape more readily into vapour, resulting in higher total vapour pressure.

CBSE 2022, Term-11 Mark
Q13. Which of the following liquid pairs forms an ideal solution?

(A) $\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3$      (B) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3$
(C) $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$      (D) $\text{HNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Solution (Marking Scheme)

Correct Option: (B) Benzene + Toluene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3$)

Reason: Both are non-polar hydrocarbons of similar molecular geometry and polarity; hence $A-B$ intermolecular forces match $A-A$ and $B-B$ forces ($\Delta H_{\text{mix}} = 0, \Delta V_{\text{mix}} = 0$).

CBSE 2020, All India3 Marks
Q14. (a) Why is the vapour pressure of an aqueous glucose solution lower than that of pure water?
(b) What are azeotropes? Can they be separated by fractional distillation?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

(a) Glucose is a non-volatile solute. In solution, glucose molecules occupy part of the liquid surface area, reducing the fraction of surface occupied by volatile water molecules. This lowers the evaporation rate and decreases vapour pressure.

(b) Azeotropes are binary liquid mixtures that boil at a constant temperature with identical compositions in both liquid and vapour phases. They cannot be separated by fractional distillation because both components boil off simultaneously in the same proportion.

CBSE 2019, Delhi3 Marks
Q15. Give one example each of a minimum boiling azeotrope and a maximum boiling azeotrope, comparing their boiling points to the pure components.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

1. Minimum Boiling Azeotrope: $95.4\%$ Ethanol and $4.6\%$ Water (by volume). Exhibits large positive deviation from Raoult's law. It boils at $351.15\text{ K}$, lower than pure ethanol ($351.5\text{ K}$) and pure water ($373.15\text{ K}$).

2. Maximum Boiling Azeotrope: $68\%$ Nitric Acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) and $32\%$ Water (by mass). Exhibits large negative deviation. It boils at $393.5\text{ K}$, higher than pure water ($373.15\text{ K}$) and pure $\text{HNO}_3$ ($359\text{ K}$).

CBSE 2018, Delhi2 Marks
Q16. A mixture of phenol and aniline exhibits negative deviation from Raoult's law. Justify this behavior based on intermolecular forces.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Phenol and aniline form intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the phenolic hydrogen atom and the lone pair on the aniline nitrogen atom ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{O}-\text{H}\cdots:\text{NH}_2-\text{C}_6\text{H}_5$). Because these newly formed solute-solvent attractions ($A-B$) are stronger than the pure solvent-solvent ($A-A$) and solute-solute ($B-B$) attractions, molecules escape less readily into the vapour phase, reducing total vapour pressure.

CBSE 2017, All India3 Marks
Q17. At $300\text{ K}$, $30\text{ g}$ of glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) is dissolved in $500\text{ g}$ of water. If the vapour pressure of pure water at $300\text{ K}$ is $31.82\text{ mm Hg}$, calculate the vapour pressure of this solution.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$n_{\text{glucose}} = \frac{30}{180} = 0.1667\text{ mol}$,    $n_{\text{water}} = \frac{500}{18} = 27.778\text{ mol}$.

$$\frac{p^\circ - p}{p^\circ} = \frac{n_{\text{glucose}}}{n_{\text{water}} + n_{\text{glucose}}} = \frac{0.1667}{27.778 + 0.1667} = 0.005965$$
$$31.82 - p = 31.82 \times 0.005965 = 0.19\text{ mm Hg} \implies p = 31.82 - 0.19 = \mathbf{31.63\text{ mm Hg}}$$
CBSE 2014, Delhi3 Marks
Q18. Vapour pressures of chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$) and dichloromethane ($\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$) at $298\text{ K}$ are $200\text{ mm Hg}$ and $415\text{ mm Hg}$ respectively. Calculate the total vapour pressure of a mixture of $25.5\text{ g}$ of $\text{CHCl}_3$ and $40.0\text{ g}$ of $\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$n_{\text{CHCl}_3} = \frac{25.5}{119.5} = 0.213\text{ mol}$,    $n_{\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2} = \frac{40.0}{85.0} = 0.471\text{ mol}$. Total moles = $0.684\text{ mol}$.
$x_{\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2} = \frac{0.471}{0.684} = 0.688$,    $x_{\text{CHCl}_3} = 1 - 0.688 = 0.312$.

$$P_{\text{total}} = p_1^\circ x_1 + p_2^\circ x_2 = (415 \times 0.688) + (200 \times 0.312) = 285.52 + 62.40 = \mathbf{347.92\text{ mm Hg}}$$
topic four

Module 4: Colligative Properties & Applications

CBSE 2024, Set 11 Mark
Q19. Why does food cook faster in a pressure cooker, especially on high mountains?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

On high mountains, reduced atmospheric pressure lowers the normal boiling point of water ($< 100^\circ\text{C}$), slowing down cooking. A pressure cooker raises internal pressure above $1\text{ atm}$, elevating the boiling point of water ($> 120^\circ\text{C}$) and cooking food much faster.

CBSE 2023, Set 22 Marks
Q20. Define "Molal Elevation Constant" (Ebullioscopic Constant, $K_b$). State its SI unit.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Definition: $K_b$ is the elevation in boiling point produced when 1 mole of a non-volatile, non-electrolyte solute is dissolved in $1\text{ kg}$ ($1000\text{ g}$) of a solvent (i.e., a $1\text{ molal}$ solution).

SI Unit: $\mathbf{\text{K kg mol}}^{-1}$ (or $^\circ\text{C kg mol}^{-1}$).

CBSE 2022, Term-23 Marks
Q21. Calculate the mass of ascorbic acid (Vitamin C, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_8\text{O}_6$, molar mass $= 176\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) that must be dissolved in $75\text{ g}$ of acetic acid to lower its freezing point by $1.5^\circ\text{C}$. ($K_f = 3.9\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)
$$\Delta T_f = \frac{K_f \times w_B \times 1000}{M_B \times w_A} \implies 1.5 = \frac{3.9 \times w_B \times 1000}{176 \times 75}$$
$$w_B = \frac{1.5 \times 176 \times 75}{3.9 \times 1000} = \frac{19800}{3900} = \mathbf{5.08\text{ g}}$$
CBSE 2020, Delhi2 Marks
Q22. Why are rock salt ($\text{NaCl}$) or calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$) spread on snow-covered roads in cold climates?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Salt dissolves into the thin surface liquid film on ice, creating an electrolytic solution that depresses the freezing point of water well below $0^\circ\text{C}$ (down to $-15^\circ\text{C}$ for $\text{NaCl}$ and $-30^\circ\text{C}$ for $\text{CaCl}_2$). At typical winter temperatures, snow melts into liquid solution and drains away, clearing the road.

CBSE 2019, All India2 Marks
Q23. Explain: (i) Why meat is salted and fruits are preserved in thick sugar syrup, and (ii) Why individuals taking excess salt suffer from Edema.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

(i) Food Preservation: Concentrated brine and sugar syrups are hypertonic to bacterial and fungal cells. Water is drawn out of microbes via exosmosis (plasmolysis), dehydrating and killing them.

(ii) Edema: Excess sodium elevates osmotic pressure in extracellular tissue fluids, drawing water out of blood capillaries into surrounding tissue spaces via osmosis, causing physical swelling (edema).

CBSE 2018, Foreign3 Marks
Q24. A $5\%$ solution ($w/w$) of cane sugar ($M = 342\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) in water has a freezing point of $271\text{ K}$. Calculate the freezing point of a $5\%$ solution ($w/w$) of glucose ($M = 180\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) in water. Pure water freezes at $273.15\text{ K}$.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$\Delta T_{f,\text{sugar}} = 273.15 - 271.00 = 2.15\text{ K}$.
$2.15 = K_f \times \frac{5 \times 1000}{342 \times 95} \implies K_f = \frac{2.15 \times 342 \times 95}{5000} = 13.97\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}$.

For $5\%$ Glucose solution:
$\Delta T_{f,\text{glucose}} = 13.97 \times \frac{5 \times 1000}{180 \times 95} = 4.08\text{ K}$.
$T_f = 273.15 - 4.08 = \mathbf{269.07\text{ K}}$.

CBSE 2017, Delhi2 Marks
Q25. Explain the operational principle of Reverse Osmosis (RO) used in water purification and seawater desalination.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

When an external hydrostatic pressure greater than the osmotic pressure ($P > \Pi$) is applied to the saline solution side, pure water molecules are forced backward through a semipermeable membrane (such as cellulose acetate) into the pure water reservoir, leaving dissolved salts and impurities behind.

CBSE 2016, All India3 Marks
Q26. $200\text{ cm}^3$ of an aqueous protein solution contains $1.26\text{ g}$ of protein. The osmotic pressure at $300\text{ K}$ is $2.57 \times 10^{-3}\text{ bar}$. Calculate the molar mass of the protein. ($R = 0.083\text{ L bar K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)
$$M_B = \frac{w_B \cdot R \cdot T}{\Pi \cdot V} = \frac{1.26 \times 0.083 \times 300}{(2.57 \times 10^{-3}) \times 0.200} = \frac{31.374}{0.000514} \approx \mathbf{61,039\text{ g mol}}^{-1}$$
topic five

Module 5: Van 't Hoff Factor, Abnormal Molar Mass & Association/Dissociation

CBSE 2024, Set 21 Mark
Q27. What is the value of the van 't Hoff factor ($i$) for a dilute aqueous solution of barium chloride ($\text{BaCl}_2$), assuming complete dissociation?
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$\text{BaCl}_2 \rightarrow \text{Ba}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^-$. Since 1 formula unit produces $1 + 2 = 3$ ions in solution, $\mathbf{i = 3}$.

CBSE 2023, Set 33 Marks
Q28. A $0.01\text{ m}$ aqueous solution of $\text{AlCl}_3$ freezes at $-0.068^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate the percentage dissociation of $\text{AlCl}_3$. ($K_f \text{ for water} = 1.86\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$\Delta T_{f,\text{obs}} = 0 - (-0.068) = 0.068\text{ K}$.
$\Delta T_{f,\text{calc}} = K_f \times m = 1.86 \times 0.01 = 0.0186\text{ K}$.

$$i = \frac{0.068}{0.0186} = 3.656$$

For $\text{AlCl}_3 \rightarrow \text{Al}^{3+} + 3\text{Cl}^-$ ($n = 4$):

$$\alpha = \frac{i - 1}{n - 1} = \frac{3.656 - 1}{4 - 1} = \frac{2.656}{3} = 0.8853 \implies \mathbf{88.53\%}$$
CBSE 2020, Set 11 Mark
Q29. Arrange the following $0.1\text{ M}$ aqueous solutions in increasing order of boiling point: $0.1\text{ M}\ \text{Glucose}, \ 0.1\text{ M}\ \text{NaCl}, \ 0.1\text{ M}\ \text{BaCl}_2, \ 0.1\text{ M}\ \text{AlCl}_3$.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

Elevation in boiling point depends on effective particle count ($i \times C$):
Glucose ($i=1, iC=0.1$) < $\text{NaCl}$ ($i=2, iC=0.2$) < $\text{BaCl}_2$ ($i=3, iC=0.3$) < $\text{AlCl}_3$ ($i=4, iC=0.4$).

Order: $0.1\text{ M Glucose} < 0.1\text{ M NaCl} < 0.1\text{ M BaCl}_2 < 0.1\text{ M AlCl}_3$

CBSE 2019, Compartment3 Marks
Q30. $2.0\text{ g}$ of benzoic acid ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COOH}$) in $25.0\text{ g}$ benzene lowers freezing point by $1.62\text{ K}$. ($K_f = 4.9\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}$, $M = 122\text{ g mol}^{-1}$). Calculate percentage association if it forms a dimer.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$m = \frac{2.0 \times 1000}{122 \times 25.0} = 0.6557\text{ mol kg}^{-1}$.
$\Delta T_{f,\text{calc}} = 4.9 \times 0.6557 = 3.213\text{ K}$.
$i = \frac{1.62}{3.213} = 0.5042$.

$$\alpha = \frac{1 - i}{1 - \frac{1}{n}} = \frac{1 - 0.5042}{1 - 0.5} = \frac{0.4958}{0.5} = 0.9916 \implies \mathbf{99.16\%}$$
CBSE 2018, All India3 Marks
Q31. Calculate boiling point elevation when $10.0\text{ g}$ of $\text{CaCl}_2$ is dissolved in $200\text{ g}$ water, assuming complete dissociation. ($K_b = 0.52\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}, M_{\text{CaCl}_2} = 111\text{ g mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)

For complete dissociation of $\text{CaCl}_2 \rightarrow \text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^-$, $i = 3$.
$m = \frac{10.0 \times 1000}{111 \times 200} = 0.4505\text{ mol kg}^{-1}$.

$$\Delta T_b = i \times K_b \times m = 3 \times 0.52 \times 0.4505 = \mathbf{0.703\text{ K (or }^\circ\text{C)}}$$
CBSE 2017, Delhi3 Marks
Q32. Calculate the freezing point of an aqueous solution containing $10.50\text{ g}$ of $\text{MgBr}_2$ in $200\text{ g}$ of water, assuming complete dissociation. ($M_{\text{MgBr}_2} = 184\text{ g mol}^{-1}, K_f = 1.86\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$\text{MgBr}_2 \rightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+} + 2\text{Br}^- \implies i = 3$.
$m = \frac{10.50 \times 1000}{184 \times 200} = 0.2853\text{ mol kg}^{-1}$.

$$\Delta T_f = i \times K_f \times m = 3 \times 1.86 \times 0.2853 = 1.592\text{ K}$$
$$T_f = 273.15\text{ K} - 1.592\text{ K} = \mathbf{271.56\text{ K}} \quad (\text{or } \mathbf{-1.59^\circ\text{C}})$$
CBSE 2016, All India3 Marks
Q33. $19.5\text{ g}$ of fluoroacetic acid ($\text{CH}_2\text{FCOOH}$, molar mass $= 78\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) is dissolved in $500\text{ g}$ of water. The depression in the freezing point of water observed is $1.0^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate the van 't Hoff factor and the dissociation constant of fluoroacetic acid. ($K_f = 1.86\text{ K kg mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)

$m = \frac{19.5 \times 1000}{78 \times 500} = 0.50\text{ mol kg}^{-1}$.
$\Delta T_{f,\text{calc}} = K_f \times m = 1.86 \times 0.50 = 0.93\text{ K}$.

$$i = \frac{\Delta T_{f,\text{obs}}}{\Delta T_{f,\text{calc}}} = \frac{1.0}{0.93} = \mathbf{1.0753}$$

For $\text{CH}_2\text{FCOOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_2\text{FCOO}^- + \text{H}^+$ ($n = 2$):
$\alpha = \frac{i - 1}{n - 1} = \frac{1.0753 - 1}{2 - 1} = \mathbf{0.0753}$.

$$K_a = \frac{C \alpha^2}{1 - \alpha} = \frac{0.50 \times (0.0753)^2}{1 - 0.0753} = \frac{0.50 \times 0.00567}{0.9247} = \mathbf{3.07 \times 10^{-3}}$$
CBSE 2015, All India / 2014, Delhi3 Marks
Q34. Determine the mass of $\text{CaCl}_2$ ($M = 111\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $i = 2.47$) that must be dissolved in $2.5\text{ L}$ of water so that its osmotic pressure is $0.75\text{ atm}$ at $27^\circ\text{C}$. ($R = 0.0821\text{ L atm K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$).
Solution (Marking Scheme)
$$\Pi = i \left(\frac{w_B}{M_B \cdot V}\right) R \cdot T \implies 0.75 = 2.47 \times \left(\frac{w_B}{111 \times 2.5}\right) \times 0.0821 \times 300$$
$$0.75 = \frac{w_B \times 60.836}{277.5} \implies w_B = \frac{0.75 \times 277.5}{60.836} = \mathbf{3.42\text{ g}}$$
CBSE 2014, All India2 Marks
Q35. What physical behavior does the van 't Hoff factor ($i$) represent when: (i) $i = 1$, (ii) $i > 1$, and (iii) $i < 1$? Give one example for each case.
Solution (Marking Scheme)

(i) $i = 1$: Normal behavior. Solute undergoes neither dissociation nor association in the solvent (e.g., Glucose, Urea, Sucrose in water).

(ii) $i > 1$: Dissociation. Solute breaks apart into two or more ions, increasing total particle concentration (e.g., $\text{NaCl}, \text{CaCl}_2, \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$ in water).

(iii) $i < 1$: Association. Solute molecules assemble into dimers or multimers, decreasing total particle count (e.g., Benzoic acid or Ethanoic acid in benzene).
