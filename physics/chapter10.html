Interference of Light

Definition: Interference of light is the superposition of two or more coherent wave‑fronts resulting in a resultant intensity distribution.

Conditions for stable interference

  • Sources must be coherent (constant phase difference).
  • Same wavelength (monochromatic).
  • Constant path difference.

Path difference ($\\Delta$) for two slits separated by $d$ and observed at angle $\\theta$:

$\\Delta = d\\sin\\theta$

Interference conditions

  • Constructive: $\\Delta = m\\lambda \;(m=0,1,2,\\dots)$
  • Destructive: $\\Delta = \left(m+\\frac12\right)\\lambda$

For small $\\theta$, $\\sin\\theta \approx \\tan\\theta = \frac{y}{D}$ where $y$ is the fringe position on a screen at distance $D$.

Hence, bright fringe position: $y_m = \frac{m\\lambda D}{d}$ and fringe width $\\beta = \frac{\\lambda D}{d}$.

QuantityFormulaSI Unit
Path difference $\\Delta$$d\\sin\\theta$m
Fringe width $\\beta$$\frac{\\lambda D}{d}$m
Bright fringe position $y_m$$\frac{m\\lambda D}{d}$m

Example

Double‑slit separation $d = 0.20\,\text{mm}$, screen distance $D = 2.0\,\text{m}$, wavelength $\\lambda = 600\,\text{nm}$. Find (i) fringe width $\\beta$, (ii) position of the 4th bright fringe ($m=4$).

Solution:

$\\beta = \frac{\\lambda D}{d}=\frac{600\times10^{-9}\times2}{0.20\times10^{-3}}=6\times10^{-3}\,\text{m}=6\,\text{mm}$.

$y_4 = \frac{4\\lambda D}{d}=4\\beta = 24\,\text{mm}$.

Thus the fringes are $6\,\text{mm}$ apart and the 4th bright fringe lies $24\,\text{mm}$ from the central maximum.

Young’s Double Slit Experiment

Definition: Young’s Double‑slit Experiment (YDSE) demonstrates the wave nature of light by producing a stationary interference pattern on a screen placed at a distance $D$ from two narrow, coherent slits separated by distance $d$.

  • Condition for bright fringe (constructive interference): $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$, $m=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots$
  • Condition for dark fringe (destructive interference): $d\sin\theta = \left(m+\tfrac12\right)\lambda$

For small angles $\sin\theta\approx\tan\theta = y/D$, where $y$ is the linear distance of the $m^{\text{th}}$ fringe from the central maximum. Hence

$$y = \frac{m\lambda D}{d}$$

The separation between two successive bright (or dark) fringes is the fringe width

$$\beta = \frac{\lambda D}{d}$$

SymbolMeaningSI Unit
$\lambda$Wavelength of lightm
$d$Separation between slitsm
$D$Distance screen‑slitm
$y$Fringe positionm
$\beta$Fringe widthm

Example: In a YDSE, $d = 0.20\;\text{mm}$, $D = 2.0\;\text{m}$ and $\lambda = 600\;\text{nm}$. Find (i) fringe width $\beta$, (ii) position of the $5^{\text{th}}$ bright fringe.

Solution:

(i) $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}= \dfrac{600\times10^{-9}\times2}{0.20\times10^{-3}} = 6.0\times10^{-3}\;\text{m}=6\;\text{mm}$.

(ii) $y_5 = \dfrac{5\lambda D}{d}=5\beta = 5\times6\;\text{mm}=30\;\text{mm}$ below the central maximum.

Division of Amplitude

Division of Amplitude refers to the way the resultant amplitude of two coherent waves is shared between the interfering beams. It is obtained by vector (phasor) addition of the individual amplitudes.

Resultant amplitude (phaser sum):

$$A_R = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1A_2\cos\delta}$$

where $A_1$ and $A_2$ are the individual amplitudes and $\delta$ is the phase difference between them.

Resultant intensity:

$$I = A_R^2$$

For equal amplitudes $A_1=A_2=A$ the expressions simplify \to

$$A_R = 2A\cos\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad I = 4A^2\cos^2\frac{\delta}{2}$$

SI Units:

  • Amplitude $A$: metre (m) for displacement or volt per metre (V/m) for electric field.
  • Intensity $I$: watt per square metre (W/m$^2$).

Derivation (brief): Represent each wave as a rotating vector (phasor) $\vec{A}_1=A_1e^{i\omega t}$ and $\vec{A}_2=A_2e^{i(\omega t+\delta)}$. The vector \sum gives $\vec{A}_R=\vec{A}_1+\vec{A}_2$, whose magnitude yields the formula above.

Example:

Two coherent waves have equal amplitude $A=2\times10^{-6}\,\text{m}$ and a phase difference $\delta=60^{\circ}=\pi/3$. Find the resultant amplitude and the intensity ratio $I/I_0$, where $I_0$ is the intensity of a single wave.

Solution:

$$A_R = \sqrt{2A^2(1+\cos\delta)} = A\sqrt{2(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{3})}=A\sqrt{2(1+0.5)}=A\sqrt{3}$$

$$A_R = 2\times10^{-6}\times\sqrt{3}=3.46\times10^{-6}\,\text{m}$$

Intensity ratio:

$$\frac{I}{I_0}=\left(\frac{A_R}{A}\right)^2 = (\sqrt{3})^2 = 3$$

Thus the resultant amplitude is $3.46\times10^{-6}\,$m and the intensity is three times that of a single wave.

Division of Wavefront

Division of Wavefront – When a continuous wavefront encounters a narrow opening or an obstacle, it gets divided into two or more parts. Each part acts as an independent secondary source (Huygens’ principle) and interferes with others, producing a diffraction pattern.

Key Result (Single‑slit Diffraction)

  • Path difference between extreme rays: $a\\sin\\theta$.
  • Condition for minima: $a\\sin\\theta = m\\lambda$, $m=\\pm1,\\pm2,\\dots$.
  • Angular width of central maximum (between first minima): $\\Delta\\theta = 2\\lambda/a$.

SI Units

QuantitySymbolSI Unit
Wavelength$\\lambda$metre (m)
Slit width$a$metre (m)
Angle$\\theta$radian (rad)

Derivation Sketch

Consider two extreme points of the slit separated by $a$. The extra distance travelled by the ray from the lower point to a point P on the screen is $a\\sin\\theta$. For destructive interference this must equal an integer multiple of $\\lambda$, giving the minima condition above.

Example

Light of wavelength $\\lambda = 600\\,\\text{nm}$ falls on a slit of width $a = 0.20\\,\\text{mm}$. Find the angular width of the central maximum.

Using $\\Delta\\theta = 2\\lambda/a$:

$\\Delta\\theta = 2\\times 600\\times10^{-9}\\,\\text{m} / 0.20\\times10^{-3}\\,\\text{m} = 6.0\\times10^{-3}\\,\\text{rad} \approx 0.34^{\\circ}$.

The central bright fringe spans about $0.34^{\\circ}$ on either side of the central axis.

Interference due to Thin Film

Definition: Thin‑film interference occurs when light reflects from the two parallel surfaces of a film of thickness $t$ and refractive index $n$, producing a path difference that leads to constructive or destructive superposition.

Key derivation: For near‑normal incidence ($r\approx0$), the extra optical path travelled inside the film is $2nt$. If a phase reversal ($\pi$) occurs on reflection from a medium of higher index, the effective path difference becomes $2nt+\frac{\lambda}{2}$. Hence the condition for bright (constructive) and dark (destructive) fringes are:

ConditionResult
$2nt = m\lambda$Constructive (bright) when both reflections involve $\pi$ shift or none.
$2nt = (m+\tfrac{1}{2})\lambda$Constructive when only one reflection has $\pi$ shift.
$2nt = (m+\tfrac{1}{2})\lambda$Destructive (dark) for the opposite case.

SI units: $t$ – metre (m), $\lambda$ – metre (m), $n$ – dimensionless, $m$ – integer (order of fringe).

Example: A soap film ($n=1.33$) is illuminated by monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda=600\,\text{nm}$ in air. The film appears bright for the 3rd order ($m=3$) at normal incidence. Find the film thickness $t$.

  • Use constructive condition without phase reversal: $2nt = m\lambda$.
  • Substitute: $2(1.33)t = 3(600\times10^{-9})$.
  • Calculate: $t = \frac{3\times600\times10^{-9}}{2\times1.33}=6.77\times10^{-7}\,\text{m}=677\,\text{nm}$.

Thus a thickness of approximately $677\,\text{nm}$ yields the 3rd bright fringe.

Newton’s Rings

Definition: Newton’s rings are concentric circular interference fringes formed between a plano‑convex lens of large radius of curvature $R$ and a flat glass plate when illuminated by monochromatic light.

Formation principle: A thin air film of thickness $t$ separates the two surfaces. Light reflected from the top (air‑glass) surface undergoes a $\\pi$ phase shift, while the lower surface does not. The resultant path difference is $2t+\\frac{\\lambda}{2}$.

Geometrical relation: For a lens of radius $R$, the film thickness at a distance $r$ from the centre is $t=\\frac{r^{2}}{2R}$ (using the paraxial approximation).

Fringe typeConditionRadius $r_m$
Bright (constructive)$2t= m\\lambda$$r_{m}=\\sqrt{m\\lambda R}$
Dark (destructive)$2t= (m+\\tfrac{1}{2})\\lambda$$r_{m}=\\sqrt{(m+\\tfrac{1}{2})\\lambda R}$

SI units: $r$ – metre (m), $\\lambda$ – metre (m), $R$ – metre (m), $t$ – metre (m), $m$ – dimensionless.

Example: Monochromatic light of wavelength $\\lambda=600\\,\\text{nm}$ falls on a lens of $R=1.0\\,\\text{m}$. Find the radius of the 4th dark ring ($m=4$).

  • Use dark‑ring formula: $r_{4}=\\sqrt{(4+\\tfrac{1}{2})\\lambda R}$.
  • Convert $\\lambda$: $600\\,\\text{nm}=6\\times10^{-7}\\,\\text{m}$.
  • $r_{4}=\\sqrt{4.5\\times6\\times10^{-7}\\times1}=\\sqrt{2.7\\times10^{-6}}\\approx1.64\\times10^{-3}\\,\\text{m}=1.64\\,\\text{mm}$.

Thus the 4th dark Newton’s ring has a radius of approximately $1.64\\,\\text{mm}$.

Resolving Power of Optical Instruments

Definition: The resolving power of an optical instrument is its ability to distinguish two closely spaced point objects as separate. It is quantified by the smallest angular separation $\theta_{\min}$ that can be resolved.

Rayleigh criterion (circular aperture)

  • Two images are just resolved when the principal maximum of one diffraction pattern coincides with the first minimum of the other.
  • Mathematically, $\theta_{\min}=\frac{1.22\,\lambda}{D}$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength (m) and $D$ is the aperture diameter (m). $\theta_{\min}$ is in radians.

Key formulas

InstrumentResolving power (angular)SI unit
Telescope$\theta_{\min}=\frac{1.22\,\lambda}{D}$rad
Microscope$R=\frac{1}{2\,NA}$ (where $NA=n\sin\alpha$)dimensionless (rad$^{-1}$)

Linear resolution

If the object is at distance $L$, the minimum resolvable separation $d$ is $d=L\,\theta_{\min}$.

Example (Solved)

Find the smallest separation $d$ between two stars that a telescope of aperture $D=0.20\,\text{m}$ can resolve at wavelength $\lambda=550\,\text{nm}$. The stars are $L=1.0\times10^{16}\,\text{m}$ away.

  1. Compute $\theta_{\min}=\frac{1.22\times550\times10^{-9}}{0.20}=3.35\times10^{-6}\,\text{rad}$.
  2. Linear separation $d=L\,\theta_{\min}=1.0\times10^{16}\times3.35\times10^{-6}=3.35\times10^{10}\,\text{m}$.

Thus the telescope can just resolve two stars separated by $3.35\times10^{10}\,\text{m}$ at that distance.

Diffraction (Single Slit)

Definition: Diffraction is the bending of light when it encounters an obstacle or aperture comparable to its wavelength. In single‑slit diffraction a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength $\\lambda$ passes through a narrow slit of width $a$ and produces a characteristic intensity pattern on a screen placed at a distance $D$ ( $D\\gg a$ ).

Condition for minima:

  • The path difference between rays from the extreme edges of the slit is $m\\lambda$ ( $m=\\pm1,\\pm2,\dots$ ).
  • Hence the angular positions of dark fringes satisfy $$a\\sin\\theta = m\\lambda$$ where $\\theta$ is measured from the central axis.

Angular width of central maximum: The first minima occur at $\\theta = \\pm\\lambda/a$, so the angular width $\\Delta\\theta$ is $$\\Delta\\theta = \\frac{2\\lambda}{a}$$ (\in radians).

Intensity distribution: Using Huygens’ principle, the resultant amplitude is proportional \to $\\frac{\\sin\\beta}{\\beta}$ with $\\beta = \\frac{\\pi a \\sin\\theta}{\\lambda}$. The intensity $I(\\theta)$ is $$I(\\theta)=I_0\\left(\\frac{\\sin\\beta}{\\beta}\\right)^2$$ where $I_0$ is the central maximum intensity. Units: $I$ \in $\\text{W/m}^2$.

ParameterSymbolSI Unit
Wavelength$\\lambda$metre (m)
Slit width$a$metre (m)
Order of minima$m$dimensionless
Angle$\\theta$radian (rad)

Example: A monochromatic light of $\\lambda = 600\\,\\text{nm}$ falls on a slit of width $a = 0.03\\,\\text{mm}$. Find the angular position of the third minima ($m=3$) and the linear distance between the third minima on a screen $D = 2\\,\\text{m}$ away.

Solution:

  • Using $a\\sin\\theta = m\\lambda$, $$\\sin\\theta = \\frac{3\\times600\\times10^{-9}}{0.03\\times10^{-3}} = 0.06$$ so $\\theta \approx 3.44^{\\circ}$.
  • Linear distance from centre $y = D\\tan\\theta \approx 2\\times\\tan3.44^{\\circ}=0.12\\,\\text{m}$. The separation between the two symmetric third minima is $2y = 0.24\\,\\text{m}$.
