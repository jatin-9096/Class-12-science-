📌 Core NCERT Definitions

1 Mark Essentials
1. Conservative Force

A force field where total work done in transporting a charge between two points is completely independent of the path taken.

2. Electric Field Intensity

The electrostatic force experienced per unit infinitesimal positive test charge placed at that spatial location.

3. Electrostatic Potential ($V$)

External mechanical work required to bring a unit positive test charge from infinity up to a designated point without acceleration.

4. Work Done ($W$)

Energy transferred to or from a system via vector force acting across displacement ($W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}$).

5. Potential Energy ($U$)

Energy stored within a collective system of charges governed by their relative spatial geometric configuration.

6. Test Charge ($q_0$)

An infinitesimal charge magnitude chosen explicitly so it does not distort the source electric field being mapped.

7. Point Charge Potential

The localized scalar potential generated at distance $r$ from an isolated point charge ($V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$).

8. Electric Dipole & Moment ($\vec{p}$)

Pair of equal and opposite charges ($\pm q$) separated by $2a$. Vector $\vec{p} = q(2\vec{a})$ points conventionally from $-q$ to $+q$.

9. Multi-Charge Superposition

Scalar superposition property: total potential equals direct algebraic sum of potentials produced by all individual charges.

💡 High-Yield Conceptual Pearls

Board Highlights
01

Work done moving a charge in an electrostatic field is independent of path, proving the conservative nature of electrostatic forces.

02

By standard universal reference convention, both electrostatic potential and potential energy are set to zero at infinity ($\infty$).

03

Point charge potential varies inversely with distance: $V \propto 1/r$, while electric field varies as $E \propto 1/r^2$.

04

Electrostatic potential along the entire perpendicular bisector (equatorial line) of a dipole is always exactly zero ($V = 0$), though electric field $\vec{E} \neq 0$.

05

In static equilibrium, electric potential inside any hollow or solid conductor is completely uniform and equal to its surface potential.

06

Potential is positive for positive source charges ($+q$) and negative for negative charges ($-q$). It is strictly a scalar quantity.

📐 Essential Formula Reference Bank

Formula Summary
1. External Work & Line Integral

Work required to move charge $q$ against field $\vec{E}$:

$$W_{RP} = \int_{R}^{P} \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} \cdot d\vec{r} = -\int_{R}^{P} \vec{F}_{\text{elec}} \cdot d\vec{r}$$
2. Point Charge Potential

Scalar potential at distance $r$ from charge $Q$:

$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$$
3. Electric Dipole Potential ($r \gg a$)

Axial ($V = \pm\frac{kp}{r^2}$) | Equatorial ($V = 0$):

$$V_{\text{general}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}\cdot\hat{r}}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p\cos\theta}{r^2}$$
4. Multi-Charge System Potential

Superposition sum across $n$ discrete charges:

$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q_i}{r_i}$$

⚡ Derivation: Electric Potential Due to a Point Charge

Core Proof

Fig 2.2: Coordinate layout for deriving electrostatic potential at point $P$ from charge $+q$.

Step 1 Repulsive Coulomb force acting on test charge $q_0$ at intermediate distance $x$:
$$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q q_0}{x^2}$$
Step 2 Incremental work done against repulsive force across displacement $dx$ ($\theta = 180^\circ$):
$$dW = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{x} = F dx \cos(180^\circ) = -F dx$$
Step 3 Total work required to bring charge $q_0$ from infinity ($\infty$) to radial distance $r$:
$$W = -\int_{\infty}^{r} \left( \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q q_0}{x^2} \right) dx = -\frac{q q_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left[ -\frac{1}{x} \right]_{\infty}^{r} = \frac{q q_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$$
Step 4 By definition, potential $V = W / q_0$:
$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r}$$

Proof: Electric Field as Negative Potential Gradient ($E = -dV/dr$)

Core Derivation

Displacement $d\vec{r}$ connecting adjacent equipotential levels within field $\vec{E}$.

1. External work done to move unit test charge $q_0$ from surface $A$ to $B$ against electric field $\vec{E}$:

$$dW = \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} \cdot d\vec{r} = -q_0 \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$$

2. By potential difference definition: $\frac{dW}{q_0} = V_B - V_A = V - (V + dV) = -dV$.

3. Equating both expressions:

$$-\vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r} = -dV \implies E = -\frac{dV}{dr}$$

Physical Takeaway: The negative sign dictates that electric field lines point in the direction of steepest potential decline.

Unit Equivalency: $\text{V/m} \equiv \text{N/C}$
$$\frac{\text{Volt}}{\text{metre}} = \frac{\text{Joule / Coulomb}}{\text{metre}} = \frac{(\text{Newton} \cdot \text{metre}) / \text{Coulomb}}{\text{metre}} = \frac{\text{Newton}}{\text{Coulomb}} \implies \mathbf{1\text{ V/m} = 1\text{ N/C}}$$

Potential Energy Assembly: Two Point Charges ($U = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r_{12}}$)

Core Derivation
Step 1 Bringing charge $q_1$ from infinity to point $P_1$ requires zero work since no external field is initially present ($W_1 = 0$).
Step 2 Potential established by $q_1$ at location $P_2$ at distance $r_{12}$:
$$V_1 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1}{r_{12}}$$
Step 3 Work performed bringing charge $q_2$ from infinity to point $P_2$:
$$W_2 = q_2 V_1 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{12}}$$
$$U = W_1 + W_2 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{12}}$$

Analytical Coupling: Field as Potential Gradient ($E = -dV/dr$)

Core Derivation

Displacement $d\vec{r}$ between adjacent equipotential levels within field $\vec{E}$.

1. External work done to move unit test charge $q_0$ from surface $A$ to $B$ against field $\vec{E}$:

$$dW = \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} \cdot d\vec{r} = -q_0 \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$$

2. By potential difference definition: $\frac{dW}{q_0} = V_B - V_A = V - (V + dV) = -dV$.

3. Equating both expressions:

$$-\vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r} = -dV \implies E = -\frac{dV}{dr}$$

Physical Takeaway: Electric field vectors point in the direction of steepest potential decline.

4. Potential from known field via integration ($\vec{r}_1 \to \infty, V_1 = 0$):

$$V(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\infty}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$$
Unit Equivalency: $\text{V/m} \equiv \text{N/C}$
$$\frac{\text{Volt}}{\text{metre}} = \frac{\text{Joule / Coulomb}}{\text{metre}} = \frac{(\text{Newton} \cdot \text{metre}) / \text{Coulomb}}{\text{metre}} = \frac{\text{Newton}}{\text{Coulomb}} \implies \mathbf{1\text{ V/m} = 1\text{ N/C}}$$

Electrostatic Potential of a Charged Spherical Shell (Radius $R$, Charge $Q$)

Radial Zones

Spherical shell of radius $R$ and observation point $P$.

Radial potential decay: constant inside, $1/r$ outside.

1. External Point ($r > R$)

Acts as if entire charge $Q$ is concentrated at center:

$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$$
2. Surface Point ($r = R$)

Potential at the outer conductive boundary:

$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{R}$$
3. Internal Point ($r < R$)

Because internal field $E = 0$, potential remains constant:

$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{R} \quad (\text{Constant})$$

Conductors in Equilibrium & Electrostatic Shielding

Physical Laws
Property I: Zero Internal Field

Free electrons redistribute instantly until induced internal fields cancel external fields completely ($\vec{E} = 0$).

Property II: Surface Normal Field

Electric field lines are strictly perpendicular to the outer surface. Any tangential component would trigger continuous current.

Property III: Absence of Interior Charge

Applying Gauss's Law inside the metal reveals zero net volume charge; all excess charge resides strictly on the outer boundary.

Property IV: Equipotential Volume

Because $\vec{E} = -\frac{dV}{dr} = 0$, the potential gradient vanishes, maintaining identical potential everywhere inside and on the surface.

Surface Field Derivation: Gaussian Pillbox ($E = \sigma / \varepsilon_0$)

Constructing a tiny cylindrical pillbox across the surface of a charged conductor with cross-sectional area $\Delta S$:

$$\Phi_E = E \cdot \Delta S = \frac{q_{\text{enclosed}}}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma \Delta S}{\varepsilon_0} \implies \mathbf{E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}}$$

Faraday Cage Principle: Field inside a cavity within a conductor is zero regardless of external fields. Protects passengers in cars/airplanes during lightning strikes.

Electric Dipole: Torque, Potential Energy & Equilibrium States

Torque & Energy

Rotational couple $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$ acting on dipole in uniform field.

1. Restoring torque magnitude: $\tau = qE(2a\sin\theta) = pE\sin\theta \implies \vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.

2. Integrating work done during rotation from $\theta_1$ to $\theta_2$:

$$W = \int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2} pE\sin\theta \, d\theta = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$$

3. Potential energy definition ($U = -\vec{p}\cdot\vec{E} = -pE\cos\theta$):

Stable Equilibrium ($\theta = 0^\circ$) $\tau = 0$, $U_{\min} = -pE$
Zero Energy ($\theta = 90^\circ$) $\tau = pE$, $U = 0$
Unstable Equilibrium ($\theta = 180^\circ$) $\tau = 0$, $U_{\max} = +pE$

Capacitance Standards, Metric Units & Isolated Spherical Conductor

The Farad Unit

Fundamental Definition: $Q \propto V \implies Q = CV \implies C = \frac{Q}{V}$. The SI unit is the Farad ($\text{F}$), where $1\text{ F} = 1\text{ C/V}$. Dimensional formula: $[M^{-1}L^{-2}T^4A^2]$.

Capacitance of an Isolated Spherical Conductor ($R$)

Surface potential due to uniform charge $Q$: $V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{R}$.

$$C = \frac{Q}{V} = \mathbf{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R} \quad (C \propto R)$$

Planetary Context: For an isolated sphere to have $C = 1\text{ F}$, radius $R = 9 \times 10^9\text{ m} = 9 \times 10^6\text{ km}$, roughly 1,500 times larger than Earth's radius ($R_E \approx 6400\text{ km}$). This proves a single isolated farad is impossibly huge; practical units use $\mu\text{F}, \text{nF}, \text{pF}$.

Solved Exemplar: Coalescence of 8 Identical Charged Droplets

Numerical Analysis

Problem: Eight identical spherical droplets, each containing charge $q = 1\text{ nC}$ and at potential $V = 900\text{ V}$, merge without loss to form a single large drop. Compute the resultant potential $V'$.

1. Volume Conservation: $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = 8\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\right) \implies R = 2r$.

2. Charge Conservation: Total charge $Q' = 8q$.

3. Capacitance Scaling: $C' = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 R = 4\pi\varepsilon_0(2r) = 2C$.

4. Resultant Potential:

$$V' = \frac{Q'}{C'} = \frac{8q}{2C} = 4 \left(\frac{q}{C}\right) = 4 \times 900\text{ V} = \mathbf{3600\text{ V}}$$

Redistribution of Charges & Proof of Energy Loss ($\Delta U$)

Sharing Theorem

Conductors linked by a wire equalize at common potential $V$.

1. Common Potential Formula:

$$V = \frac{Q_1 + Q_2}{C_1 + C_2} = \frac{C_1 V_1 + C_2 V_2}{C_1 + C_2}$$

2. Charge Sharing Ratio: After sharing, $Q_1' = C_1 V$ and $Q_2' = C_2 V$:

$$\frac{Q_1'}{Q_2'} = \frac{C_1}{C_2} \quad \text{(Charges divide in direct ratio of capacities)}$$

3. Mathematical Proof of Energy Dissipation ($\Delta U = U_i - U_f$):

$$\Delta U = \frac{1}{2}\frac{C_1 C_2 (V_1 - V_2)^2}{C_1 + C_2}$$

Because $(V_1 - V_2)^2 > 0$, energy loss is strictly positive ($\Delta U > 0$). This energy dissipates as heat in the connecting wire and spark radiation.

Multi-Dielectric Composite Slabs: Series vs Parallel Formulations

Composite Geometry
Tandem Series Slabs ($d = t_1 + t_2 + \dots$)

Slabs stacked sequentially across thickness $d$. System behaves as capacitors in series:

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{\frac{t_1}{K_1} + \frac{t_2}{K_2} + \dots + \frac{t_n}{K_n}}$$
Side-by-Side Parallel Slabs ($A = A_1 + A_2 + \dots$)

Slabs placed side-by-side spanning separation $d$. System behaves as capacitors in parallel:

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{d}(K_1 A_1 + K_2 A_2 + \dots + K_n A_n)$$

Concentric Spheres: Potential Difference & Unidirectional Charge Transfer

Core Principle

Concentric sphere ($r, q$) inside hollow shell ($R, Q$).

1. Potential of inner sphere: $V_r = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{q}{r} + \frac{Q}{R}\right]$.

2. Potential of outer shell: $V_R = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{Q + q}{R}\right]$.

3. Potential Difference:

$$V_r - V_R = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R}\right)$$

Because $r < R$, the term $\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R}\right) > 0$. Hence, if $q > 0$, $V_r$ is always strictly greater than $V_R$. When connected by a wire, charge $q$ flows entirely to the outer shell regardless of how large charge $Q$ is.

Surface Electric Field Proof: Gaussian Pillbox ($E = \sigma / \varepsilon_0$)

Gauss Application

Fig 2.7: Cylindrical Gaussian pillbox spanning the conductor boundary.

1. Inside the conductor, field is zero; bottom cap contributes zero flux.

2. Field is normal to the surface; curved cylindrical walls are parallel to $\vec{E}$, contributing zero flux.

3. Flux emerges solely through the upper cap of area $\Delta S$:

$$\Phi_E = E \cdot \Delta S$$

4. Total charge enclosed with surface density $\sigma$ is $q = \sigma \Delta S$. Applying Gauss's law:

$$E \cdot \Delta S = \frac{\sigma \Delta S}{\varepsilon_0} \implies \mathbf{E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} \hat{n}}$$

Rotational Dynamics & Potential Energy of an Electric Dipole

Torque & Work

Fig 2.8: Equal and opposite electrostatic forces creating couple torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.

1. Net Translatory Force: $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = (+q\vec{E}) + (-q\vec{E}) = 0$. Dipole experiences no net linear acceleration.

2. Torque Magnitude: Force $\times$ perpendicular arm: $\tau = qE(2a\sin\theta) = pE\sin\theta \implies \vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.

3. Angular Work: Work in rotating dipole from $\theta_1$ to $\theta_2$ against restoring torque:

$$W = \int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2} pE\sin\theta \, d\theta = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$$

4. Potential Energy: $U(\theta) = -\vec{p}\cdot\vec{E} = -pE\cos\theta$.

Stable Equilibrium ($\theta = 0^\circ$) $\tau = 0$, $U_{\min} = -pE$ (aligned)
Zero Energy State ($\theta = 90^\circ$) $\tau = pE$ (maximum), $U = 0$
Unstable Equilibrium ($\theta = 180^\circ$) $\tau = 0$, $U_{\max} = +pE$ (opposed)

Working Principle of a Capacitor: Electrostatic Induction & Grounding

Capacity Boost

Fig 2.9: Grounding outer positive charge leaves bound negative charge, dramatically lowering potential and amplifying capacitance ($C = Q/V$).

1. When uncharged plate $B$ approaches charged plate $A$, induced negative charge on $B$'s inner face lowers $A$'s potential, while induced positive charge on $B$'s outer face raises it.

2. Because negative charges are nearer to $A$, the net potential of $A$ decreases slightly.

3. Earthing Effect: When plate $B$ is grounded, free positive charges are neutralized by electrons from earth. Bound negative charges remain, driving potential $V_A$ down substantially.

4. Since $C = Q / V$, lowering potential for the same charge expands capacitance exponentially.

Parallel-Plate Capacitor: Field Superposition & Capacitance Derivation

Core Derivation

Fig 2.10: Complete regional field distribution showing outer cancellation ($E=0$) and inner reinforcement ($E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0$).

1. Outer Regions: Opposing fields from plates cancel: $E_{\text{outer}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0$.

2. Inner Region: Fields add up constructively:

$$E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} + \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A}$$

3. Potential Difference: Uniform field across distance $d$ gives $V = E \cdot d = \frac{Q d}{\varepsilon_0 A}$.

4. Capacitance Expression: Substituting into $C = Q / V$:

$$C_0 = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$
Governing Structural Factors: • Area ($C \propto A$): Larger plates store more charge.
• Separation ($C \propto 1/d$): Bringing plates closer boosts attraction.
• Medium ($C \propto K$): Dielectrics amplify capacity by factor $K$.

📐 Parallel-Plate Capacitor: Field Superposition & Capacitance Derivation

Core Derivation

Two expansive planar conducting armatures of active area $A$, separated across distance $d$, carrying uniform surface charge densities $\pm\sigma = \pm Q/A$.

Fig 2.10: Regional field distribution showing cancellation in outer regions ($E=0$) and inner constructive reinforcement ($E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0$).

1. Outer Cancellation: Opposing fields from plates cancel: $E_{\text{outer}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0$.

2. Inner Reinforcement: Internal fields add constructively: $E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} + \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A}$.

3. Terminal Potential Drop: Uniform field across distance $d$ yields: $V = E \cdot d = \frac{\sigma d}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q d}{\varepsilon_0 A}$.

4. Capacitance Expression ($C = Q / V$):

$$C_0 = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$
1. Plate Surface Area ($C \propto A$) Expansive plate areas accommodate larger total charge accumulations.
2. Inter-Plate Separation ($C \propto 1/d$) Decreasing plate distance strengthens electrostatic attraction, raising capacity.
3. Medium Permittivity ($C \propto \varepsilon$) Inserting dielectric media ($K$) dampens internal field and enhances capacity ($C = K C_0$).

🌐 Concentric Spherical Storage Architectures & Capacitance Derivation

Spherical Shells

Dual concentric spherical armatures: inner shell of radius $a$ carrying charge $-Q$, and outer shell of radius $b$ carrying charge $+Q$.

Fig 2.11: Concentric spherical configuration with intermediate Gaussian surface $r$ ($a < r < b$).

1. Field in the Gap: Applying Gauss's law for $a < r < b$: $E \cdot 4\pi r^2 = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}$.

2. Integrating Potential Difference ($V$):

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} E dr = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int_{a}^{b} r^{-2} dr = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{b - a}{ab}\right)$$

3. Capacitance Formula ($C = Q / V$):

$$C = \frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 ab}{b - a}$$

🔢 Solved Exemplars: Voltage-Charge Variations

Board Problem Sets
Problem 1: Voltage Reduction & Subsequent Rise

A capacitor holds $360\,\mu\text{C}$ at potential $V$. Dropping potential by $120\text{ V}$ reduces charge to $120\,\mu\text{C}$. Find $V$, $C$, and charge if voltage rises by $120\text{ V}$.

• $\frac{V}{V - 120} = \frac{360}{120} = 3 \implies V = 180\text{ V}$

• $C = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{360\,\mu\text{C}}{180\text{ V}} = \mathbf{2\,\mu\text{F}}$

• If $V$ rises by $120\text{ V}$: $Q'' = C(V + 120) = 2\,\mu\text{F}(300\text{ V}) = \mathbf{600\,\mu\text{C}}$

Problem 2: Unknown Capacitance & Voltage Shifts

A capacitor holds $120\,\mu\text{C}$ at potential $V$. Lowering potential by $40\text{ V}$ drops charge to $40\,\mu\text{C}$. Find base potential $V$, capacity $C$, and charge if voltage increases by $40\text{ V}$.

• $\frac{120}{V} = \frac{40}{V - 40} \implies 3(V - 40) = V \implies V = 60\text{ V}$

• $C = \frac{120\,\mu\text{C}}{60\text{ V}} = \mathbf{2\,\mu\text{F}}$

• If $V$ rises by $40\text{ V}$: $Q_{\text{new}} = C(V + 40) = 2\,\mu\text{F}(100\text{ V}) = \mathbf{200\,\mu\text{C}}$

🔌 Network Architectures: Series vs. Parallel Circuits

Equivalent Capacitance
Tandem Series Circuit

Charge Constant ($Q$), Voltages Divide

$$\frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3}$$

• Total voltage: $V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$.
• Equivalent capacity is smaller than the smallest component in the chain.

Parallel Bus Circuit

Voltage Constant ($V$), Charges Add

$$C_p = C_1 + C_2 + C_3$$

• Total charge: $Q = Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3$.
• Equivalent capacity is larger than the largest component in the network.

⚡ Stored Electrostatic Energy & Field Energy Density ($u$)

Volumetric Density

Fig 2.12: Incremental charge transfer $dQ'$ establishing internal field energy density.

1. Integration of Work Done: $W = \int_{0}^{Q} \frac{Q'}{C} dQ' = \frac{1}{C}\left[\frac{Q'^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{Q} = \frac{1}{2}\frac{Q^2}{C}$.

$$U = \frac{1}{2} C V^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2} Q V$$

2. Volumetric Energy Density Derivation ($u$):

$$u = \frac{U}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}\right)(E d)^2}{A \cdot d} \implies \mathbf{u = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2}$$

Universal Principle: This energy density formula holds true for electric fields of any geometry or origin (measured in $\text{J/m}^3$).

⚡ Mathematical Framework & Quantitative Relations

Analytical Overview

Spatial gradient relationships linking fields and potentials, multi-charge interaction energies within external fields, and dipole rotational dynamics.

Gradient Connection

Spatial Field-Potential Link

The magnitude of local electric field intensity equals the maximum spatial rate of potential decrease.

$$|E| = -\frac{\delta V}{\delta l}$$
External Field

Charge Energy in External Potential

Electrostatic potential energy acquired by positioning a charge within an existing spatial potential distribution.

$$U = q \cdot V(r)$$
Orientation State

Dipole Alignment Energy

Potential energy stored in a dipole oriented at angle $\theta$ relative to a uniform electric field.

$$U = -\vec{p}\cdot\vec{E} = -pE\cos\theta$$
Torque & Work

Rotational Work on Dipole

Net external mechanical work required to pivot an electric dipole between two angular orientations.

$$W = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$$
Multi-Body System

Potential Energy of Two Charges in External Field

Includes external field interactions of each charge along with their mutual Coulombic interaction energy:

$$U = q_1 V(r_1) + q_2 V(r_2) + \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{12}}$$

Spatial position vectors $\vec{r}_1$, $\vec{r}_2$, and mutual separation $r_{12}$.

1. Parallel-Plate Capacitor: Internal Field & Geometric Capacitance

Fig 2.13: Uniform field formation between plates and cancellation in external regions.

1. Field Formulation: Electric field intensity between plates is given by $E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A}$.

2. Terminal Potential Difference: $V = E \cdot d = \frac{Q d}{\varepsilon_0 A}$.

3. Capacitance Equation:

$$C_0 = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$
Plate Area ($C \propto A$) Directly proportional: larger surface areas allow greater charge accumulation.
Separation Distance ($C \propto 1/d$) Inversely proportional: decreasing distance elevates electrostatic coupling.
Medium Permittivity ($C \propto K$) Directly proportional: dielectric constant $K$ multiplies total storage capacity ($C = K C_0$).

2. Concentric Spherical Capacitor Derivation

Fig 2.14: Concentric spherical configuration with intermediate Gaussian surface $r$.

1. Field in the Gap ($a < r < b$): By Gauss's Law: $E \cdot 4\pi r^2 = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}$.

2. Potential Difference Line Integral:

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} E dr = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \int_{a}^{b} \frac{1}{r^2} dr = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{b}\right) = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{b - a}{ab}\right)$$

3. Resultant Capacitance ($C = Q / V$):

$$C = \frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 ab}{b - a}$$

3. Solved Board Numericals: Voltage & Charge Variations

Exemplar 1: Voltage Drop & Escalation

A capacitor holds $360\,\mu\text{C}$ at potential $V$. Lowering potential by $120\text{ V}$ reduces charge to $120\,\mu\text{C}$. Calculate $V$, $C$, and charge if voltage rises by $120\text{ V}$.

• Ratio: $\frac{V}{V - 120} = \frac{360}{120} = 3 \implies V = 180\text{ V}$

• Capacity: $C = \frac{360\,\mu\text{C}}{180\text{ V}} = \mathbf{2\,\mu\text{F}}$

• Charge at $(V + 120\text{ V})$: $Q = 2\,\mu\text{F} \times (180 + 120) = \mathbf{600\,\mu\text{C}}$

Exemplar 2: Capacity Under Potential Variation

A capacitor holds $120\,\mu\text{C}$ at potential $V$. Lowering potential by $40\text{ V}$ drops charge to $40\,\mu\text{C}$. Find $V$, $C$, and charge if voltage rises by $40\text{ V}$.

• Ratio: $\frac{120}{V} = \frac{40}{V - 40} \implies 3(V - 40) = V \implies V = 60\text{ V}$

• Capacity: $C = \frac{120\,\mu\text{C}}{60\text{ V}} = \mathbf{2\,\mu\text{F}}$

• Charge at $(V + 40\text{ V})$: $Q = 2\,\mu\text{F} \times (60 + 40) = \mathbf{200\,\mu\text{C}}$

4. Capacitor Combinations: Series & Parallel Topologies

Series Combination ($Q = \text{Constant}$)

Charge is uniform across each plate; terminal voltages divide: $V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$.

$$\frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{C_n}$$
Parallel Combination ($V = \text{Constant}$)

Voltage is identical across all branches; charges accumulate: $Q = Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3$.

$$C_p = C_1 + C_2 + \dots + C_n$$

5. Stored Electrostatic Energy & Field Energy Density ($u$)

Fig 2.15: Incremental charge transfer $dQ'$ against potential $V'$, establishing stored potential energy.

1. Energy Integration: Total work done during charging: $W = \int_{0}^{Q} \frac{Q'}{C} dQ' = \frac{1}{2}\frac{Q^2}{C}$.

$$U = \frac{1}{2} C V^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2} Q V$$

2. Volumetric Energy Density ($u$): Dividing total energy by enclosed plate volume ($A \cdot d$):

$$u = \frac{U}{A \cdot d} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}\right)(E d)^2}{A \cdot d} \implies \mathbf{u = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2}$$

6. Charge Redistribution & Energy Dissipation ($\Delta U$)

Fig 2.16: Charges flow until potential reaches common value $V$.

1. Common Potential: $V = \frac{Q_1 + Q_2}{C_1 + C_2} = \frac{C_1 V_1 + C_2 V_2}{C_1 + C_2}$.

2. Charge Sharing Ratio: $Q_1' = C_1 V$ and $Q_2' = C_2 V \implies \mathbf{\frac{Q_1'}{Q_2'} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}}$ (Charges divide in direct ratio of capacities).

3. Energy Loss Equation ($\Delta U = U_i - U_f$):

$$\Delta U = \frac{1}{2}\frac{C_1 C_2 (V_1 - V_2)^2}{C_1 + C_2}$$

Because $(V_1 - V_2)^2 > 0$, energy loss is strictly positive ($\Delta U > 0$). This energy dissipates as heat in the wire and electromagnetic radiation.

7. Partial Dielectric & Conducting Slab Insertion

Fig 2.17: Field reduction across thickness $t$ inside a dielectric slab.

1. Reduced field inside dielectric slab: $E = \frac{E_0}{K}$.

2. Potential drop summation: $V = E_0(d - t) + \left(\frac{E_0}{K}\right)t = E_0\left(d - t + \frac{t}{K}\right)$.

3. Modified Capacitance Expression:

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t\left(1 - \frac{1}{K}\right)}$$
Completely Filled ($t = d$) $C = K \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \implies \mathbf{C = K \cdot C_0}$
Conducting Slab ($K = \infty$) $C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t} \implies \mathbf{C = \left(\frac{d}{d-t}\right) C_0}$

8. Multi-Dielectric Slabs: Series vs Parallel Configurations

Series layers: division across thickness.

Parallel sections: division across area.

Series Stacking ($d = \sum t_i$):
$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{\frac{t_1}{K_1} + \frac{t_2}{K_2} + \dots + \frac{t_n}{K_n}}$$
Parallel Partitioning ($A = \sum A_i$):
$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{d}\left(K_1 A_1 + K_2 A_2 + \dots + K_n A_n\right)$$

9. Suspended Concentric Spherical Shells: Unidirectional Charge Transfer

Fig 2.18: Inner sphere $r$ connected to outer shell $R$.

1. Inner sphere potential: $V_r = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{q}{r} + \frac{Q}{R}\right]$.

2. Outer shell potential: $V_R = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{q + Q}{R}\right]$.

3. Potential difference:

$$V_r - V_R = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R}\right)$$

Because $r < R$, term $\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R}\right) > 0$. If $q > 0$, then $V_r > V_R$ always. When joined by a wire, charge $q$ flows entirely to the outer shell, regardless of the magnitude of $Q$.
