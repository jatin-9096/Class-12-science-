Chapter 01 Mind Map

Ch 01: Electric Charges & Fields

Branch 1 · Electric Charge & Charging Methods

  • Two types: Positive (electron deficit) & Negative (electron excess). Like repel, unlike attract; repulsion is the surest test.
  • Core Properties: Additivity ($q = \sum q_i$), Conservation (isolated system), Quantisation ($q = \pm ne$, $e = 1.6\times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$), Speed invariance.
  • Charging Methods: Friction (rubbing insulators), Conduction (contact → same sign), Induction (no contact + earthing → opposite sign).

Branch 2 · Coulomb's Law & Superposition

  • Scalar Form: $F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$, where $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}$.
  • Vector Form: $\vec{F}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}\hat{r}_{21}$ and $\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$ (central force + Newton's 3rd Law).
  • Dielectric Medium: $F_{\text{med}} = \frac{F_{\text{vac}}}{K}$ where $K = \varepsilon/\varepsilon_0$.
  • Superposition: Net force on $q_1$ is the vector sum $\vec{F}_1 = \vec{F}_{12} + \vec{F}_{13} + \dots + \vec{F}_{1n}$.

Branch 3 · Electric Field & Field Lines

  • Definition: $\vec{E} = \lim_{q_0\to 0}\frac{\vec{F}}{q_0}$ ($\text{N C}^{-1}$ or $\text{V m}^{-1}$); for a point charge $E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}$.
  • Force on Charge: $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}$ (along $\vec{E}$ for $+q$, opposite for $-q$).
  • Field Lines Rules: $+ \to -$, never intersect, never form closed loops, perpendicular to conductor surface, zero inside conductor.

Branch 4 · Electric Dipole

  • Dipole Moment: $\vec{p} = q(2\vec{a})$, directed from $-q$ to $+q$, SI unit $\text{C m}$.
  • Axial Field ($r \gg a$): $\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2\vec{p}}{r^3}$ (parallel to $\vec{p}$).
  • Equatorial Field ($r \gg a$): $\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}}{r^3}$ (antiparallel to $\vec{p}$, ratio $E_{\text{axial}} = 2E_{\text{eq}}$).
  • Uniform Field Torque: $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p}\times\vec{E} = pE\sin\theta$, $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0$. Stable at $0^\circ$, unstable at $180^\circ$, max torque at $90^\circ$.

Branch 5 · Electric Flux & Gauss's Law

  • Electric Flux: $\Phi_E = \int \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = ES\cos\theta$ ($\text{N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$), scalar quantity.
  • Gauss's Law: $\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$ (independent of surface shape or size).
  • 3 Key Applications: Infinite Wire ($E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$), Infinite Sheet ($E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$), Thin Spherical Shell ($E_{\text{out}} = \frac{kq}{r^2}$, $E_{\text{in}} = 0$).
02.

Exam Problem-Solving Flowchart

Step 1: Read the Charge System in the Question

Step 2: Identify Which Category It Belongs To

  • Point charges (2, 3, or 4 charges)? → Go to Coulomb's Law / Superposition.
  • Equal & opposite pair ($+q, -q$) separated by $2a$? → Go to Dipole Formulas.
  • Flux / closed surface / wire / sheet / shell? → Go to Gauss's Law.

Step 3: Pick the Exact Formula & Direction

Point Charges Find magnitude $F = k\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$ or $E = k\frac{|q|}{r^2}$. Draw arrows first, then combine using $\sqrt{A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta}$ or $x, y$ components.
Electric Dipole Compute $p = q(2a)$. Check if $r \gg a$. Use $\frac{2kp}{r^3}$ (axial, along $\vec{p}$), $\frac{kp}{r^3}$ (equatorial, opposite $\vec{p}$), or $\tau = pE\sin\theta$.
Gauss's Law Find $q_{\text{enc}}$ only. If asked for flux, use $\Phi_E = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$. If asked for $E$, match the Gaussian surface: Cylinder (wire), Pillbox (sheet), Sphere (shell).

Step 4: Final Check Before Writing the Answer

  • Convert $\mu\text{C} \to 10^{-6}\text{ C}$, $\text{nC} \to 10^{-9}\text{ C}$, $\text{cm} \to 10^{-2}\text{ m}$.
  • Write the SI unit ($\text{N}$, $\text{N C}^{-1}$, $\text{C m}$, $\text{N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$) and direction for vector quantities.
03 · Concept Bridges

How Chapter 1 Connects to Upcoming Chapters

  • Two Parallel Sheets ($E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0$) → Used directly in Chapter 2 to derive the capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor ($C = \varepsilon_0 A/d$).
  • Torque on Electric Dipole ($\vec{\tau} = \vec{p}\times\vec{E}$) → Mirrors the torque on a magnetic dipole in Chapter 4 & 5 ($\vec{\tau} = \vec{M}\times\vec{B}$).
  • Gauss's Law in Electrostatics ($\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = q/\varepsilon_0$) → Contrasts directly with Gauss's Law in Magnetism ($\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{S} = 0$, proving no magnetic monopoles exist).
