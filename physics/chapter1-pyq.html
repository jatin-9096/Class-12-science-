01.

Very Short Answer Questions (1 Mark)

Official CBSE PYQ 1 Mark

Q1. Why do electrostatic field lines never intersect each other?

Marking Scheme Answer: If two electric field lines were to intersect at a point, two different tangents could be drawn at that point of intersection. This would mean the net electric field has two different directions at the exact same point, which is physically impossible.

Official CBSE PYQ 1 Mark

Q2. A point charge $+q$ is enclosed at the centre of a spherical Gaussian surface of radius $R$. How does the electric flux through the surface change if the radius of the sphere is doubled?

Marking Scheme Answer: The total electric flux remains unchanged ($\Phi_E = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}$). By Gauss's law, electric flux depends only on the net charge enclosed inside the closed surface and is completely independent of its radius, size, or shape.

Official CBSE PYQ 1 Mark

Q3. Write the orientation of an electric dipole of dipole moment $\vec{p}$ in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$ for which it is in (a) stable equilibrium, and (b) unstable equilibrium.

Marking Scheme Answer:

  • (a) Stable equilibrium: $\theta = 0^\circ$ ($\vec{p}$ is parallel to $\vec{E}$).
  • (b) Unstable equilibrium: $\theta = 180^\circ$ ($\vec{p}$ is antiparallel to $\vec{E}$).
Board Pattern · Practice 1 Mark

Q4. Two charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ satisfy the condition $q_1 + q_2 = 0$. What does this signify about the two charges?

Answer: Since $q_1 = -q_2$, the two charges are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign. If separated by a small fixed distance, they form an electric dipole.

02.

Short Answer Questions (2 Marks)

Official CBSE PYQ 2 Marks

Q5. Derive the expression for the torque $\vec{\tau}$ acting on an electric dipole of dipole moment $\vec{p}$ placed in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$. What is the net translational force acting on it?

Marking Scheme Answer:

  1. Force on $+q$ is $\vec{F}_+ = +q\vec{E}$ and force on $-q$ is $\vec{F}_- = -q\vec{E}$.
  2. Net translational force: $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = q\vec{E} - q\vec{E} = \vec{0}$.
  3. Since the two equal and opposite forces act at different points separated by perpendicular distance $2a\sin\theta$, they form a couple: $$\tau = (qE)(2a\sin\theta) = (q\cdot 2a)E\sin\theta = pE\sin\theta$$
  4. In vector form: $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.
Official CBSE PYQ 2 Marks

Q6. Given a uniform electric field $\vec{E} = 5 \times 10^3\hat{i}\text{ N C}^{-1}$, find the flux of this field through a square of side $10\text{ cm}$ whose plane is parallel to the $yz$-plane. What would be the flux through the same square if the plane makes a $30^\circ$ angle with the $x$-axis?

Marking Scheme Answer:

Area of square $S = (0.1\text{ m})^2 = 10^{-2}\text{ m}^2$.

Case 1 (Plane parallel to $yz$-plane): Area vector $\vec{S}$ is along $+\hat{i}$, so angle with $\vec{E}$ is $\theta = 0^\circ$:

$$\Phi_1 = ES\cos 0^\circ = (5 \times 10^3)(10^{-2})(1) = 50\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$

Case 2 (Plane makes $30^\circ$ with $x$-axis): The normal to the plane makes angle $\theta = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$ with the $x$-axis:

$$\Phi_2 = ES\cos 60^\circ = 50 \times \frac{1}{2} = 25\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$
Official CBSE PYQ 2 Marks

Q7. Two concentric metallic spherical shells $S_1$ and $S_2$ enclose charges $Q$ and $2Q$ respectively ($S_1$ is inside $S_2$, carrying $Q$, and $2Q$ is placed in the space between $S_1$ and $S_2$). (a) Find the ratio of electric flux through $S_1$ and $S_2$. (b) How will the flux through $S_1$ change if a medium of dielectric constant $K$ is introduced inside $S_1$?

Marking Scheme Answer:

(a) Ratio of flux:

  • Charge enclosed by $S_1$: $q_1 = Q \implies \Phi_1 = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}$.
  • Charge enclosed by $S_2$: $q_2 = Q + 2Q = 3Q \implies \Phi_2 = \frac{3Q}{\varepsilon_0}$.
  • Ratio: $\frac{\Phi_1}{\Phi_2} = \frac{Q/\varepsilon_0}{3Q/\varepsilon_0} = \frac{1}{3}$ (i.e., $1 : 3$).

(b) With dielectric $K$ inside $S_1$: Permittivity becomes $\varepsilon = K\varepsilon_0$, so the flux through $S_1$ decreases by a factor of $K$: $\Phi_1' = \frac{Q}{K\varepsilon_0}$.

Board Pattern · Practice 2 Marks

Q8. A point charge $+12\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ is placed at a distance of $5\text{ cm}$ directly above the centre of a square of side $10\text{ cm}$. Calculate the magnitude of electric flux passing through the square.

Solution (Symmetry Trick): Imagine the square of side $10\text{ cm}$ as one face of a closed cube of edge $10\text{ cm}$. Since the charge is $5\text{ cm}$ (half the edge) above the centre of the square, it sits right at the centre of the cube.

Total flux through all 6 faces of the cube = $\frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}$. By symmetry, flux through one face (the square):

$$\Phi_{\text{face}} = \frac{q}{6\varepsilon_0} = \frac{12 \times 10^{-6}}{6 \times 8.854 \times 10^{-12}} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-6}}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}} \approx 2.26 \times 10^5\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$
03.

Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)

Official CBSE PYQ 3 Marks

Q9. Using Gauss's law, deduce the expression for the electric field due to a uniformly charged infinitely long straight thin wire of linear charge density $\lambda\text{ C m}^{-1}$. Plot a graph showing the variation of $E$ with distance $r$ from the wire.

Marking Scheme Answer:

  1. Gaussian surface: Consider a coaxial cylinder of radius $r$ and length $l$ around the wire. By cylindrical symmetry, $\vec{E}$ is radially outward and constant on the curved surface.
  2. Flux calculation:
    • Flat top and bottom caps: $\vec{E} \perp d\vec{S} \implies \Phi_{\text{caps}} = 0$.
    • Curved surface: $\vec{E} \parallel d\vec{S} \implies \Phi_{\text{curved}} = E(2\pi r l)$.
  3. Gauss's Law: Enclosed charge $q_{\text{enc}} = \lambda l$. $$E(2\pi r l) = \frac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$$
  4. Graph ($E$ vs $r$): Since $E \propto \frac{1}{r}$, the graph is a smooth downward-sloping rectangular hyperbola approaching both axes asymptotically.
Official CBSE PYQ 3 Marks

Q10. Two large parallel thin plane sheets have uniform surface charge densities $+\sigma$ and $-\sigma$. Find the electric field in: (a) the region to the left of the first sheet, (b) the region between the two sheets, and (c) the region to the right of the second sheet.

Marking Scheme Answer:

The electric field due to a single infinite plane sheet of surface charge density $\sigma$ is $E_0 = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, directed away from $+\sigma$ and towards $-\sigma$.

  • (a) Left of $+\sigma$ sheet: Field due to $+\sigma$ points left ($-\hat{i}$), field due to $-\sigma$ points right ($+\hat{i}$). Net field: $E_I = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0$.
  • (b) Between the two sheets: Both fields point to the right (from $+\sigma$ toward $-\sigma$). Net field: $$E_{II} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} + \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} \quad (\text{directed from } +\sigma \text{ to } -\sigma)$$
  • (c) Right of $-\sigma$ sheet: Field due to $+\sigma$ points right ($+\hat{i}$), field due to $-\sigma$ points left ($-\hat{i}$). Net field: $E_{III} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0$.
Board Pattern · Practice 3 Marks

Q11. A hollow cylindrical box of length $1\text{ m}$ and cross-sectional area $25\text{ cm}^2$ is placed in a region where the electric field is $\vec{E} = 60x\hat{i}\text{ N C}^{-1}$. The axis of the cylinder lies along the $x$-axis with its left cap at $x = 1\text{ m}$ and right cap at $x = 2\text{ m}$. Find (a) the net flux through the cylinder, and (b) the charge enclosed inside it.

Solution:

Area of each flat cap $S = 25\text{ cm}^2 = 25 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$. Curved surface contributes zero flux since $\vec{E} \perp d\vec{S}$.

Left cap ($x = 1\text{ m}$, outward normal $-\hat{i}$): $E_1 = 60(1) = 60\text{ N C}^{-1}$.

$$\Phi_L = -E_1 S = -60 \times 25 \times 10^{-4} = -0.15\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$

Right cap ($x = 2\text{ m}$, outward normal $+\hat{i}$): $E_2 = 60(2) = 120\text{ N C}^{-1}$.

$$\Phi_R = +E_2 S = +120 \times 25 \times 10^{-4} = +0.30\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$

(a) Net flux: $\Phi_{\text{net}} = 0.30 - 0.15 = 0.15\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$.

(b) Enclosed charge: $q_{\text{enc}} = \varepsilon_0 \Phi_{\text{net}} = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times 0.15 \approx 1.33 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}$.

Official CBSE PYQ 3 Marks

Q12. State Coulomb's law in vector form. Show that it obeys Newton's third law of motion. What is the effect of introducing a dielectric medium of constant $K$ between the two charges?

Marking Scheme Answer:

  1. Let charges $q_1, q_2$ be at position vectors $\vec{r}_1, \vec{r}_2$, with $\vec{r}_{21} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1$ and unit vector $\hat{r}_{21} = \vec{r}_{21}/r$.
  2. Force on $q_2$ due to $q_1$: $\vec{F}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}\hat{r}_{21}$.
  3. Force on $q_1$ due to $q_2$: $\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}\hat{r}_{12}$. Since $\hat{r}_{12} = -\hat{r}_{21}$, we immediately get $\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$, proving Newton's third law.
  4. Effect of dielectric medium ($K$): The electrostatic force decreases by a factor of $K$: $F_{\text{med}} = \frac{F_{\text{vac}}}{K}$.
04.

Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

Official CBSE PYQ 5 Marks

Q13. (a) Define electric dipole moment. Is it a scalar or a vector quantity? Write its SI unit.
(b) Derive an expression for the electric field at a point on the equatorial plane of an electric dipole of length $2a$.
(c) How does the electric field of a short dipole vary with distance $r$? Compare the magnitudes of axial and equatorial fields at the same distance $r$.

Marking Scheme Answer:

(a) Electric dipole moment is the product of the magnitude of either charge and the separation $2a$ between them: $\vec{p} = q(2\vec{a})$. It is a vector quantity directed from $-q$ to $+q$. SI unit: $\text{C m}$.

(b) Equatorial field derivation: For a point $P$ at distance $r$ on the perpendicular bisector, $AP = BP = \sqrt{r^2 + a^2}$, so:

$$E_{+q} = E_{-q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2 + a^2}$$

Perpendicular ($\sin\theta$) components cancel out; parallel ($\cos\theta$) components add along $-\hat{p}$:

$$\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -2E_{+q}\cos\theta\,\hat{p} = -2\left(\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2 + a^2}\right)\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{r^2 + a^2}}\right)\hat{p} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}}{(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2}}$$

For a short dipole ($r \gg a$), $\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}}{r^3}$.

(c) For a short dipole, $E \propto \frac{1}{r^3}$. At the same distance $r$, $E_{\text{axial}} = 2E_{\text{eq}}$ (ratio $2 : 1$).

Official CBSE PYQ 5 Marks

Q14. (a) State Gauss's theorem in electrostatics.
(b) Using this theorem, derive the expression for the electric field due to a uniformly charged thin spherical shell of radius $R$ at a point (i) outside the shell ($r > R$), and (ii) inside the shell ($r < R$).
(c) Plot a graph showing the variation of electric field $E$ as a function of distance $r$ from the centre of the shell.

Marking Scheme Answer:

(a) Gauss's Law: The total electric flux through any closed surface in vacuum is equal to $\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}$ times the net charge enclosed inside it: $\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$.

(b) Spherical shell (radius $R$, charge $q = 4\pi R^2\sigma$): Draw a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius $r$.

  • (i) Outside ($r > R$): $q_{\text{enc}} = q$, flux $= E(4\pi r^2)$. By Gauss's law: $$E(4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2} = \frac{\sigma R^2}{\varepsilon_0 r^2}$$
  • (ii) Inside ($r < R$): Since all charge resides on the outer surface, $q_{\text{enc}} = 0$. Therefore: $$E(4\pi r^2) = 0 \implies E = 0$$

(c) Graph ($E$ vs $r$): $E = 0$ from $r = 0$ to $r < R$; jumps vertically to $E_{\max} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ at $r = R$; then falls smoothly as $E \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$ for $r > R$.

Case-Study Based Question (4 Marks)

Board Pattern · Case Study 4 Marks

Q15. Read the passage below and answer the questions that follow:

Inside an electrostatic precipitator used in thermal power plants to clean smoke, fine ash particles pass through a region of strong electric field created by negatively charged thin vertical wires placed between grounded flat metal plates. The ash particles pick up negative charge and drift rapidly toward the positively grounded plates, sticking to them instead of escaping into the atmosphere.
  1. (i) Why does the electric field near a thin charged wire behave as $E \propto \frac{1}{r}$ instead of $\frac{1}{r^2}$?
  2. (ii) If a negatively charged ash particle of mass $m$ and charge $-q$ enters a uniform horizontal field $\vec{E}$ directed to the right, in which direction does it accelerate?
  3. (iii) A thin vertical wire in the precipitator carries a linear charge density $\lambda = -4 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C m}^{-1}$. Calculate the magnitude of the electric field at a distance of $4\text{ cm}$ from the wire.

Answers:

  1. (i) Because the wire has cylindrical symmetry—the electric field spreads out radially over a cylindrical area $2\pi r l$ (proportional to $r$), not a spherical area $4\pi r^2$. By Gauss's law, $E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r} \propto \frac{1}{r}$.
  2. (ii) Since $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}$ and $q$ is negative, the force (and acceleration) is directed to the left (opposite to $\vec{E}$).
  3. (iii) Using $E = \frac{2k|\lambda|}{r}$ with $r = 4 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$: $$E = \frac{2 \times 9 \times 10^9 \times 4 \times 10^{-6}}{4 \times 10^{-2}} = 1.8 \times 10^6\text{ N C}^{-1} \quad (\text{radially inward toward the wire})$$
