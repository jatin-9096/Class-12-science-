Set 01.

Quantisation of Charge & Coulomb's Law

Problem 1 · NCERT Exercise 1.11

A polythene piece rubbed with wool is found to have a negative charge of $3 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C}$. (a) Estimate the number of electrons transferred (from which to which?). (b) Is there a transfer of mass from wool to polythene?

Given: Charge on polythene $q = -3 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C}$, elementary charge $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, mass of one electron $m_e = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$.

(a) Number of electrons transferred: By quantisation of charge, $q = ne$:

$$n = \frac{|q|}{e} = \frac{3 \times 10^{-7}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 1.875 \times 10^{12}$$

Since polythene acquires a negative charge, electrons are transferred from wool to polythene.

(b) Transfer of mass: Yes, because every electron carries a finite mass $m_e$. Total mass transferred from wool to polythene:

$$\Delta m = n \times m_e = (1.875 \times 10^{12}) \times (9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}) = 1.706 \times 10^{-18}\text{ kg}$$
Answer: (a) $1.875 \times 10^{12}$ electrons from wool to polythene; (b) Yes, $\Delta m \approx 1.71 \times 10^{-18}\text{ kg}$ (negligibly small).
Problem 2 · NCERT Ex 1.12 & 1.13 (Board Classic)

Two insulated charged copper spheres $A$ and $B$ have their centres separated by $50\text{ cm}$. The charge on each is $6.5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C}$. (a) What is the mutual force of electrostatic repulsion? (b) A third identical uncharged sphere $C$ is brought in contact with $A$, then brought in contact with $B$, and finally removed. What is the new force of repulsion between $A$ and $B$?

Given: $q_A = q_B = q = 6.5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C}$, separation $r = 50\text{ cm} = 0.5\text{ m}$, $k = 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}$.

(a) Initial repulsive force:

$$F = k\frac{q_A q_B}{r^2} = \frac{9 \times 10^9 \times (6.5 \times 10^{-7})^2}{(0.5)^2} = \frac{9 \times 10^9 \times 42.25 \times 10^{-14}}{0.25}$$ $$F = \frac{380.25 \times 10^{-5}}{0.25} = 1521 \times 10^{-5} = 1.521 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N}$$

(b) Charge sharing with identical sphere $C$:

1. When uncharged $C$ touches $A$, the total charge $q$ splits equally: $q_A' = \frac{q}{2}$ and $q_C' = \frac{q}{2}$.

2. When $C$ (carrying $\frac{q}{2}$) touches $B$ (carrying $q$), the combined charge $\frac{q}{2} + q = \frac{3q}{2}$ splits equally: $q_B' = \frac{3q}{4}$.

3. New force between $A$ and $B$ at the same separation $r$:

$$F' = k\frac{q_A' q_B'}{r^2} = k\frac{(q/2)(3q/4)}{r^2} = \frac{3}{8}\left(k\frac{q^2}{r^2}\right) = \frac{3}{8}F$$ $$F' = \frac{3}{8} \times 1.521 \times 10^{-2} = 5.70 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N}$$
Answer: (a) $1.52 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N}$; (b) $5.70 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N}$.
Problem 3 · Dielectric Medium

Two point charges $+4\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ and $+9\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ are separated by $30\text{ cm}$ in air. (a) Find the electrostatic force between them in air. (b) If they are immersed in an insulating oil of dielectric constant $K = 4$, what must be the new separation so that the force remains unchanged?

Given: $q_1 = 4 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C}$, $q_2 = 9 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C}$, $r = 30\text{ cm} = 0.3\text{ m}$, $K = 4$.

(a) Force in air:

$$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} = \frac{9 \times 10^9 \times 4 \times 10^{-6} \times 9 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.3)^2} = \frac{324 \times 10^{-3}}{0.09} = 3.6\text{ N}$$

(b) Equivalent separation in oil ($K = 4$): Setting $F_{\text{oil}} = F_{\text{air}}$:

$$\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 K}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r'^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \implies K r'^2 = r^2 \implies r' = \frac{r}{\sqrt{K}}$$ $$r' = \frac{30\text{ cm}}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{30}{2} = 15\text{ cm}$$
Answer: (a) $3.6\text{ N}$ (repulsive); (b) $15\text{ cm}$.
Set 02.

Superposition Principle & Equilibrium of Charges

Problem 4 · CBSE Board Repeated

Two point charges $+4e$ and $+e$ are fixed at a distance $a$ apart. Where should a third point charge $q$ be placed on the line joining them so that it remains in equilibrium? Discuss whether this equilibrium is stable or unstable for a positive charge $q$ displaced slightly along the line.

Analysis: Since both fixed charges have the same sign ($+$), their forces on $q$ oppose each other only in the region between them. Let $q$ be placed at distance $x$ from $+4e$, so its distance from $+e$ is $(a - x)$.

Condition for equilibrium ($F_1 = F_2$):

$$\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(4e)q}{x^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(e)q}{(a - x)^2} \implies \frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{1}{(a - x)^2}$$

Taking the positive square root (since $0 < x < a$):

$$\frac{2}{x} = \frac{1}{a - x} \implies 2(a - x) = x \implies 2a = 3x \implies x = \frac{2a}{3}$$

Nature of equilibrium (for $+q$): If $+q$ is nudged slightly toward $+e$, the repulsive force from $+e$ increases while that from $+4e$ decreases, so the net force pushes $+q$ right back to its equilibrium position. Thus, it is in stable equilibrium for displacements along the line.

Answer: At $x = \frac{2a}{3}$ from $+4e$ (or $\frac{a}{3}$ from $+e$); Stable equilibrium along the line for $+q$.
Problem 5 · NCERT Example 1.6 & Triangle Superposition

Three equal point charges $q$ each are placed at the three vertices $A, B, C$ of an equilateral triangle of side $l$. (a) Find the net force on a charge $Q$ placed at the centroid $O$ of the triangle. (b) If $Q$ is removed, find the net force on the charge at vertex $C$ due to the charges at $A$ and $B$.

(a) Force on $Q$ at centroid $O$: Distance of centroid from each vertex is $r = \frac{l}{\sqrt{3}}$. Magnitude of force due to each vertex charge is identical:

$$F_1 = F_2 = F_3 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{qQ}{(l/\sqrt{3})^2} = \frac{3kqQ}{l^2}$$

By symmetry, the three force vectors lie in the plane of the triangle at $120^\circ$ to each other. Resultant of $\vec{F}_2$ and $\vec{F}_3$:

$$F_{23} = \sqrt{F^2 + F^2 + 2F^2\cos 120^\circ} = \sqrt{2F^2 - F^2} = F$$

This resultant $F_{23}$ points directly opposite to $\vec{F}_1$, so $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_{23} = \vec{0}$.

(b) Force on charge $q$ at vertex $C$ (with $Q$ removed): Forces due to $A$ and $B$ have equal magnitude $F_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q^2}{l^2}$ and act at an angle $\theta = 60^\circ$ to each other:

$$F_C = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + 2F_0^2\cos 60^\circ} = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + F_0^2} = \sqrt{3}F_0 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q^2}{l^2}$$
Answer: (a) Zero; (b) $\frac{\sqrt{3}q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 l^2}$ directed outward along the angle bisector at $C$.
Problem 6 · NCERT Exercise 1.6

Four point charges $q_A = 2\text{ }\mu\text{C}$, $q_B = -5\text{ }\mu\text{C}$, $q_C = 2\text{ }\mu\text{C}$, and $q_D = -5\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ are located at the corners of a square $ABCD$ of side $10\text{ cm}$. What is the force on a charge of $1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ placed at the centre $O$ of the square?

Analysis: The centre $O$ is equidistant from all four corners ($OA = OB = OC = OD = \frac{10}{\sqrt{2}}\text{ cm} = 5\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$).

1. Pair $A$ and $C$: Both $q_A = +2\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ and $q_C = +2\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ repel the $+1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ charge at $O$ with equal magnitude along diagonal $AC$ in opposite directions $\implies \vec{F}_A + \vec{F}_C = \vec{0}$.

2. Pair $B$ and $D$: Both $q_B = -5\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ and $q_D = -5\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ attract the $+1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ charge at $O$ with equal magnitude along diagonal $BD$ in opposite directions $\implies \vec{F}_B + \vec{F}_D = \vec{0}$.

3. Net force: $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = (\vec{F}_A + \vec{F}_C) + (\vec{F}_B + \vec{F}_D) = \vec{0}$.

Answer: Net electrostatic force on the $1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ charge at $O$ is $0\text{ N}$.
Set 03.

Electric Field Intensity & Motion of Charges

Problem 7 · NCERT Exercise 1.8

Two point charges $q_A = +3\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ and $q_B = -3\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ are located $20\text{ cm}$ apart in vacuum. (a) What is the electric field at the midpoint $O$ of the line $AB$ joining the two charges? (b) If a negative test charge of magnitude $1.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ C}$ is placed at this point, what is the force experienced by it?

Given: $q_A = +3 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C}$, $q_B = -3 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C}$, distance of midpoint from each charge $r = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$.

(a) Electric field at midpoint $O$:

Field $\vec{E}_A$ due to $+3\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ points away from $A$ (along $OB$). Field $\vec{E}_B$ due to $-3\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ points toward $B$ (also along $OB$). Since both act in the same direction, they add up:

$$E_A = E_B = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q|}{r^2} = \frac{9 \times 10^9 \times 3 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.1)^2} = 2.7 \times 10^6\text{ N C}^{-1}$$ $$E_{\text{net}} = E_A + E_B = 2 \times (2.7 \times 10^6) = 5.4 \times 10^6\text{ N C}^{-1} \quad (\text{along } OB)$$

(b) Force on test charge $q_0 = -1.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ C}$:

$$F = |q_0| E_{\text{net}} = (1.5 \times 10^{-9}) \times (5.4 \times 10^6) = 8.1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N}$$

Because $q_0$ is negative, the force acts opposite to $\vec{E}_{\text{net}}$, i.e., along $OA$.

Answer: (a) $5.4 \times 10^6\text{ N C}^{-1}$ along $OB$; (b) $8.1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N}$ along $OA$.
Problem 8 · Null Point (Unlike Charges)

Two point charges $+4\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ and $-1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ are separated by $60\text{ cm}$ in air. Find the point on the line joining them where the net electric field is zero.

Analysis: Because the charges have opposite signs, between them both electric fields point in the same direction (from $+4\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ to $-1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$) and can never cancel. Hence, the null point $P$ must lie outside on the side of the smaller magnitude charge ($-1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$).

Let $P$ be at distance $x$ from $-1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ on the outer side. Then its distance from $+4\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ is $(60 + x)\text{ cm}$. Setting $E_1 = E_2$:

$$\frac{k(4 \times 10^{-6})}{(60 + x)^2} = \frac{k(1 \times 10^{-6})}{x^2} \implies \frac{4}{(60 + x)^2} = \frac{1}{x^2}$$

Taking positive square root:

$$\frac{2}{60 + x} = \frac{1}{x} \implies 2x = 60 + x \implies x = 60\text{ cm}$$
Answer: $60\text{ cm}$ from the $-1\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ charge on the side away from $+4\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ (i.e., $120\text{ cm}$ from $+4\text{ }\mu\text{C}$).
Problem 9 · NCERT Example 1.8 (Kinematics in $\vec{E}$)

An electron falls through a distance of $1.5\text{ cm}$ in a uniform electric field of magnitude $2.0 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}$. The direction of the field is then reversed keeping its magnitude unchanged, and a proton falls through the same distance. Compute the time of fall in each case. (Neglect gravity.)

Given: $h = 1.5\text{ cm} = 1.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$, $E = 2.0 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}$, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$, $m_p = 1.67 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$.

Formula: Acceleration $a = \frac{eE}{m}$, and starting from rest ($u = 0$), $h = \frac{1}{2}at^2 \implies t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{2hm}{eE}}$.

1. Time of fall for electron ($t_e$):

$$a_e = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2.0 \times 10^4}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}} = 3.51 \times 10^{15}\text{ m s}^{-2}$$ $$t_e = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-2}}{3.51 \times 10^{15}}} = \sqrt{8.55 \times 10^{-18}} \approx 2.92 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s}$$

2. Time of fall for proton ($t_p$):

$$a_p = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2.0 \times 10^4}{1.67 \times 10^{-27}} = 1.92 \times 10^{12}\text{ m s}^{-2}$$ $$t_p = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-2}}{1.92 \times 10^{12}}} = \sqrt{1.56 \times 10^{-14}} \approx 1.25 \times 10^{-7}\text{ s}$$
Answer: $t_e = 2.92 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s}$; $t_p = 1.25 \times 10^{-7}\text{ s}$ (the heavier proton takes longer—unlike free fall under gravity where all masses fall in the same time!).
Set 04.

Electric Dipole & Torque

Problem 10 · NCERT Ex 1.9 & 1.10

(a) A system has two charges $q_A = +2.5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C}$ and $q_B = -2.5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C}$ located at $A: (0, 0, -15\text{ cm})$ and $B: (0, 0, +15\text{ cm})$. Find the total charge and electric dipole moment of the system. (b) An electric dipole of dipole moment $4 \times 10^{-9}\text{ C m}$ is aligned at $30^\circ$ with a uniform electric field of $5 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}$. Calculate the magnitude of torque acting on it.

(a) Total charge & dipole moment:

$$q_{\text{total}} = q_A + q_B = (2.5 \times 10^{-7}) + (-2.5 \times 10^{-7}) = 0\text{ C}$$

Separation $2a = 15 - (-15) = 30\text{ cm} = 0.30\text{ m}$.

$$p = q(2a) = (2.5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ C})(0.30\text{ m}) = 7.5 \times 10^{-8}\text{ C m}$$

Direction of $\vec{p}$ is from negative charge $B$ ($+15\text{ cm}$) to positive charge $A$ ($-15\text{ cm}$), i.e., along the negative $z$-axis ($-\hat{k}$).

(b) Torque on dipole: Using $\tau = pE\sin\theta$:

$$\tau = (4 \times 10^{-9})(5 \times 10^4)\sin 30^\circ = (20 \times 10^{-5})(0.5) = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ N m}$$
Answer: (a) $q_{\text{total}} = 0$, $\vec{p} = -7.5 \times 10^{-8}\hat{k}\text{ C m}$; (b) $\tau = 10^{-4}\text{ N m}$.
Problem 11 · NCERT Example 1.10 (Axial & Equatorial)

Two charges $\pm 10\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ are placed $5.0\text{ mm}$ apart. Determine the electric field at (a) a point $P$ on the axis of the dipole $15\text{ cm}$ away from its centre $O$ on the side of the positive charge, and (b) a point $Q$, $15\text{ cm}$ away from $O$ on a line passing through $O$ and normal to the axis of the dipole.

Given: $q = 10 \times 10^{-6}\text{ C}$, $2a = 5.0\text{ mm} = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$, $r = 15\text{ cm} = 0.15\text{ m}$.

Dipole moment: $p = q(2a) = (10 \times 10^{-6})(5 \times 10^{-3}) = 5 \times 10^{-8}\text{ C m}$.

Since $r = 15\text{ cm} \gg a = 0.25\text{ cm}$, the short-dipole approximation holds with $r^3 = (0.15)^3 = 3.375 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3$.

(a) Field on the axial line ($P$):

$$E_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2p}{r^3} = \frac{9 \times 10^9 \times 2 \times 5 \times 10^{-8}}{3.375 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{900}{3.375 \times 10^{-3}} = 2.67 \times 10^5\text{ N C}^{-1}$$

Directed along $\vec{p}$ (from $-q$ to $+q$).

(b) Field on the equatorial line ($Q$):

$$E_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{r^3} = \frac{E_{\text{axial}}}{2} = 1.33 \times 10^5\text{ N C}^{-1}$$

Directed antiparallel to $\vec{p}$ (from $+q$ to $-q$).

Answer: (a) $2.67 \times 10^5\text{ N C}^{-1}$ along $\vec{p}$; (b) $1.33 \times 10^5\text{ N C}^{-1}$ opposite to $\vec{p}$.
Set 05.

Electric Flux & Applications of Gauss's Law

Problem 12 · NCERT Ex 1.15 & 1.16

Consider a uniform electric field $\vec{E} = 3 \times 10^3\hat{i}\text{ N C}^{-1}$. (a) What is the flux of this field through a square of $10\text{ cm}$ on a side whose plane is parallel to the $yz$-plane? (b) What is the flux through the same square if the normal to its plane makes a $60^\circ$ angle with the $x$-axis? (c) What is the net flux of this uniform field through a cube of side $20\text{ cm}$ oriented with faces parallel to the coordinate planes?

Given: $E = 3 \times 10^3\text{ N C}^{-1}$ along $+\hat{i}$, area of square $S = (0.1\text{ m})^2 = 10^{-2}\text{ m}^2$.

(a) Plane parallel to $yz$-plane: Normal to the square points along the $x$-axis ($\hat{i}$), so $\theta = 0^\circ$:

$$\Phi_1 = ES\cos 0^\circ = (3 \times 10^3)(10^{-2})(1) = 30\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$

(b) Normal at $\theta = 60^\circ$ to $x$-axis:

$$\Phi_2 = ES\cos 60^\circ = 30 \times 0.5 = 15\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$

(c) Net flux through the closed cube: Because $\vec{E}$ is uniform, the number of field lines entering the left face equals the number of field lines leaving the right face (and no lines cross the other four faces). Hence $\Phi_{\text{net}} = 0$.

Answer: (a) $30\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$; (b) $15\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$; (c) $0\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$.
Problem 13 · NCERT Example 1.11 (CBSE 5-Mark Favourite!)

The electric field components in a region are $E_x = \alpha x^{1/2}$ and $E_y = E_z = 0$, where $\alpha = 800\text{ N C}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-1/2}$. A cube of side $a = 0.1\text{ m}$ is placed with its left face at $x = a$ and right face at $x = 2a$. Calculate (a) the net electric flux through the cube, and (b) the charge enclosed within the cube.

Given: $\alpha = 800\text{ N C}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-1/2}$, side $a = 0.1\text{ m}$, area of each face $S = a^2$.

(a) Flux through the 6 faces: Since $\vec{E}$ is purely along the $x$-axis, flux through the top, bottom, front, and back faces is zero ($\vec{E} \perp d\vec{S}$). Only the left ($x = a$) and right ($x = 2a$) faces contribute:

1. Left face ($x = a$, outward normal along $-\hat{i}$, $\theta = 180^\circ$):

$$\Phi_L = E_L a^2 \cos 180^\circ = -(\alpha a^{1/2})a^2 = -\alpha a^{5/2}$$

2. Right face ($x = 2a$, outward normal along $+\hat{i}$, $\theta = 0^\circ$):

$$\Phi_R = E_R a^2 \cos 0^\circ = +(\alpha (2a)^{1/2})a^2 = \sqrt{2}\,\alpha a^{5/2}$$

3. Net flux through the cube:

$$\Phi_{\text{net}} = \Phi_R + \Phi_L = \alpha a^{5/2}(\sqrt{2} - 1)$$

Compute $a^{5/2} = (0.1)^{2.5} = 0.01 \times \sqrt{0.1} = 0.01 \times 0.3162 = 3.162 \times 10^{-3}$:

$$\Phi_{\text{net}} = 800 \times (3.162 \times 10^{-3}) \times (1.414 - 1) = 2.5296 \times 0.414 \approx 1.05\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$$

(b) Charge enclosed inside the cube (by Gauss's Law):

$$q_{\text{enc}} = \varepsilon_0 \Phi_{\text{net}} = (8.854 \times 10^{-12}) \times 1.047 \approx 9.27 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}$$
Answer: (a) $\Phi_{\text{net}} = 1.05\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$; (b) $q_{\text{enc}} = 9.27 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}$.
Problem 14 · NCERT Exercise 1.23 (Infinite Line Charge)

An infinite line charge produces a field of $9 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}$ at a distance of $2\text{ cm}$. Calculate the linear charge density $\lambda$.

Given: $E = 9 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}$, perpendicular distance $r = 2\text{ cm} = 2 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$.

Formula: $E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r} = \frac{2k\lambda}{r}$ where $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}$.

$$\lambda = \frac{E r}{2k} = \frac{(9 \times 10^4) \times (2 \times 10^{-2})}{2 \times (9 \times 10^9)} = \frac{1.8 \times 10^3}{1.8 \times 10^{10}} = 10^{-7}\text{ C m}^{-1}$$
Answer: $\lambda = 10^{-7}\text{ C m}^{-1} = 0.1\text{ }\mu\text{C m}^{-1}$.
Problem 15 · NCERT Exercise 1.24 (Two Parallel Sheets)

Two large, thin metal plates are parallel and close to each other. On their inner faces, the plates have surface charge densities of opposite signs and of magnitude $17.0 \times 10^{-22}\text{ C m}^{-2}$. What is $\vec{E}$: (a) in the outer region of the first plate, (b) in the outer region of the second plate, and (c) between the plates?

Given: $|\sigma| = 17.0 \times 10^{-22}\text{ C m}^{-2}$, $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2\text{ N}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-2}$. Field due to a single infinite sheet is $E_1 = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$.

(a) Outer region of the first plate: The fields due to $+\sigma$ and $-\sigma$ are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction:

$$E_{\text{out, 1}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0$$

(b) Outer region of the second plate: Again, the two fields cancel out completely:

$$E_{\text{out, 2}} = 0$$

(c) Region between the plates: Both fields point from the positive plate to the negative plate, so they add:

$$E_{\text{between}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} + \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{17.0 \times 10^{-22}}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}} = 1.92 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N C}^{-1}$$
Answer: (a) $0$; (b) $0$; (c) $1.92 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N C}^{-1}$ directed from the positive plate to the negative plate.
Problem 16 · NCERT Ex 1.21 & 1.22 (Spherical Shell)

A conducting sphere of radius $10\text{ cm}$ has an unknown charge. If the electric field $20\text{ cm}$ from the centre of the sphere is $1.5 \times 10^3\text{ N C}^{-1}$ and points radially inward, calculate: (a) the net charge on the sphere, (b) its surface charge density $\sigma$, and (c) the electric field at a distance of $5\text{ cm}$ from the centre.

Given: Radius $R = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$, distance $r = 20\text{ cm} = 0.2\text{ m}$ ($r > R$), $E = 1.5 \times 10^3\text{ N C}^{-1}$ (radially inward).

(a) Net charge $q$: Because the field points radially inward, the charge on the sphere is negative. For $r > R$, $E = \frac{k|q|}{r^2}$:

$$|q| = \frac{Er^2}{k} = \frac{(1.5 \times 10^3) \times (0.2)^2}{9 \times 10^9} = \frac{1.5 \times 10^3 \times 0.04}{9 \times 10^9} = \frac{60}{9 \times 10^9} = 6.67 \times 10^{-9}\text{ C}$$ $$q = -6.67\text{ nC}$$

(b) Surface charge density $\sigma$:

$$\sigma = \frac{q}{4\pi R^2} = \frac{-6.67 \times 10^{-9}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times (0.1)^2} = \frac{-6.67 \times 10^{-9}}{0.1257} \approx -5.31 \times 10^{-8}\text{ C m}^{-2}$$

(c) Electric field at $r = 5\text{ cm}$: Since $5\text{ cm} < R = 10\text{ cm}$, the point lies inside the conducting sphere where $q_{\text{enc}} = 0$, so by Gauss's Law $E = 0$.

Answer: (a) $q = -6.67\text{ nC}$; (b) $\sigma = -5.31 \times 10^{-8}\text{ C m}^{-2}$; (c) $E = 0$.
