01.

All NCERT Definitions (One by One)

Part A · Charge & Fundamental Properties

Electric Charge & Its Properties

Definition 1

Electric Charge ($q$)

An intrinsic property of elementary particles (protons and electrons) by virtue of which they exert and experience electrostatic forces. It is a scalar quantity. SI unit: Coulomb ($\text{C}$).

Definition 2

Additivity of Charge

The total electric charge of an extended system is the algebraic sum of all individual charges present at different points inside the system: $q_{\text{net}} = q_1 + q_2 + \dots + q_n$.

Definition 3

Conservation of Charge

The total electric charge of an isolated system always remains constant. Charge can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transferred from one body to another.

Definition 4

Quantisation of Charge

Every free electric charge in nature is always an integral multiple of the basic elementary charge $e$ ($1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$): $$q = \pm n e \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$

Definition 5

Conductors & Insulators

Conductors: Materials that allow electric charges to pass through them easily due to free electrons (e.g., copper, aluminium, earth).
Insulators (Dielectrics): Materials that offer high resistance to the flow of charge because electrons are tightly bound (e.g., glass, plastic, dry wood).

Definition 6

Charging by Induction

The process of charging an uncharged conductor by bringing a charged body near it without actual physical contact. Opposite charge is induced on the nearer face and like charge on the farther face.

Part B · Forces, Fields, Dipoles & Gauss's Law

Coulomb's Law, Electric Field, Dipole & Flux

Definition 7

Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two stationary point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, acting along the line joining the charges: $$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}, \qquad \vec{F}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{21}^2}\hat{r}_{21}$$ where $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2\text{ N}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-2}$ and $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}$.

Definition 8

One Coulomb ($1\text{ C}$)

One coulomb is the charge which, when placed at a distance of $1\text{ m}$ from an equal and similar charge in vacuum, repels it with a force of $9 \times 10^9\text{ N}$.

Definition 9

Dielectric Constant / Relative Permittivity ($K$ or $\varepsilon_r$)

The ratio of permittivity of a medium ($\varepsilon$) to permittivity of free space ($\varepsilon_0$), equal to the ratio of electrostatic force in vacuum to force in the medium for the same charges and separation: $$K = \varepsilon_r = \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{F_{\text{vac}}}{F_{\text{med}}}$$

Definition 10

Principle of Superposition

The net electrostatic force on any charge due to a system of other charges is the vector sum of the individual forces exerted on it by each charge taken one at a time, unaffected by the presence of other charges: $$\vec{F}_1 = \vec{F}_{12} + \vec{F}_{13} + \dots + \vec{F}_{1n}$$

Definition 11

Electric Field Intensity ($\vec{E}$)

The electrostatic force experienced per unit vanishingly small positive test charge ($q_0 \to 0$) placed at a point: $$\vec{E} = \lim_{q_0 \to 0}\frac{\vec{F}}{q_0}$$ SI unit: $\text{N C}^{-1}$ or $\text{V m}^{-1}$. Vector quantity.

Definition 12

Electric Field Lines

Imaginary smooth curves drawn in an electric field such that the tangent at any point gives the direction of the net electric field at that point. They originate on positive charges, terminate on negative charges, never intersect, and never form closed loops.

Definition 13

Electric Dipole & Dipole Moment ($\vec{p}$)

An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite point charges ($+q$ and $-q$) separated by a small distance $2a$. Its dipole moment is $\vec{p} = q(2\vec{a})$, directed from $-q$ to $+q$. SI unit: $\text{C m}$.

Definition 14

Continuous Charge Densities ($\lambda, \sigma, \rho$)

Linear charge density: $\lambda = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta l}$ ($\text{C m}^{-1}$) · Surface charge density: $\sigma = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta S}$ ($\text{C m}^{-2}$) · Volume charge density: $\rho = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta V}$ ($\text{C m}^{-3}$).

Definition 15

Electric Flux ($\Phi_E$)

The measure of the total number of electric field lines crossing a given surface normally: $$d\Phi_E = \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{S} = E\,dS\cos\theta, \qquad \Phi_E = \int \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S}$$ SI unit: $\text{N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1}$ (or $\text{V m}$). Scalar quantity.

Definition 16

Gauss's Law

The total electric flux through any closed surface (Gaussian surface) in vacuum is equal to $\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}$ times the net electric charge enclosed inside it: $$\Phi_E = \oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

02.

All 7 Full Step-by-Step Derivations

Derivation 1 · High Yield

Electric Field due to a Dipole on the Axial Line

Setup: Consider a dipole of charges $-q$ at $A$ and $+q$ at $B$ separated by $2a$, with midpoint $O$. Let $P$ be a point on the axial line at distance $r$ from $O$ on the side of $+q$. Let $\hat{p}$ be the unit vector along $\vec{p}$ (from $-q$ to $+q$).

  1. Distance of $P$ from $+q$ is $(r - a)$, and from $-q$ is $(r + a)$.
  2. Field at $P$ due to $+q$ (away from $+q$, along $\hat{p}$): $$\vec{E}_{+q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{(r - a)^2}\hat{p}$$
  3. Field at $P$ due to $-q$ (towards $-q$, along $-\hat{p}$): $$\vec{E}_{-q} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{(r + a)^2}\hat{p}$$
  4. Net field at $P$ by superposition ($\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \vec{E}_{+q} + \vec{E}_{-q}$): $$\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{1}{(r - a)^2} - \frac{1}{(r + a)^2}\right]\hat{p} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{(r + a)^2 - (r - a)^2}{(r^2 - a^2)^2}\right]\hat{p}$$ Since $(r + a)^2 - (r - a)^2 = 4ar$ and $p = q(2a)$: $$\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2(q \cdot 2a)r}{(r^2 - a^2)^2}\hat{p} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2pr}{(r^2 - a^2)^2}\hat{p}$$
  5. Short Dipole ($r \gg a$): Neglecting $a^2$ compared to $r^2$, $(r^2 - a^2)^2 \approx r^4$: $$\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2\vec{p}}{r^3}$$
Direction: Always parallel to $\vec{p}$ (from $-q$ towards $+q$).
Derivation 2 · High Yield

Electric Field due to a Dipole on the Equatorial Line

Setup: Consider a dipole ($-q$ at $A$, $+q$ at $B$, separation $2a$, centre $O$). Let $P$ be a point on the perpendicular bisector at distance $r$ from $O$.

  1. By Pythagoras theorem, distance $AP = BP = \sqrt{r^2 + a^2}$. Let $\angle PAB = \angle PBA = \theta$.
  2. Magnitudes of fields at $P$ due to $+q$ and $-q$ are equal: $$E_{+q} = E_{-q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2 + a^2}$$
  3. Resolve both vectors parallel and perpendicular to the dipole axis:
    • Perpendicular components ($E_{+q}\sin\theta$ upwards and $E_{-q}\sin\theta$ downwards) are equal and opposite, so they cancel out.
    • Parallel components ($E_{+q}\cos\theta$ and $E_{-q}\cos\theta$) both act opposite to $\vec{p}$ (along $-\hat{p}$), so they add up.
  4. Net electric field at $P$: $$\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -(E_{+q}\cos\theta + E_{-q}\cos\theta)\hat{p} = -2E_{+q}\cos\theta\,\hat{p}$$ From right triangle $\triangle AOP$, $\cos\theta = \frac{a}{\sqrt{r^2 + a^2}}$: $$\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -2\left(\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2 + a^2}\right)\frac{a}{(r^2 + a^2)^{1/2}}\hat{p} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}}{(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2}}$$
  5. Short Dipole ($r \gg a$): Neglecting $a^2$, $(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2} \approx r^3$: $$\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}}{r^3}$$
Key Result: $\vec{E}_{\text{eq}}$ is antiparallel to $\vec{p}$, and for a short dipole $E_{\text{axial}} = 2E_{\text{eq}}$.
Derivation 3

Torque on an Electric Dipole in a Uniform Electric Field

Setup: Place an electric dipole ($-q, +q$, length $2a$) in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$ at an angle $\theta$ with $\vec{E}$.

  1. Force on $+q$: $\vec{F}_+ = +q\vec{E}$ (along $\vec{E}$). Force on $-q$: $\vec{F}_- = -q\vec{E}$ (opposite to $\vec{E}$).
  2. Net translational force: $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = q\vec{E} - q\vec{E} = \vec{0}$ (no linear acceleration).
  3. Since these two equal and opposite forces act at different points, they form a couple. The perpendicular distance between their lines of action is $2a\sin\theta$.
  4. Magnitude of torque: $$\tau = (\text{Force}) \times (\perp \text{ distance}) = (qE)(2a\sin\theta) = (q \cdot 2a)E\sin\theta = pE\sin\theta$$
  5. Vector form: $$\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$$
Special Cases: $\theta = 0^\circ \Rightarrow \tau = 0$ (Stable equilibrium) · $\theta = 180^\circ \Rightarrow \tau = 0$ (Unstable equilibrium) · $\theta = 90^\circ \Rightarrow \tau_{\max} = pE$.
Derivation 4

Proof of Gauss's Law from Coulomb's Law (Sphere)

Setup: Consider a point charge $+q$ at the centre $O$ of an imaginary sphere of radius $r$.

  1. Electric field on the sphere's surface: $\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\hat{r}$.
  2. Consider an area element $d\vec{S} = dS\,\hat{r}$ directed radially outward. Here angle between $\vec{E}$ and $d\vec{S}$ is $\theta = 0^\circ$.
  3. Flux through $d\vec{S}$: $$d\Phi_E = \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = E\,dS\cos 0^\circ = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\,dS$$
  4. Total flux over the closed sphere (since $r$ is constant and $\oint dS = 4\pi r^2$): $$\Phi_E = \oint d\Phi_E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\oint dS = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}(4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}$$
Derivation 5 · Gauss Application 1

Electric Field due to an Infinitely Long Straight Charged Wire

Setup: Consider an infinitely long thin straight wire with uniform linear charge density $\lambda$. Find $\vec{E}$ at perpendicular distance $r$.

  1. Gaussian Surface: By cylindrical symmetry, choose a coaxial cylinder of radius $r$ and length $l$. $\vec{E}$ is radially outward everywhere.
  2. Flux through 3 surfaces:
    • Top & bottom flat caps: $\vec{E} \perp d\vec{S} \Rightarrow \theta = 90^\circ \Rightarrow \Phi_{\text{top}} = \Phi_{\text{bottom}} = 0$.
    • Curved surface (area $2\pi r l$): $\vec{E} \parallel d\vec{S} \Rightarrow \theta = 0^\circ \Rightarrow \Phi_{\text{curved}} = E(2\pi r l)$.
  3. Enclosed charge: $q_{\text{enc}} = \lambda l$.
  4. Apply Gauss's Law ($\Phi_E = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$): $$E(2\pi r l) = \frac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}, \qquad \vec{E} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\hat{n}$$
Derivation 6 · Gauss Application 2

Electric Field due to a Uniformly Charged Infinite Plane Sheet

Setup: Consider an infinite thin plane sheet with uniform surface charge density $\sigma$.

  1. Gaussian Surface: Choose a cylindrical pillbox of cross-sectional area $A$ piercing the sheet perpendicularly on both sides.
  2. Flux calculation:
    • Curved side wall: $\vec{E} \perp d\vec{S} \Rightarrow \Phi_{\text{curved}} = 0$.
    • Two flat end caps (area $A$ each): $\vec{E} \parallel d\vec{S}$ on both sides, so $\Phi_E = EA + EA = 2EA$.
  3. Enclosed charge: $q_{\text{enc}} = \sigma A$.
  4. Apply Gauss's Law: $$2EA = \frac{\sigma A}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}, \qquad \vec{E} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\hat{n}$$
Board Note: $E$ is uniform and completely independent of distance $r$ from the infinite sheet.
Derivation 7 · Gauss Application 3

Electric Field due to a Uniformly Charged Thin Spherical Shell

Setup: Consider a thin spherical shell of radius $R$ with uniform surface charge density $\sigma$ and total charge $q = 4\pi R^2\sigma$. Draw a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius $r$.

Case 1: Outside the Shell ($r > R$)

  1. Flux over Gaussian sphere: $\Phi_E = E(4\pi r^2)$. Enclosed charge: $q_{\text{enc}} = q$.
  2. By Gauss's Law: $$E(4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2} = \frac{\sigma R^2}{\varepsilon_0 r^2}$$

Case 2: On the Surface ($r = R$)

Putting $r = R$ gives: $$E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{R^2} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$$

Case 3: Inside the Shell ($r < R$)

  1. Gaussian sphere of radius $r < R$ encloses zero charge: $q_{\text{enc}} = 0$.
  2. By Gauss's Law: $$E(4\pi r^2) = 0 \implies E = 0$$

High-Yield CBSE Exam Traps:
  • Charge is speed-invariant; mass increases with relativistic velocity.
  • In a non-uniform field, a dipole experiences both a net force and a torque.

04.

Chapter Mind Map

Ch 01: Electric Charges & Fields

1. Electric Charge & Properties

  • Scalar quantity, SI unit Coulomb ($\text{C}$), elementary charge $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$.
  • Three core laws: Additivity ($q = \sum q_i$), Conservation (isolated system), Quantisation ($q = \pm ne$).
  • Methods of charging: Friction, Conduction, Induction (no physical contact).

2. Coulomb's Law & Superposition

  • Force $F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$ acts along the line joining two point charges (central force).
  • In a dielectric medium of constant $K$, force reduces by a factor of $K$: $F_{\text{med}} = F_{\text{vac}}/K$.
  • Superposition: Total force on a charge is the vector sum of individual two-body forces.

3. Electric Field & Electric Dipole

  • Field intensity $\vec{E} = \lim_{q_0\to 0}\vec{F}/q_0$; field lines never intersect and never form closed loops.
  • Dipole moment $\vec{p} = q(2\vec{a})$ directed from $-q$ to $+q$.
  • Short dipole: $E_{\text{axial}} = \frac{2kp}{r^3}$ (along $\vec{p}$), $E_{\text{eq}} = \frac{kp}{r^3}$ (opposite to $\vec{p}$), Torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$.

4. Electric Flux & Gauss's Law

  • Flux $\Phi_E = \oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$ depends only on enclosed charge, not surface shape.
  • Infinite line charge: $E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ ($E \propto 1/r$).
  • Infinite plane sheet: $E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ (independent of $r$).
  • Spherical shell: $E_{\text{out}} = \frac{kq}{r^2}$, $E_{\text{surface}} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, $E_{\text{in}} = 0$.
05.

Exam Problem-Solving Flowchart

Identify the Charge Distribution

Discrete Point Charges?

  • Use Coulomb's Law $F = k\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$ or $E = k\frac{q}{r^2}$.
  • For multiple charges, resolve into $x, y$ components and apply Superposition.

Equal & Opposite Pair ($+q, -q$)?

  • Calculate dipole moment $p = q(2a)$.
  • Use Axial ($\frac{2kp}{r^3}$), Equatorial ($\frac{kp}{r^3}$), or Torque ($\tau = pE\sin\theta$).

Symmetric Continuous Distribution?

  • Choose matching Gaussian surface (Cylinder for wire, Pillbox for sheet, Sphere for shell).
  • Apply $\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$ to extract $E$ directly.
